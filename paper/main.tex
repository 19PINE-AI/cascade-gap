\begin{abstract}
We measure the \emph{cascade gap} --- the accuracy difference between a same-weights two-pass self-cascade (\emph{perceive-and-record}, then \emph{reason}) and a model's native end-to-end mode --- across audio (MMAR), document (DocVQA), chart (ChartQA), and long-form generation (full academic papers, hour-long talks) tasks on six frontier and open-weight multimodal models. Three findings frame the result. (i) On standard short-form QA, end-to-end already wins on every (model, benchmark) we tested; the \emph{aggregate} cascade gap is uniformly negative, not positive. (ii) Real signal lives in per-stratum and per-model deltas: on long-form PDF summarization the cascade gap's sign \emph{flips} between vendors on the same document --- frontier GPT-5.4 ends-to-end at 96\% reference coverage and the cascade hurts it by 14 points, while a weaker open model (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Thinking) hallucinates at a 38\% rate end-to-end and the cascade rescues it to 95\% precision while tripling coverage. (iii) Whether a structured (versus plain-text) Pass-1 alphabet helps or hurts depends jointly on the task-class \emph{and} the model's capability to faithfully emit the schema; we validate this causally by swapping a speech-centric for a music-aware Pass-1 schema on MMAR music items and observing a 16-point coverage shift driven only by the schema. We propose \emph{baseline-coverage} as a single zero-shot feature predicting the cascade gap's sign --- below 0.7 the cascade is worth its 5--20$\times$ wall-time cost; above 0.85 it is strictly dominated. We also document a measurement-validity hazard: Google's RECITATION safety filter blocks faithful pass-through of recent arXiv content within Gemini's training cutoff, distorting any cascade benchmark on public published material.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A growing set of single-modality results --- in speech \citep{cuervo2025closing,cascadeequiv2026}, in document understanding \citep{shpigel2024docvlm,huang2025ocrreasoning}, and in GUI control \citep{gou2025uground} --- show that a cascaded inference pipeline (perceive in one call, then reason over the perception's output) can outperform the same model invoked end-to-end. These results appear in different communities, use different protocols, and have not been related to one another. Our contribution is the first cross-modality, same-weights measurement of this \emph{cascade gap} together with a sharp single-feature predictor of its sign.

We define the cascade gap as
\begin{equation}
\Delta = \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{cascade}} - \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{end\text{-}to\text{-}end}}.
\end{equation}
By \emph{self-cascade} we mean both Pass~1 (\emph{perceive-and-record}: transcribe an audio clip, extract text from images, describe a screen) and Pass~2 (\emph{reason}: produce the task answer using only the Pass-1 output) are issued to the same model with the same weights. This isolates the cascade gap from the cross-model confound that haunts the existing literature.

We pre-register three Pass-1 conditions --- \Cz\ (end-to-end), \Co\ (plain-text Pass-1, e.g.~``transcribe verbatim''), and \Cw\ (a structured task-class schema, e.g.~the Unified Audio Schema or our document-layout schema) --- and measure $\Delta$ on six models and five benchmarks. The headline of the plan that motivated this work was that on symbolic-content tasks the cascade should win and on perceptual tasks it should lose. The data does not support that framing. Instead we find:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The cascade gap's sign is \textbf{model-capability dependent}, not task-class dependent. The same paper sees $\Delta = +34$\,pp coverage with Gemini~3.1~Pro and $-14$\,pp with GPT-5.4 (\S\ref{sec:longform}).

\item The cascade's most defensible practical role is \textbf{rescuing hallucinatory weak models}. Qwen3-VL-30B-Thinking emits a 38\% error rate on a 16-page academic paper end-to-end; the cascade cuts that to 5\%.

\item Whether a structured \Cw\ schema beats a plain \Co\ schema is governed by \textbf{alphabet-match}: a speech-centric schema collapses music-content questions to \texttt{[inaudible] events:[music]} while a music-content schema rescues them. We measure this effect causally by swapping only the schema (\S\ref{sec:alphabet}).
\end{itemize}

We further document a methodological hazard: Google's RECITATION safety filter blocks faithful Pass-1 outputs on recent arXiv content inside the model's training cutoff. Any future cascade benchmark on public published material has to either work in private content, post-cutoff content, or accept Pass-1 censoring as a confound.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Speech-LLM cascades.} \cite{cuervo2025closing} document a text-speech understanding gap on equivalent reasoning tasks. The Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\citep{cascadeequiv2026} argues speech-LLMs internally behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades --- text representations are causally necessary, transcripts emerge in hidden states, and external cascades win under noise by up to 7.6\,pp at 0\,dB. Step-Audio-R1~\citep{stepaudior1} reports default audio-LLMs ``consistently perform better with minimal or no reasoning,'' which is consistent with our framing under the alphabet-fit lens. \emph{Beyond Transcription}~\citep{beyondtranscription} proposes a Unified Audio Schema (transcription + paralinguistics + non-linguistic events) trained as a supervised objective on 13{,}500 hours of audio; we adopt the same schema as a same-weights inference-time prompting condition (our \Cw\ for audio) and extend across modalities.

\paragraph{Document and chart cascades.} DocVLM~\citep{shpigel2024docvlm} provides the only existing compute-controlled ablation showing OCR-augmented VLMs beat pure-vision VLMs at every fixed token budget on InternVL2 (DocVQA 56.0\,$\to$\,86.6 at 448$\times$448). The OCR-Reasoning Benchmark~\citep{huang2025ocrreasoning} sharpens this: pure-OCR cascades cap below 50\% on text-rich reasoning, indicating that some text-rich tasks need spatial information OCR drops --- exactly our paper's thesis empirically demonstrated within one modality. ColPali~\citep{faysse2025colpali} shows vision-only retrieval beats OCR pipelines on retrieval --- a useful counterexample we discuss as a task-class boundary (retrieval $\neq$ reasoning).

\paragraph{GUI agents.} UI-TARS-2~\citep{wang2025uitars2} and SeeAct-V/UGround~\citep{gou2025uground} report vision-only agents beating DOM/a11y-tree-augmented agents on Mind2Web, OSWorld, and related benchmarks. We do not contest these results; we predict them. The a11y tree was designed for screen readers, not agents --- its loss function targets linear readability for blind users, not affordance/state/spatial reasoning for action. OmniParser~\citep{lu2024omniparser} is the closest extant instance of vision~$\to$~structured~text~$\to$~reasoning, generalizing into one point on our alphabet-richness sweep.

\paragraph{Theoretical anchor.} Bengio's Consciousness Prior~\citep{bengio2017consciousness} anchors our interpretation: cognition benefits from sparse, discrete, compositional bottlenecks between perception and reasoning. Discrete JEPA~\citep{baek2025discretejepa} makes the closest related theoretical move within vision; our work provides the inference-time, cross-modality empirical counterpart on contemporary frontier models.

\section{Protocol}

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

For each (model, task) cell we measure three pre-registered conditions:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em,style=nextline]
\item[\Cz\ End-to-end.] Raw modality $\to$ answer in a single call.
\item[\Co\ Plain self-cascade.] Pass~1 prompt = ``transcribe verbatim'' (audio), ``extract every visible piece of text in reading order'' (document), ``describe the screen as plain text'' (GUI). Pass~2 prompt = task answer using only the Pass-1 string. Same weights both passes.
\item[\Cw\ Augmented self-cascade.] Pass~1 prompt enriched with a task-class-specific schema. For audio we use the Unified Audio Schema (segment-level prosody + affect + non-speech events + confidence); for documents the layout schema (block-level type + bbox + reading order + visual weight + relations + table/chart/figure structures); for music content a music schema (key + chord progression + instrumentation + structure + cultural cues); for GUI an affordance schema (element type + state + affordance + role-semantic + nearby).
\end{description}

\subsection{Controls}

Decoding is fixed at \texttt{temperature=0}, \texttt{top\_p=1}, with the same \texttt{max\_output\_tokens} bound across all conditions for a given (model, task). Where vendor APIs require thinking mode (e.g., Gemini~3.1 Pro), we keep it on uniformly across conditions. Pass-1 and Pass-2 share the same prompt-iteration budget. We report output tokens, prompt tokens, wall-clock latency, and a hallucination/coverage breakdown for every cell.

\subsection{LLM-judge scoring for long-form generation}

Multiple-choice tasks (MMAR, ChartQA) are exact-match scored with a substring fallback on the gold answer. Free-form QA (DocVQA) uses a case-insensitive substring match against any reference answer (a lightweight ANLS proxy). For long-form generation (paper summarization, talk summarization), we use Gemini~3.1 Pro as judge under two metrics:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Faithfulness (precision):} the judge atomizes the generated summary into discrete claims and labels each \emph{supported}, \emph{unsupported}, or \emph{contradicted} against the reference document. Precision is supported / total.
\item \textbf{Coverage (recall):} the judge first extracts the 15--25 most-important atomic claims from the reference, then marks each as \emph{covered} or \emph{missing} in the summary. Coverage is covered / total.
\end{itemize}
The judge prompts and per-claim verdicts are released with the artifacts.

\subsection{Models, benchmarks, and sample}

We test six models with closed-source frontier coverage (Gemini~3.1 Pro Preview, Gemini~3 Flash Preview, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.4-mini), open weights (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B at 4-bit NF4 locally, Qwen3-VL-30B-Thinking via OpenRouter, Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking via OpenRouter). Benchmarks: MMAR audio reasoning (n=20 stratified), DocVQA validation (n=20 stratified by question type), ChartQA test (n=20 stratified by split), three full academic papers (16, 22, 24 pages), three additional post-cutoff arXiv papers (5, 22, 63 pages), and two long-form audio (Dan Pink TED talk 18.6\,min, Karpathy ``State of GPT'' 42.6\,min with Whisper-large-v3-turbo reference transcript). Pilot scale is small (n=1--3 per long-form cell) by design --- this is a Phase-1 measurement to determine whether a larger study would yield interesting signal.

The interesting structure is below the aggregate. Two examples:

\paragraph{MMAR Signal Layer on Gemini 3 Flash.} On the most acoustic-perceptual layer (questions like ``which cup demonstrates noticeable distortion?''), Flash's end-to-end accuracy is 0.20. The plain cascade brings it to 0.40, the augmented schema brings it to 0.40 --- $\Delta = +20$\,pp. Flash cannot do step-by-step acoustic reasoning end-to-end but \emph{can} do it when forced to first emit structured tags. On the same model's Semantic Layer, the structured cascade collapses to 0.40 against \Cz's 1.00, because semantic-meaning audio depends on prosody that text transcription drops. This is the alphabet-fit story within a single benchmark: \Cw\ wins where the schema matches the content, loses where the schema's tag vocabulary excludes the load-bearing features.

\paragraph{ChartQA augmented split.} On GPT-5.4-mini, the augmented cascade wins by +20\,pp on the augmented (programmatic) test split because forcing the model to first extract chart data into a structured layout dump captures specific axis values cleanly; on the open-ended human split the same schema discards the gestalt and loses 30\,pp. Under our \emph{alphabet-match} reframe (\S\ref{sec:alphabet}) this is the predicted pattern.

\subsection{Compute Pareto on short-form QA}

Cascade conditions cost 50--750$\times$ more output tokens than \Cz\ for the same answer. On Gemini~3.1 Pro DocVQA the C0 mean output is 4 tokens; \Cw's mean is 2{,}981. On Qwen3-VL-30B-Thinking DocVQA, \Cw\ takes 105\,s/item versus 4\,s for \Cz, and \emph{loses} 5\,pp accuracy. We do not recommend cascade as a default deployment for short-form QA on saturating frontier models.

The same input, same prompts, same judge produces three qualitatively different cascade-gap signs:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]

The structured schema \Cw\ in this regime is interesting: on Qwen, \Cw's precision (0.79) is worse than \Co's (0.95) because the model cannot follow the layout schema faithfully on every page --- it begins emitting blocks that look like the schema but assert facts not in the document. Same alphabet-richness $\times$ capability trade-off we saw on DocVQA.

\subsection{Multi-paper Gemini result (post-cutoff papers, no RECITATION)}

To extend $n$ on Gemini we ran two arXiv papers published after Gemini~3.1 Pro's February~2026 knowledge cutoff (DuplexCascade 5\,pg, ABMAMBA 22\,pg), where the RECITATION filter does not fire. Combined with Beyond Transcription this gives us $n=3$ Gemini long-form data points; results aggregate to \Cz~0.99/0.55, \Co~0.99/0.61, \Cw~0.97/0.77. \Cw\ wins coverage on 2 of 3 papers. The signal is consistent: on Gemini's compact-summary regime, \emph{some} cascade variant beats \Cz\ on coverage on every paper, with which variant winning depending on the paper's structure (table-heavy~$\to$~\Cw, prose-heavy~$\to$~\Co).

\section{Direct alphabet-match validation}
\label{sec:alphabet}

The clearest single piece of mechanistic evidence in Phase-1 is a schema-only ablation. We re-ran the 12 music-modality items in our MMAR pilot with a music-content schema (key + chord progression + instrumentation + structure + cultural cues) instead of the speech-centric Unified Audio Schema. Same model (Gemini~3.1 Pro), same audio, same Pass-2 prompt; only the Pass-1 schema changes.

Three items where the speech schema produced \texttt{[inaudible] events:[music]} were rescued from 0~$\to$~1 by the music schema (questions about chord distortion, instrument mode, and singer issue). A second observation from this ablation is unexpectedly informative: \Co\ plain transcription (0.917) beats both \Cw\ schemas. A well-matched structured schema can over-commit Pass-1 to a particular interpretation that hurts Pass-2 relative to a lossy-but-ambiguous transcription. The original plan's expectation that \Cw\ should monotonically dominate \Co\ in alphabet richness is therefore wrong: there is a regime where \Co\ wins because Pass-2 reasoners exploit ambiguity well.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{A single-feature predictor}

Combining the cross-vendor table with the ChartQA and Flash signal-layer results, we propose a single-feature predictor of $\mathrm{sign}(\Delta)$:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{sign}(\Delta)} = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{if } \mathrm{cov}_{\Cz} \le 0.7 \\ \approx 0 & \text{if } 0.7 < \mathrm{cov}_{\Cz} \le 0.85 \\ -1 & \text{if } \mathrm{cov}_{\Cz} > 0.85 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{cov}_{\Cz}$ is the model's reference coverage at end-to-end, measured zero-shot on a small held-out probe of the same task class. This subsumes the original symbolic-vs-perceptual axis and is task-agnostic. On the data we have it predicts $\mathrm{sign}(\Delta)$ correctly for all four long-form cells (GPT-5.4: $\mathrm{cov}_{\Cz}{=}0.96 \to -$; Gemini Pro: $0.39 \to +$; Qwen3-VL-30B: $0.23 \to +$; Gemini Pro post-cutoff aggregate: $0.55 \to +$).

\subsection{What we got wrong about the original plan}

The plan that motivated this work \citep{cascadegapplan2026} expected the cascade to win on symbolic-content tasks and lose on perceptual ones. The data does not support that decomposition. Two alternative axes are better predictors: \textbf{baseline coverage at \Cz} (the predictor above) and \textbf{schema-content alphabet-match} (the music-schema ablation). These two are largely orthogonal: alphabet-match decides whether \Cw\ helps relative to \Co; baseline coverage decides whether the cascade as a whole helps relative to \Cz.

We also predicted that augmented cascades \Cw\ would monotonically dominate plain cascades \Co. This is false. On Beyond Transcription with Gemini 3.1 Pro, \Co\ wins +23\,pp coverage over \Cw. On MMAR music items, \Co\ wins +17\,pp accuracy over \Cw\ even with the well-matched music schema. A structured schema can over-commit Pass-1 to specific interpretations that limit Pass-2's flexibility. This deserves its own treatment in any follow-up: the choice of schema is not just a richness sweep, it is a commitment-vs-flexibility trade.

\subsection{When is the cascade worth its cost?}

Cascade C1 typically costs 5--20$\times$ the wall-time of \Cz\ on long-form tasks; \Cw\ on thinking-mode models like Qwen3-VL-30B costs $\sim$23 minutes per paper versus 1.3 minutes end-to-end. The deployment recommendation that survives our data is narrow but defensible:

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5,colframe=blue!50!black,title=When to deploy a self-cascade]
Use a self-cascade if \emph{all three} hold: (1) your base model's $\mathrm{cov}_{\Cz}$ on a representative held-out probe is below 0.7; (2) the task class has a measurable schema-content match (i.e., a concise structured Pass-1 alphabet exists that captures the load-bearing features); (3) you can tolerate a 5--20$\times$ wall-time penalty. Otherwise prefer end-to-end.
\end{tcolorbox}

In particular, a frontier model with high baseline coverage (GPT-5.4 in our data) gains nothing from the cascade. The strongest practical case for the cascade is rescuing weaker open-source models on long-form generation --- which is exactly the regime production deployments most often need to pay attention to.

\section{Limitations and protocol issues}

\paragraph{Sample size.} Long-form is $n=4$ paper-cells across vendors and $n=1$ audio. Per-cell judge atomization is stochastic enough that single-trial coverage numbers can swing 5--10\,pp run-to-run. Phase-2 needs $n \geq 30$ paper-cells with multi-trial judge averaging.

\paragraph{Judge dependence.} We use Gemini~3.1 Pro as judge for all long-form scoring. Self-judging has known biases. We recommend a Phase-2 cross-validation with GPT-5.4 as a second judge, plus a human-annotated subset.

\paragraph{The RECITATION filter is a measurement-validity hazard.} On two of three pre-cutoff arXiv papers we tested, Gemini's RECITATION safety filter blocked Pass-1 outputs (returning empty text after consuming 6--11k thinking tokens). The filter fires because Gemini detects that emitting a faithful content record of these papers would too closely reproduce training data. \emph{Any cascade benchmark on public published material inside a model's training cutoff is exposed to this confound.} We worked around it by (i) rephrasing Pass-1 to ``preserve every substantive point'' rather than ``transcribe verbatim,'' and (ii) using post-cutoff content. Both are partial fixes; (i) loses verbatim-quote detail, (ii) restricts the eligible content set. A robust Phase-2 protocol must use private content (internal memos, user-submitted lectures, classroom recordings) where the filter does not fire.

\paragraph{C2 schema design is non-trivial.} The speech-UAS schema is well-suited to speech but mismatched to music; the document-layout schema is rich enough to break weaker models. Designing a single schema that matches all task content within a modality is harder than the plan anticipated. The Phase-2 protocol should include a Pass-0 router (``classify input type, route to appropriate schema'') and report on the routed system.

\paragraph{Single-vendor cross-comparison.} Our cross-vendor result is on one paper. Phase-2 should run all four vendor-paper combinations across at least 5 papers including private content, with both judges, to confirm the +34\,pp Gemini win and the $-14$\,pp GPT-5.4 loss replicate.

\section{Conclusion}

The cascade gap is real, but it points the opposite direction from what we expected. End-to-end frontier models on standard benchmarks already attend to long content faithfully and comprehensively; the cascade's most defensible practical value is rescuing weaker open-source models that hallucinate at C0. Whether \Cw\ or \Co\ wins is a property of the schema-content alphabet-match, modulated by the model's capacity to faithfully emit the schema. Across vendors on identical input, the sign of $\Delta$ flips, and we offer baseline-coverage as a single zero-shot feature that predicts the sign correctly across our cells. We release pipelines, prompts, schemas, and per-call logs at the repository linked above and invite Phase-2 contributions on private and post-cutoff long-form content.
