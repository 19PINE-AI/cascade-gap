\begin{abstract}
The default way to put a multimodal model on a long-form review task is to hand it the raw source---the audio, the page images---and let it reason end-to-end. We give counter-intuitive evidence that this is the wrong default. \textbf{Decomposing the task into two same-weights passes is systematically more faithful and more complete}: one pass transcribes the source to text, the other writes the review from that transcript alone---even though the second pass sees strictly \emph{less} than the end-to-end pass. Across a 21-cell suite on Gemini~3.1~Pro (11 audio sources, 10 papers, 9--176~pp), decomposition reduces hallucinations on 18/21 cells and improves coverage on 16/21 (both sign-test significant), and the gain scales inversely with the end-to-end baseline ($r{=}-0.45$): the more the monolithic pass satisfices, the more decomposition recovers. A nine-experiment mechanistic study then asks \emph{why}, and the cause is not what one might assume. It is \textbf{not} a perception failure---the model's own Pass-1 transcript holds $99.7\%$ of the content the end-to-end pass drops---and \textbf{not} that text is an intrinsically better reasoning substrate---identical content is read, and reasoned over, equally well from text and from an \emph{image} of that text, both on open VLMs at the activation level (a layer-wise logit-lens) and behaviorally on the headline frontier models themselves, where with reasoning headroom the image is if anything \emph{better} than text. The cause is a \textbf{generation-side bottleneck}: one pass cannot simultaneously perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review, so it satisfices and drops roughly a third of what it saw. Decomposition helps because it gives perception and synthesis each a dedicated, full-budget pass; collapsing the two into one call crashes coverage. The account predicts exactly two failure modes, both of which appear: a \emph{compression bottleneck} when synthesis cannot fit the transcript in its output budget (Mode~A), and a \emph{training-prior leak} when removing the source lets synthesis confabulate (Mode~B), which a quote-grounded iterative-refinement variant suppresses where it binds. That same inverse-baseline law organizes the cross-vendor picture (Claude~Opus~4.7, Mimo~v2-omni, a second Gemini tier): a directional win where $C_0$ is weak and a washout where it is already strong, not a uniform replication; the headline aggregate is single-vendor. Two judges, multi-seed re-runs, and probe/reference ablations corroborate. Perceive, externalize, then synthesize. The win is decomposition, not the modality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multimodal foundation models can read audio and document images directly. The intuitive way to deploy them on a long-form review task is therefore end-to-end: feed the model the raw source and ask for the review in one call. Keeping the original modality in context looks strictly safer: no transcription step to lose information, and the model sees everything. We argue, and measure, that this intuition is backwards for long-form review.

The alternative is a same-weights two-pass decomposition: the model first transcribes the source to text (Pass-1, \emph{perceive and externalize}), then writes the review from that transcript alone (Pass-2, \emph{synthesize}, with the same weights and the raw modality removed from context). This wins. The obvious explanation, that text is simply a better substrate for the model's reasoning, turns out to be wrong. We run a nine-experiment mechanistic study (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) that isolates the cause:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Not perception.} The model's own Pass-1 transcript covers $99.9\%$ of probes. Of the $362$ probes the end-to-end pass drops, $361$ ($99.7\%$) are present in that transcript. The model perceives the content, then loses it downstream.
\item \textbf{Not substrate.} On an open VLM, a layer-wise logit-lens finds identical propositional content is read equally well from text and from an \emph{image} of that text, whether one fact or sixteen packed facts, with the same answer-token log-prob and the same readout depth. The same null holds \emph{behaviorally on the headline frontier models}: Gemini~3.1~Pro and Claude~Opus~4.7 retrieve a queried fact from pixels and from text with identical accuracy up to 48 packed facts (E8), and the equivalence extends from retrieval to \emph{multi-hop reasoning} (E9, summing six packed facts)---where, on the one model with reasoning headroom, the image is reliably \emph{better} than text, the opposite of H1. A degraded-image control that the same probes flag as non-equivalent confirms the test could detect a gap if one existed. There is no intrinsic ``pixels are harder to reason over'' penalty for legible content.
\item \textbf{It is generation under load.} A single pass cannot simultaneously perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review, so it satisfices and drops $\sim\!1/3$ of what it saw. Decomposition gives perception a dedicated externalization pass and synthesis a dedicated, \emph{full-budget} pass over complete text. Collapsing the two into one call crashes coverage or fails to emit a review at all (the transcript exhausts the output budget), so the separation, not the modality, is the active ingredient.
\end{itemize}
We call the pattern \textbf{perceive--externalize--synthesize}. This account also predicts the two failure modes (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}): a compression bottleneck when synthesis cannot fit the transcript in its output budget (Mode~A), and a training-prior leak when removing the source lets synthesis confabulate (Mode~B). The latter is a genuine but secondary cost of dropping the modality, not the engine of the win.

This relates to, but is distinct from, prior single-modality work. Cuervo et al.~\cite{cuervo2025closing} and the Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\cite{ceh2026} find speech-LLMs internally behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades, and Step-Audio-R1~\cite{stepaudior1} shows audio LLMs reasoning over a \emph{textual surrogate} of the audio. Our activation-level tests find \emph{no} reasoning-substrate penalty for propositional content in \emph{either} modality (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): a VLM reasons over an image of text as well as over text (E6), and, once the speech is intelligible, an omni model reasons over spoken facts as well as written ones (E7), with an apparent audio gap vanishing when low-quality TTS is replaced by a clean neural voice. The Step-Audio-R1 effect is best read as task-payload-specific, not a contradiction: its tasks (music, environmental sound) have \emph{acoustic} answers that transcription discards. On documents, DocVLM~\cite{docvlm2024} reports OCR-augmented VLMs beating pure-vision VLMs at fixed budget, while vision-only configurations dominate on GUI tasks~\cite{uground2024,uitars2025}. Few cross-modality same-weights measurements exist, and fewer still isolate \emph{why}. This paper provides both, focused on \textbf{long-form review generation}, the deployment case where end-to-end models are observably weak (they drop content and embellish) and two-pass decomposition is the obvious alternative.

\paragraph{Findings.}
(i) Two-pass decomposition ($C_1$) beats end-to-end ($C_0$) across 21 Gemini cells on both faithfulness (18/21) and coverage (16/21), both sign-test significant, despite the synthesis pass seeing strictly less (\S\ref{sec:main}). (ii) The gain scales inversely with the $C_0$ baseline ($r{=}-0.45$): decomposition recovers most where the monolithic pass satisfices most. (iii) \emph{The mechanism is generation-side, not perception or substrate} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): the dropped content sits in the model's own transcript, text and image-of-text are read equally well (on open VLMs and frontier models alike), yet collapsing the two passes into one call crashes coverage. (iv) The account predicts two failure modes---compression overflow (Mode~A) and training-prior leak (Mode~B, replicated on Claude). (v) A quote-grounded iterative-refinement variant ($C_{1\rm iter}$) suppresses Mode~B where it binds but trades coverage elsewhere. (vi) GPT-5.4 and Claude judges agree on direction (coverage 19/21, hallucination 14/21; per-probe median $0.94$).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~A 21-cell same-weights measurement of end-to-end ($C_0$) vs.\ two-pass decomposition ($C_1$), across two modalities, three vendors, and two judges. (2)~\emph{A nine-experiment mechanistic study} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) that adjudicates among perception, substrate, and generation-side accounts, ruling the first two out and supporting \textbf{perceive--externalize--synthesize}: perception coverage vs.\ surfacing (E3), an open-VLM cross-modal logit-lens under load (E6), an omni-model audio analogue (E7), behavioral cross-modal retrieval (E8) and multi-hop-reasoning (E9) probes on the headline frontier models, the single-call collapse test (E2), a both-modalities control (E1), and DPI/native-text controls (E4/E5, reported with their confounds). (3)~A two-mode account of where decomposition breaks down (Mode~A compression, Mode~B training-prior leak) with deployment triggers, derived from the mechanism. (4)~An iterative-refinement variant ($C_{1\rm iter}$) that re-grounds synthesis with verbatim quotes. (5)~Systematic robustness checks ($n{=}5$ multi-seed on four cells, inter-judge on all 21, probe-circularity on 6, reference-bias on 4), with all retract/preserve verdicts reported transparently.

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig0_protocol_schematic.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Reason-over-modality ($C_0$) vs.\ reduce-to-text-then-reason ($C_1$), same weights.} $C_0$ is one multimodal call: the model under test reads the raw audio or page images and writes a comprehensive review, reasoning directly over the modality. $C_1$ first reduces the source to text (Pass-1 ASR/OCR call, same model weights), then reasons over that transcript alone (Pass-2, text-only, with the raw modality absent from the Pass-2 context). The two configurations differ only in the representation the reasoning step operates over.}
\label{fig:protocol_schematic}
\end{figure}

For each source (audio or scanned paper): (1)~A \textbf{reference transcript} is produced by Gemini~3.1~Pro ASR/OCR with chunking (30-min audio chunks, 1-page-per-call OCR, Appendix~\ref{appdx:protocol}). (2)~$C_0$ \emph{reason-over-modality} (end-to-end): the model under test reads the raw modality and writes a review article in one call. (3)~$C_1$ \emph{reduce-to-text-then-reason} (the same-weights cascade): the model under test produces its own transcript (Pass-1, same prompt as the reference), then writes the review from that transcript (Pass-2, text-only). The only thing that changes between $C_0$ and $C_1$ is whether the reasoning step sees pixels/audio or text. Figure~\ref{fig:protocol_schematic} schematizes the two configurations.

\paragraph{Scoring.} GPT-5.4 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=high}) acts as judge. From the reference, it extracts 30--50 atomic factual probes. For each review the judge then lists every claim not supported by the reference (\emph{hallucination test}) and marks each probe as COVERED / MISSING (\emph{probe coverage}). The protocol is sharper than short-form QA: the review must be both exhaustive (high coverage) and faithful (zero hallucination). Because most frontier models fail the zero-hallucination bar, we report counts and rates as operational measures.

\paragraph{Noise floor and statistics.} We treat $|\Delta h|\le 1$ on cells with $h<10$, and $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 0.04$ ($\approx 2$ probes on a 50-probe cell), as within noise (presentation conventions calibrated against inter-judge direction flips, \S\ref{sec:robust}). For aggregate effects we report Wilson 95\% CIs on coverage rates, percentile-bootstrap 95\% CIs ($n_{\rm boot}{=}10000$, seeded) on cross-cell means and Pearson $r$, and sign tests against $\Delta{=}0$.

\paragraph{Cells.} 21 Gemini cells: 11 audio sources (26--85~min talks, lectures, panels, and meetings) and 10 papers (9--176~pp), the latter being seven NASA NTRS reports (1968--1972) plus three modern post-cutoff arXiv reviews; full source list in Appendix~\ref{appdx:protocol} and per-cell counts in Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}. \emph{Cross-vendor extensions} reuse the Gemini reference and probes (isolating the question to whether the advantage depends on the $C_0/C_1$ model): Claude~Opus~4.7 (a complete $C_0$-vs-$C_1$ comparison on the six papers where its multimodal $C_0$ fits, \S\ref{sec:main}; $C_1$-only on the longer papers) and Mimo~v2-omni on every cell where its $C_0$ does not return empty.

\paragraph{Notation.} $C_0$: reason-over-modality (end-to-end). $C_1$: reduce-to-text-then-reason (single-pass cascade). Subscript $c$ ($C_{1c}$): chunked Pass-2 (sub-summarize per chunk, then merge). Superscript $\rm concat$: chunked, no merge. Superscript $\rm strip$: citation surface forms replaced by neutral \texttt{[REF]} placeholders before Pass-2. Subscript $\rm iter$: the iterative-refinement variant of \S\ref{sec:iter}.

\section{Main result: two-pass decomposition beats end-to-end}
\label{sec:main}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/fig1_inverse_correlation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Reduce-to-text coverage gain vs.\ $C_0$ baseline across all 21 Gemini cells.} The worse the model reasons over the raw modality (lower $C_0$), the more reducing to text recovers. Circles: audio. Squares: paper. OLS line with bootstrap 95\% CI band, Pearson $r=-0.45$ with bootstrap CI $[-0.73,-0.10]$ (fraction $r\geq 0$: $0.9\%$). The five counter-cells (NeurIPS panel, Wagging~Tail, arXiv~Perovskite, Natural~Vibration, arXiv~Fairness~AI) sit at high $C_0$ baseline (no headroom) or trigger Mode~A/B (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).}
\label{fig:inverse}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Aggregate effect at $n=21$.}
The cascade reduces hallucinations on 18/21 cells (mean $\Delta_{\rm halluc}=-3.86$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-5.62,-2.33]$, sign-test $p=0.0015$) and improves coverage on 16/21 (mean $\Delta_{\rm cov}=+0.118$, $[+0.048,+0.189]$, $p=0.027$); both intervals exclude zero. The effect is robust to median substitution on the four high-count judge-spread cells (\S\ref{sec:robust}). Per-cell counts and Wilson CIs are in Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}.

\paragraph{The inverse-baseline law (the paper's organizing principle).}
Coverage gain scales inversely with the end-to-end baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:inverse}). This is the central prediction of the reduce-to-text account and the thread that unifies the rest of the paper: $C_0$ measures how well the model already reasons over the raw modality, the cells where it reasons worst are exactly where moving the reasoning onto text recovers the most, and the same law governs the counter-cells (no headroom $\Rightarrow$ no gain), the cross-vendor results below, and the failure modes (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}). It is a statement about \emph{headroom}, not a promise of a universal win. Excluding the two meeting-minutes re-prompts (artificially low absolute coverage because their probes use atomic-fact style while the prompt asks for structured minutes), $r=-0.47$ on $n=19$ with CI $[-0.83,+0.10]$: the direction survives but the CI just crosses zero on this subset.

\paragraph{Cascade-positive cells.}
The largest coverage gain is Harvard~Moot~Court audio ($+50$pp, multi-speaker legal argumentation with little $C_0$ headroom); the cleanest hallucination win is 3B1B~Attention ($11h\to 0h$). Gains concentrate on audio and on moderate papers (Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}).

\paragraph{Counter-cells.}
The five cells where cascade fails to improve on at least one axis split into two clusters: \emph{high-baseline / no-headroom} (NeurIPS panel, Wagging~Tail, arXiv~Perovskite, all $C_0\geq 0.80$) and \emph{mode-triggered failures} (Natural~Vibration, Mode~B; arXiv~Fairness~AI, Mode~A; \S\ref{sec:failure_modes}). Perovskite's loss shrinks from $-16$pp at $n{=}1$ to $-6$pp under $n{=}5$ multi-seed (Appendix~\ref{appdx:multiseed}), so we treat it as near-noise-floor.

\paragraph{Cross-vendor and cross-model: the inverse-baseline law, not uniform replication.}
Every cross-model result is an instance of the same law (Figure~\ref{fig:inverse}): the cascade helps in proportion to the headroom the end-to-end $C_0$ leaves, so the prediction---and the finding---is wins where $C_0$ is weak and washouts where it is already strong, \emph{not} a blanket vendor-independent win. Three checks bear this out. (i)~On Thermal~66pp, the one moderate cell all three vendors realize, all three improve coverage and Gemini/Claude both cut hallucinations sharply, converging to a common $\approx\!1.4$h per 1k words from different end-to-end rates (Appendix~\ref{appdx:perword}). (ii)~A complete within-Claude comparison on the six NASA papers Claude's $C_0$ can fit (9--104pp, Table~\ref{tab:claude_within}) reduces hallucinations on 5/6 (exception: the Heat~Pipes Mode-B cell), but the four short papers sit near $C_0$ ceiling so coverage is flat and the effect is not independently significant at $n{=}6$ ($p{=}0.22$); the one headroom cell (Thermal) is a clean Pareto win. (iii)~A clean, full-quality Gemini~2.5~Flash audio comparison (Appendix~\ref{appdx:flash_audio}) makes the point sharply: 2.5~Flash is a \emph{strong} end-to-end audio reader (its $C_0$ beats Pro's on 4/5 cells), so the cascade wins only where $C_0$ has room (MIT, Harvard) and is flat-to-negative where it is saturated (NeurIPS; Karpathy even regresses)---headroom-gated, as predicted. In each, the cascade \emph{direction} holds where the law says it should; we do not claim a uniform cross-vendor win, and the headline aggregate remains single-vendor (Gemini).

\paragraph{Long-paper divergence.}
On longer cells the three vendors diverge sharply (full numbers in Appendix~\ref{appdx:xvendor}). On Heat~Pipes~104pp, Gemini cascade is small Pareto-positive while Claude cascade \emph{regresses} on faithfulness, leaving $C_0$ on the Pareto frontier---the Mode-B signature (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}). On SP-5100~176pp both Gemini and Claude $C_1$ sit at very high hallucination rates with the same bibliographic-expansion signature, and Claude $C_0$ is not realizable (176 page-images exceed Anthropic's payload limit even with the 1M-context beta). On the modern arXiv reviews the cascade direction can \emph{reverse}, which we attribute mainly to denser typesetting forcing more aggressive synthesis-pass compression at the same output budget (Mode~A). The long-paper picture motivates the mechanism study and the two failure regimes (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}--\ref{sec:failure_modes}).

\section{Mechanism: perceive, externalize, synthesize}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Why does decomposition win? Three hypotheses are consistent with the headline result but make different predictions. \textbf{H1 (substrate):} the model reasons better over text tokens than over raw audio/image tokens, so moving the work onto text helps. \textbf{H2 (externalization / generation under load):} a single pass cannot simultaneously perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review. Decomposition gives perception a dedicated externalization step and synthesis a dedicated, full-budget step over complete text. \textbf{H3 (attention dilution):} raw-modality tokens are numerous and low-density, diluting the reasoning attention. We ran nine experiments (E1--E9) to separate them. The verdict is that H2 is the operative mechanism, H1 is \emph{not} supported (and is directly contradicted for documents---including, now, on the headline frontier models themselves, for retrieval (E8) and multi-hop reasoning (E9)), and H3 is untestable on Gemini with our tools. All experiments reuse the exact probe/hallucination judge of \S\ref{sec:protocol}, and artifacts are released (\S\ref{sec:code}). The two load-bearing generation-side contrasts (E1, E2) are run at $n{=}5$ seeds on all 21 cells (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}, statistical treatment). E4/E5 remain single-seed controls on an 8-cell subset and are reported as such. \S\ref{sec:mechanism} closes with the statistical caveats, and \S\ref{sec:discussion} with the validation plan.

\paragraph{E3: the bottleneck is not perception.}
We scored the model's \emph{own} Pass-1 transcript against the same probes used for the reviews (all 21 cells). The transcript covers a mean $99.9\%$ of probes, far above the $C_0$ review ($65\%$) and $C_1$ synthesis ($77\%$); decisively, of the $362$ probes the end-to-end pass drops across the suite, $361$ ($99.7\%$, Wilson 95\% CI $[98.5\%,99.9\%]$) are present in the model's own transcript (Appendix~\ref{appdx:substrate}). The content is lost \emph{downstream} of perception. (The transcript ceiling is partly definitional---probes come from a near-identical reference---but the load-bearing inference is one-directional: content $C_0$ drops is nonetheless capturable-as-text by the same weights.)

\paragraph{Not substrate (E6--E9).}
The other intuitive explanation---that text is an intrinsically better reasoning substrate than pixels (H1)---is false for legible content, on four tests (full detail, tables, and figures in Appendix~\ref{appdx:substrate}). At the \emph{activation} level, a layer-wise logit-lens on two open VLMs reads novel synthetic facts equally well from text and from an \emph{image} of that text---same answer log-prob, same readout depth (TOST-equivalent within $\pm 1$ layer), for one fact and up to 24 packed facts (E6); an omni-model analogue finds the same for \emph{speech} once the TTS is intelligible, an apparent audio gap being a TTS-quality artifact (E7). \emph{Behaviorally on the headline models}, Gemini~3.1~Pro and Claude~Opus~4.7 retrieve a queried fact from pixels and text with identical accuracy up to 48 packed facts (E8), and the equivalence extends from retrieval to multi-hop \emph{reasoning} (summing six packed facts): on the one model with reasoning headroom (Claude without extended thinking) the image is reliably \emph{better} than text (gap $+0.175$), the opposite of H1 (E9). A degraded-image control that the same probes flag as non-equivalent confirms the test would detect a gap if one existed. So pixels carry no reasoning penalty for legible content---on proxy models and on the headline models alike. The contrast with the headline is the point: single-fact retrieval is \emph{perfect} from pixels, yet $C_0$ on these same models drops $\sim\!1/3$ of the content (E3)---the loss is generation-side, not substrate.

\paragraph{E2: the win requires a separate, full-budget synthesis pass.}
If decomposition merely externalizes content into one context, doing both steps in a single call should suffice. It does not. A single call instructed to first transcribe verbatim and then write the review (modality attached) \emph{collapses}: coverage falls far below $C_1$ on every paper that produced a review, and on several cells the call never emits a review at all---the transcript consumes the entire output budget (Table~\ref{tab:mech_cond}). This is confirmed at scale: at $n{=}5$ seeds on all 21 cells, single-call coverage is below $C_1$ by mean $\Delta_{\rm cov}=-0.111$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.170,-0.038]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.019$, mixed-effects $p<10^{-3}$), with $7$ runs emitting no review at all. The active ingredient is the \emph{decomposition into two passes with independent output budgets}, not text-in-context per se.

\paragraph{E1: removing the modality is a minor faithfulness bonus, not the engine.}
Giving the synthesis pass \emph{both} the transcript and the raw modality ($C_{1+}$) does not break the benefit and sometimes maximizes coverage (Table~\ref{tab:mech_cond}); consistent with E6, pixels are not harmful to content recovery. What their presence does do is re-introduce a few hallucinations relative to pure-text $C_1$, the same effect Mode~B names. At $n{=}5$ seeds on all 21 cells this holds: re-attaching the modality moves coverage by only $\Delta_{\rm cov}=+0.069$ (95\% CI $[-0.001,+0.154]$, inconclusive) while adding hallucinations on $14/21$ cells. So dropping the modality buys faithfulness at the margin, but the larger coverage win comes from the pass structure (E2), not from removing pixels.

Two further controls (E4, DPI; E5, native \texttt{pdftotext}) did not separate the hypotheses---Gemini normalizes images so DPI cannot move the vision-token count, and \texttt{pdftotext} scrambles two-column reading order---so we report them with their confounds in Appendix~\ref{appdx:substrate} rather than rely on them. The 8-cell condition grid for E1/E2/E5 is in Table~\ref{tab:mech_cond}.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
The evidence converges on \textbf{perceive--externalize--synthesize}: the model perceives the content (E3); pixels-vs-text is not the reasoning bottleneck for legible content, at the activation level (E6/E7) and behaviorally on the headline models (E8/E9); and the recovery requires decomposing into two passes with independent output budgets (E2), not merely removing the modality (E1). The inverse-baseline law ($r{=}-0.45$) follows directly: a low $C_0$ marks cells where the monolithic pass satisfices most, and E3 shows the dropped content is sitting in the transcript waiting to be synthesized. The mechanism is \emph{strongly supported by E3, E6/E8/E9, and the multi-seed E2}, corroborated by E1, with E4/E5 inconclusive; the two failure modes (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}) are its predicted breakdowns. What remains is breadth, not power: activation-level access on frontier models, real scanned pages, and tasks beyond long-form review (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Where decomposition breaks down: two failure modes}
\label{sec:failure_modes}

The perceive--externalize--synthesize account (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) predicts exactly two ways decomposition can fail, and both appear in the data: when the synthesis pass cannot carry the externalized transcript through its output budget (Mode~A), and when removing the raw source lets the synthesis pass fill gaps from prior knowledge (Mode~B, the secondary cost of dropping the modality). These are the two modes below; Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes} (Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}) illustrates both on Heat~Pipes.

\paragraph{Mode~A: compression bottleneck (3 papers).}
On long sources, Pass-2 has a fixed output budget ($\approx 2$k words). When the cascade transcript is much longer than what fits, Pass-2 must compress, dropping content the same way $C_0$ does. Three Gemini cells exhibit this signature: Heat~Pipes~104pp (Pass-1 transcript 22k words, compression ratio $\sim 11\times$, $C_1$ gain $+6$pp), arXiv~Fairness~AI~53pp ($\sim 15\times$, which \emph{loses} $-10$pp), and arXiv~Perovskite~80pp ($\sim 9\times$, $-16$pp at $n{=}1$, $-6$pp under multi-seed). The fix tested on Heat~Pipes is \textbf{chunked Pass-2}: split the transcript into $\sim 5$k-word chunks, summarize each, then concatenate (or merge). Heat~Pipes Gemini $C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$ lifts coverage $0.78\to 0.94$ ($+16$pp) at $+5$ halluc (Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}~left). At $n{=}5$ multi-seed verification (Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}, Table~\ref{tab:chunked_multiseed_appdx}) the chunked-Pass-2 direction replicates on Fairness~AI and Perovskite but \emph{not} on Megagauss, so we report chunked Pass-2 as a $2/3$ replicated Mode-A fix.

\paragraph{Mode~B: training-prior leak (4 papers).}
This is the genuine cost of reduction: removing the raw source removes a grounding anchor, and on documents with heavy training-data overlap Pass-2 fills the gap from prior knowledge when the transcript abbreviates a detail. The Heat~Pipes paper cites foundational references like ``Reference~2 (Cotter)''; Claude's $C_1$ expands these to ``Cotter's 1965 analysis (LA-3246-MS)''---the year and report number are training-data details, not in the transcript (examples in Appendix~\ref{appdx:examples}). The same bibliographic-expansion signature appears on all four long citation-heavy Claude cells (Heat~Pipes, SP-5100, Fairness~AI, Megagauss), whereas the moderate citation-light Thermal~66pp paper shows only 4 Claude $C_1$ hallucinations.

\paragraph{Two diagnostic signals.} Two thresholds emerge as starting points for practitioner triage:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Mode~A trigger:} Pass-1 transcript length $\gtrsim 5\times$ Pass-2 output budget with $C_1$ coverage gain $<+10$pp (Heat~Pipes Gemini: $11\times$, $+6$pp).
\item \textbf{Mode~B trigger:} input transcript contains $>\sim 2$ ``Reference~$N$'' / ``Ref.~$N$'' surface forms per 1k words \emph{and} the topic appears in widely cited pre-cutoff literature. Heat~Pipes~104pp has 55 such surface forms (2.48/1k). Thermal~66pp has 0 and shows no Mode~B even on Claude.
\end{itemize}
The two modes can compound (Mimo Heat~Pipes shows both) or operate in isolation (Heat~Pipes Gemini is dominantly Mode~A, Heat~Pipes Claude dominantly Mode~B).

\paragraph{Engineering interventions tested.} We evaluated two interventions across cells: \emph{chunked Pass-2} (Mode-A target, $2/3$ replication under $n{=}5$ multi-seed, Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}) and \emph{citation stripping} ($C_1^{\rm strip}$, Mode-B target). \textbf{Citation stripping does not survive replication}: at $n{=}5$ Pass-2 calls on Heat~Pipes Claude the strip effect on hallucinations is $\hat{\Delta}=-1.8\pm 2.6$ (95\% CI $[-6.9,+3.3]$), the original ``$20\to 13$'' point estimate having been within the combined Pass-2 $+$ judge noise envelope (Appendix~\ref{appdx:stoch}). \textbf{We retract the citation-stripping claim on Claude}, motivating the stronger structural intervention in \S\ref{sec:iter}.

\section{Iterative refinement: a quote-grounded Mode-B fix ($C_{1\rm iter}$)}
\label{sec:iter}

Mode~B (the synthesis pass confabulating once the source is removed) calls for re-grounding synthesis in the transcript. $C_{1\rm iter}$ is a three-step variant: (a)~extract $50$--$150$ atomic claims from the Pass-1 transcript, each with a verbatim $\leq\!25$-word quote; (b)~drop any claim whose quote does not string-match the transcript (a $0.85$ fuzzy threshold tolerates OCR/ASR noise); (c)~rewrite the review from only the surviving claims, adding no new facts. The filter is a tripwire, not a culler: $99.8\%$ of claims pass ($3731/3738$), so asked for a quote the model largely self-disciplines and stops emitting the bibliographic expansions Mode~B catalogues. It is RAG's attribution discipline applied \emph{within} a same-weights self-cascade---``retrieval'' is the Pass-1 transcript, ``attribution'' the verbatim quote.

\paragraph{Verdict: conditional on the dominant failure mode.} At $n{=}5$ on seven cells (full table and Pareto plot in Appendix~\ref{appdx:iter}), $C_{1\rm iter}$ reduces hallucinations on 5/7 but lowers coverage on all but one. It is a clean \emph{Pareto win} only on the short Natural~Vibration cell ($7.8{\to}3.6$h, $0.54{\to}0.66$cov), where the rewrite does not over-compress; a \emph{faithfulness-for-coverage trade} where Mode-B leak is heavy enough to pay the rewrite's compression cost (Heat~Pipes~Gemini, SP-5100, Megagauss); and \emph{net-negative} where Mode-A compression dominates or the Mode-B gain washes out at $n{=}5$ (Fairness~AI; Heat~Pipes~Claude, where the single-seed gain vanishes, echoing the citation-strip retraction of \S\ref{sec:failure_modes}). It suppresses Mode~B where Mode~B binds, not universally.

\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robust}

We ran four independent robustness checks; full tables and figures are in Appendices \ref{appdx:inter_judge} and \ref{appdx:probe_circ}.

\paragraph{Inter-judge replication on all 21 Gemini cells.}
Claude Opus 4.7 re-scored every $C_0$ and $C_1$ review using GPT-5.4's prompts (Figure~\ref{fig:inter_judge_scatter}). Direction agreement is \textbf{19/21 on coverage} and \textbf{14/21 on hallucination}, with per-probe agreement median $0.94$. Disagreements concentrate on near-tie cells (a $\pm 1$ judge difference flips direction) and high-count cells (the judges agree ``many claims are unsupported'' but differ in absolute count by $10{+}$). Claude systematically reports fewer hallucinations than GPT-5.4 (median ratio $0.71$), so the Mode~B finding holds under both judges at different absolute counts (Appendix~\ref{appdx:inter_judge}).

\paragraph{Probe-circularity check (6 cells).}
Probes are originally extracted by GPT-5.4, which also scores them. We re-extracted probes via Claude on six cells spanning the cascade-direction spectrum (largest audio win, near-noise paper, Mode-A and Mode-B counter-cells, high-baseline saturated, low-baseline long-paper) and re-scored the existing $C_0/C_1$ reviews. Under \emph{GPT-5.4 judge}, the cascade $\Delta_{\rm cov}$ sign is preserved on \textbf{6/6} (magnitude shifts $\leq 0.10$ pp on all). Under \emph{Claude judge} the sign flips on $1/6$ (Fairness~AI $-0.10\to +0.04$, within noise). The Mode-A loss on Fairness~AI and Mode-B loss on Natural~Vibration both survive both substitutions.

\paragraph{Reference-bias check (4 cells).}
The Gemini reference advantages models that share its transcription priors. We re-transcribed two audio cells with Whisper-base and re-OCRed two paper cells with EasyOCR, re-extracted probes via Claude, and re-judged with both judges (Appendix~\ref{appdx:probe_circ}). Cascade-coverage direction is preserved on $4/4$ under both judges, and cascade-hallucination direction is preserved everywhere except Thermal~Analysis under EasyOCR---a reference-side artifact, since EasyOCR's transcript is $\approx 21\%$ shorter and loses figure captions, so claims the cascade correctly reproduces read as unsupported against the noisier reference.

\paragraph{Multi-seed Pass-2 envelopes on four headline cells.}
We re-ran Gemini $C_1$ Pass-2 with five seeds at $T{=}0.7$ on four headline cells (Figure~\ref{fig:headline_multiseed}, Appendix~\ref{appdx:multiseed}). The three cells reported as wins all preserve their direction. The one exception is Perovskite, whose published $n{=}1$ coverage sits below all five seeds: its cascade-coverage loss shrinks from $-16$pp at $n{=}1$ to $-6$pp under multi-seed, below the per-cell noise floor (hence the near-noise-floor reclassification, \S\ref{sec:main}).

\paragraph{Three sources of stochasticity.}
Re-runs separate three independent noise sources (full decomposition in Appendix~\ref{appdx:stoch}): Pass-2 generation variance (Claude deprecates \texttt{temperature}, so determinism is not SDK-enforceable; Gemini at $T{=}0$ is mostly but not strictly deterministic), GPT-5.4 judge variance (essentially deterministic on low-count cells but $\pm 10$--$25$ on high-count cells, where long unsupported-claim lists run near the judge's completion budget), and the consistent observation that \emph{coverage is far more stable than hallucination counts} across all three. Two implications: headline aggregates use median substitution on the four high-count cells, and per-cell hallucination counts $>30$ carry $\pm 10$--$25$ judge-side uncertainty; and the Mode~B \emph{observation} survives all three noise sources while the Mode~B \emph{fix} (citation stripping) does not, which motivated $C_{1\rm iter}$ (\S\ref{sec:iter}).

\paragraph{A simple held-out predictor (LOOCV, $n{=}21$).}
A leave-one-out logistic-regression predictor on four cell-level features ($C_0$ probe coverage, $\log_{10}$ source words, $C_0$ unsupported-claim count, and a paper/audio dummy) reaches LOOCV AUC $= 0.65$ on $\text{sign}(\Delta_{\rm cov}>0)$. All four coefficient signs match the qualitative picture (higher baseline reduces cascade benefit, longer source reduces benefit, more $C_0$ hallucinations \emph{increases} benefit, paper modality harder than audio). AUC $0.65$ is meaningful but well below deployment-ready, so we report it as an honest $n{=}21$ baseline. Adding a citation-density feature drops AUC to $0.61$: the residual Mode-B structure is not captured by a single density feature at this sample size.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What generalizes, and what does not.} The portable result is not ``cascade always wins'' but the \emph{inverse-baseline law}: decomposition recovers content in proportion to how much the end-to-end pass satisfices, so it pays off precisely where a model reasons poorly over the raw modality and buys nothing where it already reasons well. This is what makes the cross-vendor picture coherent rather than contradictory---Claude's near-ceiling NASA papers, Gemini~2.5~Flash's strong end-to-end audio, and Gemini~Pro's weak end-to-end audio all fall on the same line. The most deployable corollary is that decomposition \emph{unlocks} text-only frontier models for audio: a model that cannot ingest audio at all (Claude, GPT-5.4) reviews it faithfully and completely from an off-the-shelf transcript, beating a native audio model's end-to-end pass on every cell we tried (Appendix~\ref{appdx:mixed}). In production, where one mixes the best model per stage, this is the operative finding.

\paragraph{Cascade cost.}
$C_0$ is one multimodal call; $C_1$ is $N+1$ calls (chunked OCR/ASR plus one text Pass-2), with call-count ratios ranging from $\approx 2\times$ on short audio to $\approx 177\times$ on the 176pp paper, and output-token volume scaling with source length. The 21-cell evidence says cascade pays off on long-form review where end-to-end has measurable headroom, but buys nothing on already-saturated cells (full cost-vs-gain scatter in Appendix~\ref{appdx:cost}).

\paragraph{Practitioner checklist.}
Table~\ref{tab:checklist} (Appendix~\ref{appdx:checklist}) compresses the per-regime recommendations: default $C_1$ on audio and moderate papers; $C_{1\rm iter}$ on Mode-B-dominant moderate papers; chunked Pass-2 ($C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$) on Mode-A-dominant long papers; and prefer $C_0$ where it fits on Mode-B-dominant or compounded long surveys.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Cross-vendor $n$.} We now have a complete within-Claude $C_0$-vs-$C_1$ comparison on six papers (9--104pp, Table~\ref{tab:claude_within}), on top of the 3/3-vendor Thermal cell. The cascade faithfulness direction replicates within Claude on 5/6, but the four short NASA papers are near-ceiling on $C_0$, so the comparison is not independently significant at $n{=}6$, and on papers longer than 104pp Claude $C_0$ exceeds Anthropic's per-request payload cap and remains unrealizable. The single-vendor strength of the headline statistic is therefore reduced but not eliminated. \emph{Judge variance.} The inter-judge replication does not address single-judge intra-rater variance from re-runs with different random seeds. \emph{Probe and reference bias.} Both are partially controlled (6 cells / 4 cells), and the cascade-hallucination direction on paper cells is partly bounded above by Gemini OCR's recall. \emph{$C_{1\rm iter}$ judge variance.} $C_{1\rm iter}$ is verified at $n{=}5$ (\S\ref{sec:iter}), but its two high-count Claude cells (SP-5100, Megagauss) inherit the $\pm10$--$25$ GPT-5.4 judge variance, so their trade verdicts rest on means with wide per-seed spread. \emph{Mimo audio bitrate.} Mimo audio cells ran at 16kbps mono (12kbps for Sapolsky) to fit OpenRouter's 10MB limit, while the Gemini reference used 32kbps, so Mimo cells partly conflate audio quality with model weakness. A clean, full-quality Gemini~2.5~Flash audio comparison (Appendix~\ref{appdx:flash_audio}) removes this confound and shows the cross-model audio effect is headroom-gated (a win where $C_0$ is weak, flat-to-negative where $C_0$ is already strong), consistent with the mechanism but not a uniform replication. \emph{Cross-vendor audio realizability.} A clean cross-\emph{company} same-weights audio cascade is currently impossible by API constraint, not choice: a same-weights cascade needs one model that both ingests audio (for $C_0$ and Pass-1) and produces text from text alone (for Pass-2), and no non-Google frontier model does both---Claude Opus 4.7 accepts no audio input, and OpenAI's audio model ingests audio but refuses text-only requests (both verified). This is itself a deployment observation: end-to-end long-audio review and a text-only synthesis pass rarely coexist in one model, so the same-weights audio cascade is realized on Gemini, while in production the natural form is a \emph{mixed}-model pipeline (a strong transcriber feeding a strong text synthesizer), which we probe in Appendix~\ref{appdx:mixed}. \emph{Task scope.} All measurements are on long-form review, so we make no claim about short-form QA, code, or agentic tasks. \emph{Mechanism $n$.} E1/E2 are multi-seed ($n{=}5$) on all 21 cells, E6 is confirmed on two open VLMs (3B, 7B) on clean rendered text, and the substrate null is now also confirmed \emph{behaviorally on the headline frontier models} for retrieval (E8, $K$ up to 48) and multi-hop reasoning (E9). Two honest caveats remain. First, E8/E9 are behavioral, not logit-lenses, since frontier activations are inaccessible. Second, the frontier models are so capable that clean-legible-\emph{text} accuracy stays at ceiling on Gemini at every difficulty we tried, so the Gemini reasoning equivalence (E9) is at ceiling; only Claude (run without extended thinking) gave a sub-ceiling regime, where the legible image was \emph{better} than text---directionally against H1, but a single-model headroom point. Real scanned/degraded pages also remain (E8/E9's degraded control is synthetic), and E4/E5 are single-seed controls (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}). The mechanism rests on E3 (all 21 cells), the multi-seed E2 collapse, and the E6/E8/E9 substrate null. The breadth gaps await the replication below.

\paragraph{Validation plan.}
The load-bearing generation-side effects (E1/E2) are already multi-seed on all 21 cells, the E6 substrate null is confirmed on two open VLMs, and E8/E9 now confirm it \emph{behaviorally on the headline frontier models} (retrieval and multi-hop reasoning), so the remaining replication is about \emph{breadth and external validity, not statistical power}. We scope it as follows. \emph{(1)~Model generality.} Extend the E6 logit-lens beyond the Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B pair to other open families (InternVL, Llama-Vision) and, where activations become accessible, lift the E8/E9 behavioral frontier confirmation to the activation level on frontier VLMs. Extend E7 across more omni models and higher-quality voices (e.g.\ CosyVoice2, whose packaged build was unusable in our environment) and push E7 into the multi-fact load regime, since E6 holds for documents under load but audio-under-load is still untested. \emph{(2)~Realistic-page stress.} Re-test E6 on real scanned pages (dense multi-column, equations, figures) and degraded scans, where a substrate/dilution effect is most likely to appear, since E4's DPI knob is inert on Gemini. \emph{(3)~Task generality.} Extend beyond long-form review to other generation-under-load tasks (long-document QA with many required facts, multi-document synthesis, meeting-minutes) and to a short-form control where the account predicts \emph{no} decomposition benefit (the bottleneck only binds when output demand is high). \emph{(4)~Causal budget manipulation.} Hold the pipeline fixed and vary only the synthesis output budget to confirm Mode~A is budget-bound, and ablate transcript completeness to map the coverage--faithfulness frontier.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Speech and documents.} Prior cascade-vs-end-to-end results are often read as substrate claims. Our mechanism study suggests they are at least partly about decomposition and modality-specific recoverability. Cuervo et al.~\cite{cuervo2025closing} document a text/speech understanding gap, and the Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\cite{ceh2026} shows speech-LLMs \emph{internally} behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades, while X-Talk~\cite{xtalk2025} finds explicitly cascaded speech-to-speech pipelines competitive. On documents, DocVLM~\cite{docvlm2024} reports OCR-augmented VLMs beating pure-vision VLMs on DocVQA, while the OCR-Reasoning Benchmark~\cite{ocrreasoning2026} shows pure-OCR cascades cap below 50\% where spatial layout carries meaning, ColPali~\cite{colpali2025} shows vision-only retrieval beats OCR, and GUI agents do better from raw pixels than from structured-text intermediates~\cite{uground2024,uitars2025}. The common thread is not ``text is a better substrate'' (our E6 finds no such gap for legible documents) but that decomposition helps when the task is generation-under-load and the content survives transcription, and hurts when the answer lives in layout/signal that transcription discards.

\paragraph{Step-Audio-R1: a contrast, not a corroboration.} Step-Audio-R1~\cite{stepaudior1} is the sharpest statement of the boundary, and it is worth being precise, because it cuts against a naive reading of our claim. They observe that audio LLMs, when asked to deliberate, spontaneously reason over a \emph{textual surrogate} of the audio (e.g.\ attributing musical sadness to ``lyrics mention sadness'' rather than to a minor key), and they treat this as the \emph{failure} their modality-grounded reasoning distillation must correct. That is consistent with us, not contradictory: their tasks (music, environmental sound, paralinguistics) are precisely the case where the answer is in the acoustic signal, so reducing to text discards it. We study the complementary regime, long-form review, where the payload is what was \emph{said}. Crucially, our activation tests find no reasoning-substrate gap for propositional content in \emph{either} modality (E6 documents, E7 audio). The Step-Audio-R1 effect is therefore best read as \emph{task-payload}-specific, since their tasks (music, environmental sound, paralinguistics) have answers that live in the acoustic signal, which transcription discards, rather than as a general claim that audio is a worse reasoning substrate. When the payload is propositional, as in long-form review, reducing to text loses nothing and the win is decomposition. Our Mode~B (the synthesis pass confabulates once the source is removed) is the within-regime echo of their concern.

\paragraph{Cascade theory and faithfulness.} Bengio's Consciousness Prior~\cite{bengio2017consciousness} argues for sparse discrete bottlenecks between perception and reasoning, and our externalized text bottleneck is a deployment-time analogue. Discrete JEPA~\cite{discretejepa2025} makes a related architectural argument within vision. Mode~B, where the model embellishes a citation surface form from training-time knowledge, is a self-cascade analogue of the unfaithfulness problem that motivates RAG~\cite{rag2020}, RARR~\cite{rarr2023}, and Attribution to Identified Sources~\cite{ais2022}. Our $C_{1\rm iter}$ variant adopts the in-context-attribution discipline of RARR but operates entirely within a same-weights self-cascade without external retrieval.

% =================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On long-form review, a same-weights two-pass decomposition beats end-to-end at $n{=}21$ on both faithfulness and coverage, even though the synthesis pass sees strictly less, and the benefit scales with how badly the monolithic pass satisfices ($r{=}-0.45$). The result survives median substitution, probe re-extraction, reference re-generation, and multi-seed Pass-2. Our mechanistic study locates the cause: not perception ($99.7\%$ of dropped content is in the model's own transcript) and not a text-vs-image substrate gap (no logit-lens gap on open VLMs, no behavioral gap on the headline frontier models), but \textbf{generation under load}, where one pass cannot perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review at once, and only a decomposition with independent output budgets recovers the content. The boundary is the account's two predicted breakdowns: synthesis-budget overflow (Mode~A) and source-removal confabulation (Mode~B), the latter targeted by the quote-grounded $C_{1\rm iter}$ variant. The practical rule is \textbf{perceive, externalize, then synthesize}. The win is the decomposition, not the modality---a deployment-time pattern with predictable failure modes, not a universal architecture.

\paragraph{E6: text is not an intrinsically better reasoning substrate.}
We tested H1 directly at the activation level on an open VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), controlling for prior knowledge with novel synthetic facts about nonsense entities (e.g.\ ``the Zorblatt device weighs 7 kilograms''). Each fact is presented either as digital text or as an \emph{image} of that same text, with a matched single-token question, and a layer-wise logit-lens reads off the log-probability and rank of the answer token at every layer. The two modalities are indistinguishable for an isolated fact, and---crucially---\emph{under load}: packing $K\in\{1,4,8,16,24\}$ facts into one stimulus, image and text stay at $1.00$ accuracy with a readout-depth gap $<1$ layer (TOST-equivalent within $\pm 1$ layer) at every $K$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mech_e6}). The same null replicates on a second VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B); the per-$K$ ladder for both models is in Appendix~\ref{appdx:vision_ladder}. For legibly rendered text there is simply no cross-modal reasoning penalty: the strong form of H1 is false for documents. \paragraph{E7: no audio substrate gap either, once the speech is intelligible.}
We replicate the E6 logit-lens on an omni model (Qwen2.5-Omni Thinker), presenting each novel fact as text vs as \emph{synthesized speech}, across three TTS engines of increasing quality (Table~\ref{tab:e7}). So neither documents (E6) nor audio (E7) carry an intrinsic reasoning-substrate penalty for propositional content. (The espeak contrast is reported as a cautionary result: garbled TTS manufactures a spurious gap, so audio-substrate claims must control for TTS intelligibility.)

\paragraph{E9: the substrate null extends from retrieval to multi-hop reasoning.}

On Gemini~3.1~Pro, text and legible image are both at ceiling and TOST-equivalent (gap $-0.013$). The substrate hypothesis H1 predicts image $<$ text under reasoning load, yet with headroom we observe image $\geq$ text. We could not drive clean-legible-\emph{text} accuracy below ceiling on Gemini at any difficulty we tried (sums of up to 12 facts, $k$th-largest over 32), so the Gemini equivalence is at ceiling while Claude supplies the headroom regime.

\section{Protocol details}
\label{appdx:protocol}

\paragraph{Reference quality.} Gemini~3.1~Pro is used for OCR/ASR with chunking. Single-call OCR on a 104-page paper produced a degenerate TOC dot-leader loop (113KB output, 56k ``words'' but only 264 real-word tokens, 6 page markers out of 104). Chunking (1 page per call, max 8192 output tokens) eliminates this failure mode. For audio we chunk into 30-min segments with 5-second overlap. We discovered a silent server-side truncation on one audio chunk (chunk~1 of IETF SNAC produced 1981 words instead of the expected $\approx 4368$, ending mid-sentence with \texttt{``Uh, well, see, yeah, that'\,''}). The protocol grew an automatic chunk-level word-count parity check after this incident.

\paragraph{Sources.} Audio: Karpathy ``State of GPT'' (42 min, 2023), MIT~6.034 L1 Patrick Winston (47 min, 2010), Sapolsky Behavioral~Genetics~1 Stanford (85 min, post-2026 reupload), Stanford Decarbonized~Power~Sector Zach~Ming (72 min, 2025), Doudna CRISPR Basics (48 min, 2017), 3Blue1Brown ``Attention in Transformers'' (26 min, 2024), Veritasium ``Strange Math'' (32 min, 2025), NeurIPS Black-in-AI panel (31 min, 2021), Harvard Law School Ames Moot Court (82 min, 2025), IETF SNAC WG interim 2026-04-16 (60 min, $\approx 5$ speakers, recorded post the most likely training-data cutoff). Papers: NASA NTRS IDs 19720009221 (Wagging Tail, 9pp), 19680000724 (Env Test, 18pp), 19690008405 (Dynamic Response, 41pp), 19690013408 (Natural Vibration, 42pp), 19700023812 (Thermal Analysis, 66pp), 19700025120 (Heat Pipes, 104pp), and SP-5100 (Shock and Vibration Technology, 176pp). The arXiv papers are arXiv:2605.09852 (Fairness~AI, 53pp), arXiv:2605.13991 (Perovskite Tandem PV, 80pp), and arXiv:2605.11379 (Megagauss Physics, 75pp). NASA papers are rendered as page images at 200 DPI, and arXiv papers at 200 DPI full color.

\paragraph{Models and snapshots.} The models are Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (\texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview}), Claude Opus 4.7 (\texttt{claude-opus-4-7}), and Mimo v2-omni (\texttt{xiaomi/mimo-v2-omni} via OpenRouter). The judge is GPT-5.4 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=high}, completion budget 24k tokens), routed via OpenRouter due to scope restrictions on the local OpenAI key.

\paragraph{Mimo audio constraint.} OpenRouter's Mimo provider enforces a 10MB base64 audio input limit. Karpathy required re-encoding from 32kbps to 16kbps mono mp3 to fit, and Sapolsky at 85 min required 12kbps. The Mimo audio Karpathy result ($C_0$ 4h/0.569, $C_1$ 4h/0.588 across 51 probes, 1509 / 1268 review words) is within noise across the $C_0\to C_1$ transition, so we treat it as a wash. \emph{gpt-audio} (via OpenRouter) accepted audio input but refused text-only Pass-2 with HTTP 400 ``This model requires that either input content or output modality contain audio''. The same-weights cascade is not realizable on this model, and $C_0$ alone scored 10h/0.765 on Karpathy.

\paragraph{Pareto terminology.} We call a cascade condition \emph{strictly Pareto-positive} relative to $C_0$ when it has both fewer hallucinations and higher coverage by amounts exceeding the noise floor. \emph{Pareto-positive within noise} means strictly better on one axis and no worse than noise on the other. \emph{Pareto-dominant} on a set means no other condition has lower hallucination at equal or higher coverage. These are presentation labels, not statistical claims.

\section{Mixed-model audio cascade: decomposition unlocks text-only models}
\label{appdx:mixed}

The same-weights audio cascade is Gemini-only by API constraint (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). In production one would not use one model for everything; one would use the best model per stage. This probe does exactly that, and is therefore \emph{off-protocol}: it mixes vendors across stages, so it speaks to the generality of \emph{decomposition}, not the same-weights claim. We take an \emph{independent}, non-Google Pass-1 transcript from OpenAI \texttt{gpt-audio} (chunked, full quality---distinct from the Gemini reference used for judging), then run a text-only synthesis Pass-2 with three frontier text models, two of which (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.4) cannot ingest audio at all on their own. Reviews are scored on the shared Pro reference and probes with the GPT-5.4 judge, plus a Claude-judge cross-check on the GPT-5.4-authored review to rule out self-grading.

The result is unambiguous (Table~\ref{tab:mixed}): on all three cells, every mixed pipeline beats Gemini's \emph{end-to-end} $C_0$ on both axes---coverage rises to $0.90$--$1.00$ (vs $C_0$ $0.63$--$0.88$) and hallucinations fall to $2$--$9$ (vs $11$--$17$). Decomposition thus \emph{unlocks} text-only frontier models for audio review and lets them outperform the native audio model's end-to-end pass, using an off-the-shelf transcript. The GPT-5.4 self-grading concern is unfounded: the Claude judge scores the GPT-5.4-authored reviews \emph{lower} on hallucinations (1/3/3 vs the GPT-5.4 judge's 3/6/9), consistent with the $0.71\times$ inter-judge ratio (\S\ref{sec:robust}). The mixed pipeline trails the Gemini same-weights $C_1$ only where \texttt{gpt-audio}'s transcript is lossier than Gemini's own ASR (MIT coverage $0.90$ vs $0.98$), i.e.\ the ceiling is set by Pass-1 transcript quality, exactly as the perceive--externalize--synthesize account predicts.

\paragraph{Detailed decomposition.}
\emph{(a)~Claude Pass-2 generation variance.} Same input + same API + same prompt: Heat~Pipes Claude $C_1$ swings $20\to 9$ between two runs (strip $13\to 14$ more stable), and the deltas are large enough that the strip effect cannot be distinguished from Pass-2 noise. Claude Opus 4.7 deprecates the \texttt{temperature} parameter so determinism cannot be enforced from the SDK. \emph{(b)~Gemini Pass-2 generation variance.} At \texttt{temperature=0.0}, two of three Gemini Pass-2 re-runs produced byte-identical output (MD5 match) on Heat~Pipes / SP-5100, and the third differed in similar length (1929 vs 1815 words, $\Delta h = +6$). Gemini is therefore mostly but not strictly deterministic, consistent with GPU-level non-associativity. \emph{(c)~GPT-5.4 high-reasoning judge variance.} Essentially deterministic on low-count cells ($\pm 1$ on Heat~Pipes Claude across three re-judgments) but substantially stochastic on high-count cells ($\pm 25$ on SP-5100, $\pm 17$ on the byte-identical SP-5100 Gemini review). We attribute this to long unsupported-claim lists running up against our 24k-token completion budget, so the judge truncates and resamples across calls. Coverage is stable on every cell across judge re-runs.

\section{Specific hallucination examples (Heat Pipes Claude)}
\label{appdx:examples}

The 20 unsupported claims from Claude $C_1$ on Heat~Pipes 104pp are dominated by training-prior expansions of bibliographic abbreviations:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item ``Cotter's 1965 analysis (LA-3246-MS)'': the transcript cites only ``Reference 2 (Cotter)'', so the year and report number are training-data details.
\item ``correlation from Allingham and McEntire (1961)'': the transcript only says ``Ref.~12'', so the author names and year are training-data details.
\item ``McAdams' laminar horizontal-tube formula'': the transcript says ``Reference 13 for laminar condensation on horizontal tubes'', so ``McAdams'' is a training-data detail.
\item ``Vapor chamber-fin radiator (Haller, Lieblein, and Lindow at NASA Lewis)'': the transcript says only ``proposed by Haller, et al of NASA''.
\end{itemize}

One outright misreading: the transcript states the no-boiling condition as $p_v - p_\ell \le 2\sigma\cos\theta/r_c$, but Claude's review writes $\ge$. This is a sign error in Pass-2 reading of Pass-1 text, not training-prior leak.
