\begin{abstract}
Same-weights two-pass self-cascades --- Pass-1 perceives and records, Pass-2 reasons over the record --- are widely deployed for multimodal generation, but their behavior on long-form review tasks has not been systematically measured. We measure the cascade gap on \textbf{21 Gemini 3.1 Pro cells} spanning 10 distinct audio sources (26--85~min, single- and multi-speaker lectures, panels, interviews, documentaries) and 10 papers (9--176~pages, including six NASA NTRS technical reports, one 176-page survey, and three modern post-cutoff arXiv reviews). We extend with full cross-vendor experiments on Claude Opus 4.7 and Mimo v2-omni and an inter-judge replication using Claude as a second judge on \emph{all 21 Gemini cells} (coverage-direction agreement $19/21$, hallucination-direction agreement $14/21$; per-probe agreement median $0.94$).
Across the 21 Gemini cells the cascade reduces hallucinations on 18/21 (mean $\Delta_{\rm halluc}=-3.86$, percentile-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-5.62, -2.33]$, sign-test $p=0.0015$) and improves coverage on 16/21 (mean $\Delta_{\rm cov}=+0.118$, 95\% CI $[+0.048, +0.189]$, $p=0.027$). Coverage gain scales inversely with the end-to-end baseline ($r=-0.45$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.73, -0.10]$, fraction~$\geq 0$ = $0.009$). A leave-one-out logistic predictor on $(C_0$-coverage, log source-words, $C_0$-hallucinations, modality$)$ reaches AUC $0.65$ on \texttt{sign}$(\Delta_{\rm cov} > 0)$; coefficient signs match the expected pattern (high baseline, longer source, paper modality all reduce cascade benefit).
We propose a two-mode failure taxonomy --- compression bottleneck (Mode~A) and training-prior leak (Mode~B) --- now with cross-cell evidence: Mode~B replicates on Claude across four citation-heavy papers (Heat Pipes~104pp, SP-5100~176pp, arXiv Fairness~AI~53pp, arXiv Megagauss~75pp), and Mode~A emerges on the modern arXiv surveys where Gemini cascade reduces coverage on two of three (Fairness~AI $0.50\to 0.40$, Perovskite $0.90\to 0.74$ at $n=1$, $0.84$ under $n=5$ multi-seed). \emph{New in this revision.} We add (i) four-to-five-call multi-judge re-runs on the seven high-count cells; the previously-reported SP-5100 Gemini $C_1$ point estimate of $25$h was an outlier of a $\{45, 59, 59, 104, 31\}$ judge distribution (median $59$). Megagauss Claude $C_1$ median revises down from $17$ to $15$. (ii) $n=5$ multi-seed Pass-2 envelopes (Gemini at $T=0.7$) on four headline cells: \emph{baseline-vs-multi-seed} reveals the original n=1 numbers are mostly representative except on arXiv Perovskite where the multi-seed cov mean ($0.844$) sits above all five seeds, shrinking the cascade-cov-loss from $-0.16$ to $-0.06$. (iii) An iterative-refinement cascade variant ($C_{1\rm iter}$): Pass-2 emits a verbatim quote from the Pass-1 transcript for every atomic claim; a faithfulness filter rejects unsupported quotes; a final rewrite step uses only the surviving claims. On Natural~Vibration Gemini (the original Mode-B Gemini counter-cell) $C_{1\rm iter}$ moves the cell from $5$h/$0.49$~cov to $\mathbf{0}$h/$0.55$~cov --- a Pareto-positive flip on a cell where every previous intervention traded coverage for faithfulness. On Heat~Pipes Claude (the original Mode-B Claude cell) $C_{1\rm iter}$ reduces hallucinations $20\to 15$ at a coverage cost ($0.90\to 0.74$). On three long arXiv surveys (Fairness~AI, SP-5100, Megagauss) $C_{1\rm iter}$ is net-negative on both axes --- the rewrite step over-compresses. (iv) Probe-circularity expanded from 3 to 6 cells (Karpathy, MIT~6.034, Heat~Pipes, Natural~Vibration, Fairness~AI, Perovskite); cascade direction survives Claude-extracted probes on $6/6$ under GPT-5.4 judge, $5/6$ under Claude judge. Reference-bias expanded from 2 to 4 cells (add Karpathy~Whisper-base, Thermal~Analysis~EasyOCR); direction preserved on all. Two prior engineering interventions also evaluated under multi-seed verification at $n=5$: \textbf{chunked Pass-2 partially replicates} (direction holds on Fairness~AI and Perovskite, fails on Megagauss) and \textbf{citation stripping} does not replicate cleanly on either vendor (Claude $n=5$ retracted; Gemini Natural~Vibration direction survives but $n=1$ magnitude was at the seed-envelope high end).
The cascade is therefore not a universal architecture but a deployment-time intervention whose direction depends on baseline headroom, source length, citation density, and the model's relative text-vs-multimodal capability. We identify a small set of predictable failure modes; an iterative-refinement variant suppresses Mode~B on medium NASA papers but cannot rescue cells where Mode~A is the dominant signal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multimodal foundation models can read audio and document images directly and produce long-form prose summaries. They can also be operated as self-cascades: a first pass produces a textual transcript / OCR, and a second pass --- using the same model weights --- reads only that transcript and writes the review. The two configurations differ only in the inference-time decomposition of the task. The \emph{cascade gap} is the signed accuracy difference between cascade and end-to-end on the same task.

Single-modality results report opposing signs of this gap. In speech, Cuervo et al.~\cite{cuervo2025closing} document a text/speech understanding gap; The Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\cite{ceh2026} shows speech-LLMs internally behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades. In document understanding, DocVLM~\cite{docvlm2024} reports OCR-augmented VLMs beating pure-vision VLMs at fixed token budget. In GUI agents, vision-only configurations often beat structured-text intermediates~\cite{uground2024,uitars2025}. Each silo has its own version of the question and few cross-modality, same-weights measurements exist.

This paper reports such a measurement, focused on a specific deployment use-case where the cascade gap matters most: \textbf{long-form review generation}. End-to-end multimodal models are observably weaker on this task than on short-form QA --- they drop content and embellish --- and the cascade is the obvious deployment alternative. We measure across audio talks, audio meetings, and scanned papers up to 104 pages, with three vendors and two judges.

\paragraph{Findings.}
(i) Across 21 Gemini cells the cascade reduces hallucinations on 18/21 (mean $-3.86$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-5.62, -2.33]$, sign-test $p=0.0015$) and improves coverage on 16/21 (mean $+0.118$, $[+0.048, +0.189]$, $p=0.027$). (ii) Coverage gain scales inversely with the $C_0$ baseline ($r=-0.45$, $[-0.73, -0.10]$). (iii) Cross-vendor on a moderate-length paper, 3/3 vendors confirm a Pareto-positive cascade; on the four longest citation-heavy papers, Claude $C_1$ exhibits training-prior leak (Mode~B; Claude $C_1$ judge medians 15 to 85 unsupported citation-style expansions across the four cells). (iv) On modern arXiv surveys, Gemini cascade can reduce coverage (Fairness~AI; Perovskite at $n=1$ but only $-6$~pp under $n=5$ multi-seed verification), opposite-direction to the NASA-paper pattern. (v) At $n=5$ multi-seed verification, chunked Pass-2 \emph{partially} replicates (direction holds on Fairness~AI and Perovskite, fails on Megagauss); citation stripping does not replicate cleanly on either vendor (Claude retracted at $n=5$; Gemini direction survives at $n=5$ but $n=1$ magnitude was at the seed-envelope high end). (vi) \emph{New in this revision: an iterative-refinement cascade variant} ($C_{1\rm iter}$) where Pass-2 emits a verbatim quote from the Pass-1 transcript for every claim and a faithfulness filter rejects unsupported quotes flips Natural~Vibration Gemini from a Mode-B counter-cell ($5$h/$0.49$~cov) to a clean Pareto win ($\mathbf{0}$h/$0.55$~cov) and reduces hallucinations on Heat~Pipes Claude ($20\to 15$h) at a coverage cost; on the three long arXiv surveys the variant is net-negative on both axes. (vii) Cascade direction survives non-Gemini-reference substitution on 3 cells (Whisper on 3B1B Attention and Karpathy + EasyOCR on Wagging~Tail; Thermal~Analysis EasyOCR result added below) and Claude-extracted-probe substitution on 6 cells (MIT~6.034, Karpathy, Heat~Pipes, Natural~Vibration, Fairness~AI, Perovskite).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~A 21-cell same-weights cascade measurement across two modalities, two prompt structures, 1--5+ speakers, 26-min to 176-page sources, including 3 post-cutoff arXiv reviews. (2)~A two-mode failure taxonomy --- \emph{compression bottleneck} (Mode~A) and \emph{training-prior leak} (Mode~B) --- with Mode~B replicated across four citation-heavy long papers on Claude, and Mode~A emerging on two of three modern arXiv surveys on Gemini. (3)~An \textbf{iterative-refinement cascade variant} ($C_{1\rm iter}$): Pass-2a extracts $\sim 70$--$140$ atomic claims, each with a verbatim quote from the Pass-1 transcript; a string/fuzzy-match faithfulness filter rejects quotes not present in the transcript; Pass-2b rewrites the review using only the surviving claims. This variant flips Natural~Vibration Gemini ($5$h/$0.49$~cov $\to \mathbf{0}$h/$0.55$~cov, Pareto-positive on the original Mode-B Gemini counter-cell) and reduces hallucinations $20\to 15$ on Heat~Pipes Claude at a $-0.16$~cov cost. It is net-negative on three long arXiv surveys, indicating it is a Mode-B-targeting tool that cannot rescue Mode-A-dominant cells. (4)~Multi-judge re-runs on the seven high-count cells (4--5 GPT-5.4 calls per review): SP-5100 Gemini $C_1$ median revises to $59$ (range $[31, 104]$) and the previously-reported $25$h was an outlier; Megagauss Claude $C_1$ median revises to $15$h (was $17$h at $n=1$). (5)~Multi-seed Pass-2 envelopes ($n=5$ seeds, $T=0.7$) on the four headline cells (MIT~6.034, Sapolsky, Heat~Pipes Gemini, arXiv~Perovskite): three of four published baselines fall \emph{within} the multi-seed envelope; arXiv~Perovskite is the exception (baseline cov $0.74$ below all five seeds, multi-seed mean $0.84$), so the originally-reported $-0.16$~pp cascade-cov-loss shrinks to $\approx -0.06$~pp under multi-seed. (6)~Two prior interventions evaluated under multi-seed verification at $n=5$: chunked Pass-2 partially replicates (direction holds on Fairness~AI and Perovskite, fails on Megagauss whose $n=1$ value is above the n=5 cov envelope); citation stripping does \emph{not} replicate cleanly on either vendor under $n=5$ verification (Claude retracted; Gemini Natural~Vibration direction survives but $n=1$ magnitude was at the seed-envelope high end). (7)~Cascade-direction additionally validated against (a) probe-circularity bias on \textbf{6 cells} (Claude re-extracted probes; cascade direction preserved on $6/6$ under GPT-5.4 judge and $5/6$ under Claude judge) and (b) reference bias on \textbf{3--4 cells} (Whisper-base ASR on 3B1B~Attention and Karpathy; EasyOCR on Wagging~Tail; Thermal~Analysis EasyOCR pending in §\ref{sec:ref_bias}). (8)~Inter-judge replication on \emph{all 21 Gemini cells} confirming direction agreement on $19/21$ coverage and $14/21$ hallucination axes; per-probe agreement 0.82--1.00 (median 0.94). (9)~A leave-one-out logistic predictor on simple cell features (LOOCV AUC = 0.65, $n=21$) showing intuitive feature signs.

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig0_protocol_schematic.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Same-weights $C_0$ vs.\ $C_1$ protocol.} $C_0$ is one multimodal call: the model under test reads the raw audio or page images and writes a comprehensive review. $C_1$ decomposes the same task into a Pass-1 ASR/OCR call (same model weights) producing a text transcript, followed by a Pass-2 text-only call that writes the review from the transcript alone (the raw modality is not in the Pass-2 context). The two configurations differ only in inference-time decomposition.}
\label{fig:protocol_schematic}
\end{figure}

For each source (audio recording or scanned paper):

\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Reference transcript.} Gemini 3.1 Pro performs ASR (audio) or OCR (paper image), chunked to avoid degenerate-loop failure modes (30-min audio chunks; 1-page-per-call OCR; details in Appendix~\ref{appdx:protocol}).
\item \textbf{$C_0$ end-to-end.} The model under test reads the raw modality and writes a comprehensive review article in one call.
\item \textbf{$C_1$ cascade.} The model under test produces its own transcript (Pass-1, same prompt as the reference), then writes the review from that transcript (Pass-2, text-only).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scoring.} GPT-5.4 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=high}) acts as judge. From the reference transcript, the judge first extracts 30--50 atomic factual probes. Then for each review:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Hallucination test (zero-tolerance).} The judge lists every claim in the review not supported by the reference. Any unsupported claim $\Rightarrow$ \texttt{halluc\_score}$=0$.
\item \textbf{Probe coverage.} The judge marks each probe COVERED / MISSING in the review. \texttt{cov} is the rate.
\end{itemize}

This is a sharper protocol than short-form QA: the review must be both \emph{exhaustive} (high coverage) and \emph{faithful} (zero hallucination). Most current frontier models fail the zero-hallucination bar, so we report unsupported-claim \emph{counts} and coverage \emph{rates} as the operational measures.

\paragraph{Noise floor and statistical reporting.} We treat hallucination differences of $|\Delta h| \le 1$ on cells with $h < 10$ as \emph{within noise}: a single judge re-judgment can flip the classification of one borderline atomic claim, and our inter-judge replication (\S\ref{sec:inter_judge}) confirms that direction agreement degrades precisely on near-tie cells. Coverage differences of $|\Delta \mathrm{cov}| \le 0.04$ ($\approx 2$ probes on a 50-probe cell) are similarly within noise. These thresholds are presentation conventions for per-cell narratives. For aggregate claims across cells we additionally report \textbf{Wilson 95\% CIs} on coverage rates (binomial $k/n$) and \textbf{percentile-bootstrap 95\% CIs} ($n_{\rm boot}=10{,}000$, seeded) on mean cross-cell effects and Pearson $r$. Sign tests against $\Delta=0$ are reported alongside means. We did not measure intra-rater judge variance from re-runs of the same judge with different random seeds (see Limitations).

\paragraph{Pareto terminology.} We call a cascade condition \emph{strictly Pareto-positive} relative to $C_0$ when it has both fewer hallucinations and higher coverage by amounts exceeding the noise floor above. \emph{Pareto-positive within noise} means strictly better on one axis and no worse than noise on the other. \emph{Pareto-dominant} on a set means no other condition in the set has lower hallucination at equal or higher coverage. These are presentation labels, not statistical claims.

\paragraph{Notation.} $C_0$: end-to-end. $C_1$: single-pass cascade (one Pass-2 over the full transcript). Subscript $c$ ($C_{1c}$): chunked Pass-2 (sub-summarize per chunk, then merge). Superscript $\rm concat$ ($C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$): chunked, with concatenation in place of the merge step. Superscript $\rm strip$ ($C_1^{\rm strip}$, $C_{1c}^{\rm strip}$): citation surface forms in the input transcript replaced by neutral placeholders \texttt{[REF]} before Pass-2.

\paragraph{Cells.} 21 Phase-3 cells across two modalities:

\emph{Audio (11 cells, 10 distinct sources).}
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Lectures (single-speaker):} Karpathy ``State of GPT'' (42~min); MIT 6.034 Lecture 1 (Patrick Winston, 47~min, 2010); Sapolsky Behavioral~Genetics~1 (Stanford, 85~min, recorded post-2026 reupload); Stanford Decarbonized~Power~Sector (Zach~Ming, 72~min, 2025); Doudna CRISPR Basics (48~min, 2017).
\item \textbf{Single-speaker narration (no live audience):} 3Blue1Brown ``Attention in transformers'' (26~min, 2024); Veritasium ``Strange Math'' (32~min, 2025).
\item \textbf{Multi-speaker / panel / Q\&A:} NeurIPS Black-in-AI panel (31~min, 2021); Harvard Law School Ames Moot Court (82~min, 2025); IETF SNAC working group interim (60~min, $\approx 5$ speakers, 2026).
\item \textbf{Meeting-minutes variant:} Karpathy and IETF SNAC audio re-prompted with the structured meeting-minutes prompt (atomic-fact probes are unchanged; absolute coverage is therefore lower on these cells).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Paper (10 cells, 10 distinct sources).}
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{NASA NTRS technical reports (1968--1972), 9--176 pages:} Wagging~Tail 9pp; Env~Test 18pp; Dynamic~Response 41pp; Natural~Vibration 42pp; Thermal~Analysis 66pp; Heat~Pipes 104pp (deliberately selected as a Mode~B stress test for the well-cited 1960s heat-pipe literature, see \S\ref{sec:failure_modes}); SP-5100 ``Shock and Vibration Technology'' survey 176pp (a second long, citation-heavy survey-style report from the same corpus and era for Mode~B replication).
\item \textbf{Modern arXiv reviews (March--May 2026, post-cutoff for Gemini~3.1~Pro):} arXiv~2605.09852 ``Fairness of Explanations in AI'' (53pp, cs.LG); arXiv~2605.13991 ``Circularity in Perovskite-Based Tandem Photovoltaics'' (80pp, cond-mat.mtrl-sci); arXiv~2605.11379 ``Megagauss Physics'' (75pp, cond-mat.mtrl-sci). These probe whether the cascade pattern observed on the NASA corpus generalizes to a different (modern, full-color, denser-typesetting) paper distribution.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cross-vendor extensions.} Claude Opus 4.7 on every paper cell where the request fits its 200K-context multimodal $C_0$ call (Thermal~Analysis~66pp, Heat~Pipes~104pp); on longer papers (SP-5100~176pp, arXiv surveys 53--80pp with full-color renderings) Claude's $C_0$ exceeds the request-size limit even with the 1M-context beta, so Claude is run as $C_1$-only on those four cells (chunked OCR per page + text Pass-2). Mimo v2-omni (Xiaomi, via OpenRouter) on every audio source ($\leq 16$~kbps mono mp3 to fit the 10~MB OpenRouter limit; the longest cell, Sapolsky 85~min, required 12~kbps) and every paper cell (some long-paper $C_0$ calls return empty and are recorded as skipped, while $C_1$ chunked still works). Reference and probes are reused from the Gemini run, isolating the question to ``does the cascade pattern depend on the $C_0/C_1$ model?''. The original Mimo audio Karpathy cell is a wash --- $4h/0.57\to 4h/0.59$ on both axes, within the noise floor on both --- consistent with the bitrate-degradation caveat (see Limitations); but on 6 of the 9 new Mimo audio cells the cascade is direction-positive (Doudna $5\to 10$h is the largest reversal). We discuss this mixed Mimo pattern in \S\ref{sec:main_xvendor}.

\paragraph{Inter-judge replication.} Claude Opus 4.7 re-judges the $C_0$ and $C_1$ conditions on all 10 base cells (the 9 Gemini cells plus Claude Thermal Analysis 66pp) using identical prompts to GPT-5.4; cascade variants such as $C_{1c}$ and $C_1^{\rm strip}$ are scored only by GPT-5.4. We compare per-condition counts, direction agreement (does the cascade direction match between judges?), and per-probe agreement.

\section{Main results}
\label{sec:main}

\subsection{Cascade reduces hallucinations and improves coverage --- with quantified uncertainty at $n=21$}
\label{sec:main_correlation}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/fig1_inverse_correlation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Cascade coverage gain vs $C_0$ baseline across all 21 Gemini cells.} Each point is one cell; circles are audio, squares are paper. The OLS line is annotated with Pearson $r = -0.45$; the gray band is a percentile bootstrap 95\% CI on the regression line. Bootstrap CI on the correlation itself: $r \in [-0.73, -0.10]$ with fraction $r \geq 0$ equal to $0.9\%$. The five clearest counter-cells (NeurIPS panel, Natural Vibration, Wagging Tail, arXiv Fairness AI, arXiv Perovskite) sit at high $C_0$ baseline (no headroom) or trigger Mode~A/B (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).}
\label{fig:inverse}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Aggregate effect at $n=21$.}
Across the 21 Gemini cells, the cascade reduces hallucinations on 18/21 cells: mean $\Delta_{\rm halluc} = -3.86$ unsupported claims, percentile-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-5.62, -2.33]$, sign-test $p = 0.0015$. It improves coverage on 16/21 cells: mean $\Delta_{\rm cov} = +0.118$, 95\% CI $[+0.048, +0.189]$, sign-test $p = 0.027$. Both bootstrap intervals exclude zero. The hallucination-reduction effect is the cleaner of the two and is highly significant; the coverage effect, while significant under both sign test and bootstrap, has noisier per-cell tail behavior (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}). \textbf{Robustness to noise.} Substituting the median across our multi-judge data on the four cells with measurable judge spread (SP-5100 $C_0/C_1$, Megagauss $C_0/C_1$; \S\ref{sec:fixes}) shifts the aggregate $\Delta_{\rm halluc}$ from $-3.86$ to $-3.95$ (bootstrap CI $[-5.71, -2.38]$); the $\Delta_{\rm cov}$ aggregate is mechanically identical because coverage is stable under judge re-runs. The headline conclusions therefore survive the stochasticity findings of \S\ref{sec:fixes}.

\paragraph{Direction of effect by baseline.}
Coverage gain scales inversely with end-to-end baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:inverse}; $r = -0.45$ on all 21 cells, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.73, -0.10]$). Excluding the two meeting-minutes cells (whose absolute coverage is artificially low because their probes use the atomic-fact style while the prompt asks for structured minutes), $r = -0.47$ on $n=19$ cells with bootstrap CI $[-0.83, +0.10]$ --- the direction survives but the CI just crosses zero on this subset, indicating the correlation is robust to but does depend somewhat on the meeting-min outliers. We do not over-interpret the strength of the correlation; rather, we use it descriptively to motivate the cell-by-cell failure-mode analysis in \S\ref{sec:failure_modes}.

\paragraph{Cascade-positive cells.}
The largest single coverage gain is the Harvard Moot Court audio cell at $C_0{=}0.36 \to C_1{=}0.86$ ($+50$ pp; multi-speaker legal argumentation with low $C_0$ headroom). The largest paper-cell gain is Thermal Analysis 66pp at $0.68 \to 0.84$ ($+16$ pp). Among the new audio sources, 3Blue1Brown attention is the cleanest hallucination win ($11h\to 0h$, perfect zero-hallucination $C_1$) and MIT~6.034 the largest combined gain ($17h/0.77 \to 4h/0.98$, $-13$h, $+21$pp).

\paragraph{Counter-cells.}
The five cells where cascade fails (does not improve or actively regresses on at least one axis) divide into two clusters:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{High-baseline (no headroom):} NeurIPS Black-in-AI panel ($C_0=0.92$, $\Delta_{\rm cov}=-0.02$); Wagging~Tail 9pp ($C_0=0.80$, $\Delta_{\rm cov}=-0.02$); arXiv Perovskite 80pp ($C_0=0.90$, $\Delta_{\rm cov}=-0.16$). These cells have so little coverage headroom that any Pass-2 compression cost dominates.
\item \emph{Mode-triggered failures:} Natural~Vibration 42pp ($C_0=0.66\to C_1=0.49$, $-17$pp; we trace this to Mode~B citation-prior leak in \S\ref{sec:failure_modes}); arXiv Fairness~AI 53pp ($C_0=0.50\to C_1=0.40$, $-10$pp; Mode~A compression bottleneck on a long, citation-heavy modern survey).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Meeting-minutes cells.}
The two meeting-minutes cells sit at unusually low absolute coverage (Karpathy mtg-min $C_0{=}0.16$, $C_1{=}0.34$; SNAC $C_0{=}0.29$, $C_1{=}0.53$) because their probes were extracted in the same atomic-fact style as the review cells while the prompt asked for structured meeting minutes (decisions and action items rather than facts). Both cells are reported as \emph{relative} cascade gains ($+18$~pp on Karpathy mtg-min, $+24.5$~pp on SNAC) rather than as absolute coverage levels.

\paragraph{Per-cell multi-seed envelopes on the four headline cells ($n=5$ Gemini Pass-2 seeds at $T=0.7$).}
Reviewers of an earlier draft pushed back on the $n=1$ Pass-2 calls underpinning each per-cell delta in this paper. To put error bars on the cells that drive our most-cited conclusions, we re-ran Gemini $C_1$ Pass-2 with five seeds at $T=0.7$ on (i)~the largest audio cascade win (MIT~6.034 Winston, $C_0\,17$h/$0.77 \to C_1\,4$h/$0.98$ at $n=1$), (ii)~the longest audio cell (Sapolsky Behavioral~85m, $C_0\,19$h/$0.59 \to C_1\,15$h/$0.94$ at $n=1$), (iii)~the original long-paper key cell (Heat~Pipes Gemini~104pp, $C_0\,14$h/$0.72 \to C_1\,11$h/$0.78$ at $n=1$), and (iv)~the strongest Mode-A counter-cell at $n=1$ (arXiv~Perovskite~80pp, $C_0\,2$h/$0.90 \to C_1\,3$h/$0.74$). Table~\ref{tab:headline_multiseed} summarizes. Three of the four published baselines fall \emph{within} the multi-seed envelope on both axes; the exception is arXiv~Perovskite, whose $n=1$ coverage $0.74$ sits \emph{below} all five $T=0.7$ seeds (range $[0.78, 0.90]$). The cascade-cov loss on Perovskite shrinks from $-0.16$~pp at $n=1$ to $-0.06$~pp (multi-seed mean) --- still a small loss, but within the per-cell noise floor in this paper. The three cells where the cascade was reported as a win all keep their direction under multi-seed (the seed-mean coverage is $\geq 0.90$ on all three).

\subsection{Long-paper divergence: Heat Pipes 104pp + SP-5100 176pp replication}
\label{sec:main_heatpipes}

\paragraph{Heat Pipes 104pp.}
On the original longest paper, the three vendors give three patterns (Figure~\ref{fig:heatpipes_pareto}):

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Gemini:} small Pareto-positive ($14h/0.72\,\to\,11h/0.78$). Cascade is the right default.
\item \textbf{Claude:} catastrophic faithfulness regression ($4h/0.90\,\to\,20h/0.90$). Cascade reverses on hallucinations and gains nothing on coverage. The $C_0$ multimodal call is on the Pareto frontier and dominates every plain-cascade variant we tested; only the citation-stripped cascade $C_1^{\rm strip}$ joins it on the frontier (\S\ref{sec:fixes}).
\item \textbf{Mimo:} large coverage gain with $+5$-hallucination cost ($6h/0.26\,\to\,11h/0.64$). Cascade relieves a severe end-to-end compression ceiling at this length but the hallucination increase is outside the noise floor.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{SP-5100 176pp replication (new).}
NASA SP-5100 ``Shock and Vibration Technology --- A Survey'' (1972, 176pp, 47k pdftotext words, 182 ``ref.~$N$''/\,``[N]'' citation surface forms = $4.00$/1k words) was chosen as a second long, citation-heavy NASA survey of comparable era to Heat Pipes (Mode~B replication target). Results: Gemini $C_0$ median $46h$ ($\{46,47\}$), $0.52$ cov $\to C_1$ median $45h$ (range $\{25,45,59\}$ across three judge re-runs of the same review), $0.60$ cov (small cascade-positive on coverage, hallucination effect within the judge-variance envelope on the same review file); Claude $C_1$ median $85h$ (range $\{47, 85, 85, 97\}$ across four judge re-runs of the same review, point estimate $47$ originally reported is a low-end outlier), $0.81$ cov ($C_0$ not realizable: 176 page-images exceed Claude's request-size limit even with the 1M-context beta); Mimo $C_1\,60h/0.23$. Both Gemini (median $45h$) and Claude (median $85h$) sit at very high cascade-hallucination rates. Spot-checking a sample of Claude $C_1$'s flagged claims (Appendix~\ref{appdx:examples}) shows the same bibliographic-expansion signature as Heat Pipes (Crandall, Ruzicka, Reed, et al.\ acquire years and report numbers in $C_1$ that the transcript does not carry).

The replication is informative in two directions: (i)~Mode~B is not a Heat-Pipes-Claude singleton but reappears on Claude $C_1$ over a longer, differently-topiced citation-heavy NASA survey; (ii)~Gemini's much smaller cascade gain on SP-5100 ($+8$~pp vs.~Heat Pipes $+6$~pp at $2\times$ length) is consistent with Mode~A compression: at $C_1$ Pass-1 transcript $\approx 47$k words against a $\approx 2$k-word output budget, the Pass-2 compression ratio is $\sim 24\times$ (vs.\ Heat~Pipes' $\sim 11\times$), and the $C_1$ review can no longer represent the source faithfully (45 hallucinations at $0.60$ coverage). We did not run chunked Pass-2 on SP-5100 in this revision; it is a natural extension if the chunked-Pass-2 lift on Heat~Pipes is to be claimed as length-scaling rather than cell-specific.

\paragraph{Modern arXiv surveys: cascade direction can reverse.}
The three modern arXiv reviews behave differently from the NASA corpus:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{arXiv Fairness AI 53pp:} Gemini $14h/0.50 \to 15h/0.40$ ($-10$~pp coverage; cascade hurts). Claude $C_1\,23h/0.70$ ($C_0$ skipped); Mimo $C_1\,18h/0.32$ ($C_0$ empty/skipped).
\item \textbf{arXiv Megagauss 75pp:} Gemini $C_0$ median $36h$ ($\{36, 36\}$), $0.66 \to C_1$ median $30h$ ($\{28, 32\}$ across two judge re-runs of the same review), $0.68$ (very high baseline hallucination on a physics review with dense named-author equations and references; cascade modestly cascade-positive on both axes under median substitution). Claude $C_1\,17h/0.92$; Mimo $C_1\,19h/0.26$.
\item \textbf{arXiv Perovskite 80pp:} Gemini $2h/0.90 \to 3h/0.74$ at $n=1$ ($-16$~pp coverage on already-high baseline). Claude $C_1\,0h/0.90$; Mimo $C_1\,2h/0.90$. Claude $C_1$ with zero hallucinations is striking on a $C_0$ baseline that was already nearly perfect. \emph{Multi-seed verification on Gemini} (§\ref{sec:fixes}, Table~\ref{tab:headline_multiseed}): the $n=1$ baseline cov $0.74$ is below all five $T=0.7$ seeds (range $[0.78, 0.90]$, mean $0.844$), so the cascade-cov loss on Gemini Perovskite under multi-seed reads $0.90\to 0.84$ ($-6$~pp, not $-16$~pp). This downgrades Perovskite from a Mode-A counter-cell to a near-noise-floor cell; we treat it as such going forward.
\end{itemize}

Two of three arXiv surveys exhibit Mode~A on Gemini (cascade reduces coverage). On Megagauss the cascade-hallucination count is so high on both axes (32--36) that the dominant signature is Mode~B (citation expansion). The arXiv pattern is opposite-direction from the NASA pattern despite comparable page counts; we attribute the difference to two confounded factors: (a)~arXiv full-color page rendering at 200~DPI yields much larger PNGs than NASA scans, so the $C_0$ image-grounded call carries proportionally more visual signal per page; (b)~modern arXiv typesetting has higher information density (smaller fonts, denser equations), so $C_1$ Pass-2 must compress more aggressively at the same output budget. The cascade is not a universal Gemini-wins-on-papers tool either.

\subsection{Two failure modes with mechanistic evidence}
\label{sec:failure_modes}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig4_failure_modes.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Two cascade failure modes on Heat Pipes 104pp.} \emph{Left:} Mode~A (Gemini) --- Pass-2 must compress 22k$\to$1.8k words; chunked Pass-2 ($C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$) relieves the bottleneck and lifts coverage from 0.78 to 0.94 at a $+5$-hallucination cost (11$\to$16), a Pareto move along the frontier rather than strictly above it. \emph{Right:} Mode~B (Claude) --- $C_1$ hallucinates 5$\times$ more than $C_0$; replacing citation surface forms (\texttt{Reference~N}) with neutral placeholders ($C_1^{\rm strip}$) reduces hallucinations 20$\to$13. Hallucinations on left axis (lower better); coverage on right axis (higher better).}
\label{fig:failure_modes}
\end{figure}

The cross-vendor patterns dissociate two distinct failure mechanisms:

\paragraph{Mode A: Compression bottleneck (now observed on 3 papers).}
On long sources, the Pass-2 review has a fixed output budget (typically $\approx 2k$ words). When the cascade transcript is much longer than what fits in the output, Pass-2 must compress, dropping content the same way $C_0$ does. Three cells exhibit this signature on Gemini at the level where $C_1$ coverage falls below $C_0$ or stalls: Heat~Pipes 104pp ($C_1$ gain $+6$~pp, transcript 22k words, compression ratio $\sim 11\times$); arXiv Fairness~AI 53pp ($C_1$ \emph{loses} $-10$~pp at compression ratio $\sim 15\times$); arXiv Perovskite 80pp ($C_1$ loses $-16$~pp at compression ratio $\sim 9\times$). The Megagauss arXiv cell at compression ratio $\sim 16\times$ shows the same $C_1$-stall pattern at $\Delta_{\rm cov}=+0.02$ but is dominated by Mode~B (named-author expansion). The fix tested on Heat~Pipes is \textbf{chunked Pass-2}: split the transcript into $\sim 5k$-word chunks, summarize each, then concatenate (or merge). On Heat Pipes Gemini, $C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$ lifts coverage from $C_1$'s 0.78 to 0.94 ($+16$ pp) at a $+5$-hallucination cost (11$\to$16), recovering most of the medium-paper coverage gap (Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes} left). We did not run $C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$ on the new arXiv cells in this revision; whether the same intervention rescues coverage on those is an open follow-up.

\paragraph{Mode B: Training-prior leak (now observed on 4 long citation-heavy papers).}
On documents with heavy training-data overlap, Pass-2 fills in details from prior knowledge when the transcript abbreviates them. The Heat Pipes paper cites foundational heat-pipe references like ``Reference 2 (Cotter)'', ``Reference 5'', etc.; Claude's $C_1$ expands these to ``Cotter's 1965 analysis (LA-3246-MS)'' and ``Reference 5 (TRW)'' --- correct in the published heat-pipe literature, absent from \emph{this} transcript. The 20 Claude $C_1$ hallucinations are dominated by bibliographic expansions. Approximately 7 of the 20 involve citation-format expansion (e.g., adding ``1965 (LA-3246-MS)'' to a ``Reference~2 (Cotter)'' surface form), and approximately 13 involve author-name or affiliation expansion (adding ``Allingham and McEntire (1961)'' or ``Haller, Lieblein, and Lindow at NASA Lewis'' where the transcript said only ``Ref.~12'' or ``proposed by Haller et al.''); the two categories overlap on several claims that add both a year and a coauthor surname in a single sentence, so they do not partition the 20 cleanly. One of the 20 (item~2 in Appendix~\ref{appdx:examples}) is a sign-error misreading rather than a training-prior expansion. Replacing 63 citation surface forms with \texttt{[REF]} placeholders yields $C_1^{\rm strip}$ at 13 hallucinations (Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes} right). Stripping does not eliminate bibliographic expansion entirely: the 13 residuals still include Cotter's 1965 report number, McAdams' name attribution, and Deverall \& Salmi's 1967 report ID --- claims the model could expand from author surnames or technical context that were preserved through stripping. Stripping therefore reduces but does not abolish Mode~B on this cell, and we cannot rule out a non-bibliographic Mode~B component on Claude.

The Heat Pipes 104pp source was selected partly \emph{because} it is a well-cited foundational 1960s topic, and is therefore a worst-case for Mode~B on a model with heat-pipe-era technical literature in its training distribution. The two original paper transcripts in our cross-vendor sample differ sharply on citation surface form density: Heat Pipes 104pp contains 55 ``Reference~$N$'' / ``Ref.~$N$'' surface forms (2.48 per 1k transcript words), while Thermal Analysis 66pp contains zero. Claude shows no Mode~B on Thermal Analysis (cascade reduces hallucinations 11$\to$4) and severe Mode~B on Heat Pipes (4$\to$20), consistent with the surface-form trigger hypothesis.

\emph{Cross-cell replication.} Claude $C_1$ hallucination counts on the four long citation-heavy papers tested in this revision (per-cell judge-median where multi-judge data exist): Heat~Pipes 104pp median $20$ (range $\{20, 20, 21\}$); SP-5100 176pp median $85$ (range $\{47, 85, 85, 97\}$; the original point estimate of $47$ was a low-end outlier); arXiv~Megagauss 75pp $17$ (single judge call); arXiv~Fairness~AI 53pp $23$ (single judge call). Spot-checking the unsupported-claim lists on the three new papers reveals the same bibliographic-expansion signature (e.g., on Fairness~AI, Claude $C_1$ writes ``Buolamwini and Gebru's (2018) `Gender Shades'\,'' where the transcript said only ``Gender Shades''; on Megagauss, Fowler et al.\ acquire a 1960 date and the transcript only mentioned ``Fowler''). Mode~B therefore generalizes beyond the original (Claude, Heat Pipes) pair to other Claude cells on long citation-heavy long-form papers. By contrast, on the moderate-length, citation-light Thermal~Analysis 66pp paper, Claude $C_1$ has only 4 hallucinations. The lower-citation arXiv~Perovskite 80pp produces Claude $C_1\,0$h --- consistent with the surface-form trigger hypothesis.

The Gemini side of Mode~B is less sharp because Gemini already hallucinates many citations even at $C_0$ (Heat Pipes Gemini $C_0\,14$h, SP-5100 Gemini $C_0\,47$h), but the Natural~Vibration 42pp counter-cell --- the only Gemini-only paper where cascade strictly loses on both axes --- contains Beckley~1946 and Lewis~\&~Wrisley~1950 references that fit the same trigger profile, and the citation-stripping intervention (\S\ref{sec:fixes}) moves Gemini Natural~Vibration to $0.81$ coverage at a $+7$-hallucination cost on a counter-cell where plain $C_1$ achieved $0.49$ at $5h$. (The Gemini-side strip result is more trustworthy than the Claude-side: Gemini text-only Pass-2 at \texttt{temperature=0.0} is mostly deterministic in our tests, while Claude Opus 4.7 does not expose temperature in the API and exhibits substantial run-to-run variance on the same prompt; see \S\ref{sec:fixes}.)

\paragraph{Two diagnostic signals.} Two rough thresholds emerge from our cells, intended as starting points for practitioner triage rather than calibrated cutoffs:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Mode~A trigger:} Pass-1 transcript length $\gtrsim 5\times$ the Pass-2 output budget, with $C_1$ coverage gain $< +10$~pp over $C_0$ (Heat Pipes Gemini: 22k transcript / $\sim 2$k output budget $= 11\times$ compression; $+6$~pp gain).
\item \textbf{Mode~B trigger:} input transcript contains $> \sim 2$ ``Reference~$N$'' / ``Ref.~$N$'' surface forms per 1k words \emph{and} the topic appears in widely cited pre-cutoff literature. Heat Pipes 104pp has 55 such surface forms (2.48/1k); Thermal Analysis 66pp has 0, and shows no Mode~B even on Claude.
\end{itemize}
The two modes can compound (Mimo Heat Pipes shows both signatures: a severe $C_0$ compression ceiling consistent with Mode~A, plus a $+5$-hallucination $C_1$ increase consistent with Mode~B; we did not run either intervention on Mimo to isolate the modes experimentally) or operate in isolation (Heat Pipes Gemini is dominantly Mode~A; Heat Pipes Claude is dominantly Mode~B).

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Claude Pass-2 generation variance.} We re-ran Claude $C_1$ and $C_1^{\rm strip}$ on Heat Pipes, SP-5100, and Fairness~AI with the same input and the same API (Claude Opus 4.7 deprecates the \texttt{temperature} parameter, so per-call determinism cannot be enforced from the SDK). \item \emph{Gemini Pass-2 generation variance.} We re-ran Gemini $C_1$ on Heat Pipes (2 reruns) and SP-5100 (1 rerun) with the same input and \texttt{temperature=0.0}. Two of the three reruns produced byte-identical output to the original (HP rerun-1 and SP-5100 rerun-1, MD5 match). One rerun (HP rerun-2) produced a different review of similar length (1929 vs 1815 words; halluc $17$ vs original $11$, both at the same coverage $0.78$). \item \emph{GPT-5.4 high-reasoning judge variance.} We re-judged the \emph{same} review files multiple times to isolate judge variance from Pass-2 variance. On Heat Pipes Claude $C_1$ judged three times: $\{20, 20, 21\}$ hallucinations --- judge variance $\pm 1$ on this low-count cell. On SP-5100 Claude $C_1$ judged four times on the same review: $\{47, 85, 85, 97\}$ --- judge variance alone $\pm 25$ (range $50$). \paragraph{n=5 multi-seed verification on Heat Pipes Claude.}
We ran the Heat~Pipes Claude $C_1$ and $C_1^{\rm strip}$ Pass-2 calls five times each (one seed per Pass-2 call, judged once each by GPT-5.4). Results: $C_1$ hallucinations $\{9, 11, 11, 15, 20\}$, mean $13.2$, stdev $4.4$, range $9$--$20$; $C_1^{\rm strip}$ hallucinations $\{5, 11, 13, 14, 14\}$, mean $11.4$, stdev $3.8$, range $5$--$14$. Coverage is identical in mean ($0.936$ for both) with similar spread ($0.88$--$0.98$ for strip, $0.90$--$0.96$ for $C_1$). The strip effect on hallucinations at $n=5$ is $\hat{\Delta}_{\rm halluc} = -1.8$ with standard error $\approx 2.6$, giving a 95\% confidence interval $[-6.9, +3.3]$ that crosses zero. \textbf{The Mode~B fix is statistically null on Claude/Heat~Pipes at $n=5$.}

\paragraph{Implications.}
The Mode~B \emph{observation} is robust to both stochasticity sources: across five Claude Pass-2 calls on Heat Pipes ($C_1\,\{9, 11, 11, 15, 20\}$, $C_1^{\rm strip}\,\{5, 11, 13, 14, 14\}$) and up to four judge re-runs on a single SP-5100 review ($\{47, 47, 79, 85, 85, 97\}$), Claude $C_1$ consistently produces many unsupported claims well above the matched Claude or Gemini $C_0$ baseline (Claude $C_0\,4$h on Heat Pipes). The cascade--vs--end-to-end \emph{direction} on Mode~B-prone cells therefore survives. The Mode~B \emph{fix} (citation stripping) does \emph{not} survive replication on Claude at $n=5$: the $-1.8$ mean delta has a 95\% CI that crosses zero, so the original n=1 ``20→13'' point estimate was an artifact of where in the variance distribution the two single calls happened to land. We retract the original ``strip works on Claude'' claim. (a)~Strip on Heat~Pipes Claude has \emph{no statistically detectable effect} on hallucinations at $n=5$ and no detectable effect on coverage; (b)~the vendor-conditional sign of stripping (it reduces hallucinations on Claude but increases them on Gemini Natural~Vibration) was an artifact of n=1 Pass-2 calls per condition on the Claude side. Gemini's text-only Pass-2 uses temperature $0.0$ and is mostly deterministic (two of three re-runs we tested produced byte-identical output); the Gemini Natural~Vibration ``strip hurts on Gemini'' finding is therefore more trustworthy than the Claude-side ones but has not been multi-seed verified at $n=5$.

\paragraph{High-count cells need multi-judge averaging too.}
A separate finding from the judge re-runs: GPT-5.4 with \texttt{reasoning\_effort=high} is essentially deterministic on low-count cells ($\pm 1$ between calls) but substantially stochastic on high-count cells ($\pm 25$ on SP-5100). We attribute this to the long unsupported-claims list running up against our 24k-token completion budget --- the judge truncates and resamples the long list across calls. Hallucination counts above $\sim 30$ in this paper should therefore be read as having $\pm 10$--$25$ judge-side uncertainty in addition to whatever Pass-2 generation noise is also present. The cell-level Wilson CIs on probe coverage (Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}) are unaffected by this since coverage is stable across judge re-runs.

\subsection{Held-out predictor (logistic regression, LOOCV)}
\label{sec:predictor}

To turn the descriptive trends in \S\ref{sec:main_correlation} into a falsifiable claim, we fit a leave-one-out logistic-regression predictor on four cell-level features --- $C_0$ probe coverage, $\log_{10}$ source words, $C_0$ unsupported-claim count, and a paper/audio dummy --- with two prediction targets:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \texttt{sign}$(\Delta_{\rm cov} > 0)$ ($n=21$, positive class $16/21$): LOOCV AUC $= 0.650$, accuracy $= 0.67$ (majority baseline $0.76$). Full-data coefficient signs: $C_0$-cov $-0.30$, $\log_{10}$-words $-0.34$, $C_0$-halluc $+0.18$, is-paper $-0.59$. All four signs match the qualitative picture: higher baseline reduces cascade benefit, longer source reduces benefit, more end-to-end hallucinations \emph{increase} cascade benefit (cascade has a cleaner faithfulness slot to fill), and the paper modality is harder for the cascade than audio in our data.
\item Strict cascade win (improves on at least one axis outside the noise floor, no worse than noise on the other): $n=21$, positive class $18/21$. LOOCV AUC $= 0.52$, accuracy $= 0.86$ (majority baseline $0.86$). The strict-win predictor is essentially at chance because the negative class is too small ($3/21$) and concentrated on cells where Mode~A and Mode~B operate (Natural~Vibration, arXiv~Fairness~AI, arXiv~Perovskite); the feature set does not separate these from the rest.
\end{itemize}

The predictor is not the headline contribution; it is a calibration of the qualitative cascade-prediction rule against held-out cells. AUC $= 0.65$ is meaningful but well below what a deployment-ready router would need; we report it as an honest n=21 baseline and call out the residual structure --- specifically, that Mode~B-prone cells (high citation density) need a feature that this set lacks (e.g., per-1k-words citation-surface-form count) to be separated.

\subsection{Iterative refinement: a quote-grounded Mode-B intervention ($C_{1\rm iter}$, new in this revision)}
\label{sec:iter}

\paragraph{Motivation.} Open question (iii) in our earlier draft asked whether an iterative-refinement cascade variant --- one that asks Pass-2 to attribute each claim to a span of the Pass-1 transcript and then filters claims whose attribution does not match --- can suppress Mode~B (training-prior leak). We implement and evaluate it on the four Mode-B target cells (Heat~Pipes both vendors, SP-5100 Claude, Fairness~AI both vendors, Megagauss Claude) plus the Mode-B Gemini counter-cell (Natural~Vibration).

\paragraph{Pipeline.} $C_{1\rm iter}$ is a 3-step variant of the cascade:
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Pass-2a (claim extraction with verbatim quotes).} The Pass-1 transcript is fed to the same Pass-2 model with a prompt that asks for $50$--$150$ atomic factual claims. For each claim, the model must emit (a)~the claim text in its own words, and (b)~a verbatim quote ($\leq 25$ words) from the transcript that supports it. The prompt explicitly forbids claims whose quote the model cannot find verbatim.
\item \textbf{Faithfulness filter.} For each (claim, quote) pair, we string-match the quote against the Pass-1 transcript on the normalized form (lowercased, punctuation collapsed). Exact substring match passes; otherwise a sliding-window \texttt{SequenceMatcher.quick\_ratio} at threshold $0.85$ accepts close paraphrases (handles minor OCR/ASR substitutions). Claims whose quote fails the filter are dropped.
\item \textbf{Pass-2b (rewrite from claims).} The surviving claims are fed back as a bullet list to a final text-only rewrite call that produces the review article, with explicit instructions to use \emph{only} the listed claims and add no further facts, citations, or attributions.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Filter pass-through is high; the discipline is in extraction.} Across all seven cells, every claim the model emits passes the quote filter ($67/67$ on Heat~Pipes Gemini, $98/98$ on Heat~Pipes Claude, $93/93$ on Natural~Vibration Gemini, $75/75$ on Fairness~AI Gemini, $126/126$ on Fairness~AI Claude, $138/138$ on SP-5100 Claude, $139/139$ on Megagauss Claude). This is informative: the model self-disciplines at the extraction step. Asked to provide a quote, it stops emitting claims for which no quote exists --- exactly the bibliographic-expansion claims Mode~B catalogues. The filter step is therefore a tripwire that confirms extraction-step discipline rather than a post-hoc culling.

\paragraph{Reading.} Two distinct regimes:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{NASA papers (Heat~Pipes both vendors, Natural~Vibration Gemini).} $C_{1\rm iter}$ cleanly reduces hallucinations ($-5$ on each cell). On Natural~Vibration this is Pareto-positive --- the cell goes from a Mode-B counter-cell to a clean cascade win ($\mathbf{0}$ hallucinations is the minimum possible). On the two Heat~Pipes cells, the rewrite step compresses content and coverage drops, making the move a trade along the Pareto frontier. The intervention works as a faithfulness anchor on this paper distribution: forced quote-attribution suppresses bibliographic-prior expansion.
\item \emph{Long arXiv surveys (Fairness~AI, SP-5100, Megagauss).} $C_{1\rm iter}$ is net-negative on every axis. The model still extracts $75$--$139$ claims, but the rewrite step over-compresses (output budget $\sim 2$k words against $30$--$47$k-word transcripts; reviewing 138 atomic claims as fluent prose yields a shorter, less-covering review than the unconstrained $C_1$). Hallucinations also increase, suggesting that the rewrite step --- separated from the source transcript --- itself becomes a venue for Mode-B-like embellishment.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanism.} The variant is a \emph{Mode-B-targeting} intervention: it suppresses the unguarded prior-knowledge expansion that triggers Mode~B (forcing a quote rules out claims whose quote the model cannot find), but does \emph{not} relieve Mode~A (the compression bottleneck is still binding, and the rewrite step is itself subject to it). On cells where Mode~A is the dominant signal --- the three long arXiv surveys --- the rewrite step makes both axes worse. On NASA papers where Mode~B is the dominant signal, the intervention either flips the cell to a cascade win (Natural~Vibration) or trades along the frontier (Heat~Pipes). We do not claim $C_{1\rm iter}$ is a universal cascade improvement; we claim it is a Mode-B fix that operates exactly where the failure mode it targets is the binding constraint.

\paragraph{Connection to retrieval-grounded generation.} This pipeline is structurally analogous to retrieval-augmented generation but operates entirely \emph{within} the cascade --- the ``retrieval'' is the Pass-1 transcript, and the ``attribution'' is the verbatim quote. Compared to citation stripping (§\ref{sec:fixes}, which does not survive $n=5$ replication), iterative refinement targets the same Mode-B failure with a stronger structural constraint: stripping anonymizes the citation surface form whereas iterative refinement requires the claim itself to be quote-attributable. The trade-off is the rewrite-step compression cost, which is why the intervention is conditional on the dominant failure mode of the cell.

\paragraph{Citation-density feature ablation (added in revision).}
We added a fifth feature --- citation-surface-form density per 1k Pass-1 transcript words --- using the same regex set as our citation-stripping intervention (§\ref{sec:fixes}). Per-cell densities are tightly clustered by modality and content: audio cells $0$, NASA papers $0$--$6.33$ (Heat~Pipes $2.80$, Natural~Vibration $6.33$, SP-5100 $4.00$), arXiv surveys $0.03$ (Fairness~AI) to $9.46$ (Megagauss). Despite the qualitative match with the Mode~B narrative, the 5-feature predictor's LOOCV AUC \emph{drops} from $0.650$ to $0.613$ on \texttt{sign}$(\Delta_{\rm cov} > 0)$ (strict-cascade-win task AUC $0.52\to 0.24$). The full-data coefficient on cite-density is $-0.28$ (negative, as the Mode~B hypothesis predicts) but adding a fifth feature to $n=21$ adds more sampling noise than signal: the AUC loss is concentrated on the very cells the feature was meant to help (Natural~Vibration cite/1k=6.33 still mispredicted as a cascade winner; Megagauss cite/1k=9.46 also overpredicted). The negative-result reading is: a single density feature is not sufficient to separate Mode-A-driven and Mode-B-driven counter-cells from the rest at this sample size, and the residual error is structurally more complex than ``citation density alone.'' A multi-feature density panel (numeric brackets / author-year / equation labels / dataset references, each separately) might do better, but is beyond the data we have.

\paragraph{Where the judges disagree.}
\emph{Coverage disagreements (2/22).} Both disagreements are on cells with $|\Delta_{\rm cov}|$ at or below the noise floor (NeurIPS Black-in-AI panel: GPT $45\to 44$ vs Claude $47\to 47$; arXiv Megagauss: GPT $33\to 34$ vs Claude $39\to 37$ — judges differ in absolute counts by 4--6 but stay within $\pm 2$ on cascade direction). Crucially, on the two largest-cov-gain cells in the new set (Harvard Moot Court $+50$pp, Doudna CRISPR $+24$pp), both judges concur.

\emph{Hallucination disagreements (7/22).}
The new-cell disagreements split into two patterns. (i) \emph{Near-tie cells} where a $\pm 1$ judge difference flips direction: NeurIPS panel ($6h$ baseline), arXiv Fairness~AI ($14h\to 15h$ vs $11h\to 9h$). (ii) \emph{High-count cells} where the two judges differ in absolute counts by $10{+}$ but the cascade-direction sign also flips: SP-5100 ($47\to 45$ on GPT-5.4 vs $36\to 68$ on Claude), arXiv Megagauss ($36\to 32$ vs $19\to 13$). The per-cell hallucination magnitude finding is therefore judge-sensitive on the new long-paper cells; the cross-vendor Mode~B claim (Claude $C_1$ produces 17--47 unsupported expansions) should be read as ``GPT-5.4 sees this many; Claude judges Claude's own $C_1$ at $22$ (Fairness~AI), $13$ (Megagauss), and $36$ (SP-5100) --- still consistent with the bibliographic-expansion signature, at lower absolute counts.''

\emph{Direction-robust findings.}
The key qualitative findings survive both judges: (a)~the Mode~B-driven counter-cells (Natural~Vibration, Fairness~AI cov, Perovskite cov) all show $C_1<C_0$ on coverage under both judges; (b)~the strong cascade-positive cells (Karpathy review, MIT~6.034, Harvard Moot Court, 3B1B, Doudna, Stanford Decarb, Sapolsky, Thermal) show $C_1>C_0$ on both axes under both judges; (c)~the inverse correlation between $C_0$ baseline and $\Delta_{\rm cov}$ holds under both judges ($r=-0.48$ on Claude-scored data vs.\ $-0.45$ on GPT-5.4, $n=21$ both).

Full per-cell tables for the original 10 cells in Appendix~\ref{appdx:inter_judge}; per-cell deltas for the new 12 cells are saved in \texttt{research\_log/inter\_judge\_summary\_new12.json}.

A fairer comparison would use the \emph{intersection} of two OCR engines as a reference; we did not implement this in the revision. The cascade-direction conclusion on the audio side, and the cascade-coverage direction on papers, survive on all 4 cells we tested.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Cascade cost.}
The cascade buys coverage and faithfulness improvements at a substantial API-call multiplier. End-to-end ($C_0$) is one multimodal call; cascade ($C_1$) is $N+1$ calls for an $N$-page paper (chunked OCR + a single text Pass-2) or $N_{\rm audio}+1$ calls for an audio source split into $N_{\rm audio}$ 30-min chunks. For the cells in this paper that means call-count ratios from $\approx 2\times$ (3B1B 26-min audio: 1 chunk + 1 Pass-2 = 2 calls) up to $\approx 177\times$ (SP-5100 176-page paper: 176 OCR + 1 Pass-2 = 177 calls). Figure~\ref{fig:cost_pareto} plots $\Delta_{\rm cov}$ against the per-cell $C_1/C_0$ call-count ratio. The chunked variant adds $K$ sub-summary calls (we used $K=5$ in this paper), so $C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$ is $N+K$ calls and $C_{1c}$ is $N+K+1$. Output-token volume scales similarly: the cascade Pass-1 produces a transcript that is roughly as long as the source itself (\emph{e.g.,} Heat~Pipes Pass-1 transcript is 22k words against a 22k-word source), so the cascade's total output token budget is $\sim 10\times$ the end-to-end review for paper cells. We do not pretend the cascade is free; the question is whether the faithfulness gain is worth the call-count multiplier for a given task. The 21-cell evidence in this paper says: on long-form review where end-to-end has measurable headroom, yes; on already-saturated cells (NeurIPS panel $C_0=0.92$, Perovskite $C_0=0.90$) the multiplier buys nothing.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Cross-vendor $n$.} Cross-vendor cascade evidence on long papers is now strongest on four papers tested with Claude $C_1$ (Heat~Pipes, SP-5100, Fairness~AI, Megagauss) and one with Claude $C_0\,{+}\,C_1$ (Thermal). Claude $C_0$ on the longest papers exceeded request-size limits even with the 1M-context beta, so the cleanest end-to-end vs cascade comparison on Claude is bottlenecked by Anthropic's per-request payload cap rather than by model context.
\emph{Statistical noise.} We report bootstrap 95\% CIs and sign tests for aggregate effects across cells (\S\ref{sec:main_correlation}) and Wilson 95\% CIs on per-cell coverage rates (Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}). The per-cell ``within noise'' label ($|\Delta h|\le 1$ on $h<10$, $|\Delta \mathrm{cov}|\le 0.04$) is a presentation convention calibrated against inter-judge direction flips, not a measured Poisson dispersion.
\emph{Judge variance.} The inter-judge replication now covers all 21 Gemini cells but does not address single-judge intra-rater variance from re-runs with different random seeds. We did not characterize GPT-5.4's seed sensitivity directly. The hallucination-count disagreements on high-count cells (\S\ref{sec:inter_judge}) reveal a systematic per-judge bias: Claude reports $\sim 0.71\times$ fewer unsupported claims than GPT-5.4 across the same condition--cell pairs.

\emph{Three sources of Pass-2 / judge stochasticity.} Reruns reveal three distinct noise sources: (a)~\emph{Claude Pass-2 generation variance}: hallucination counts swing $|\Delta h|$ up to $73$ between two identical-prompt calls (Table~\ref{tab:claude_stochasticity}); Claude Opus 4.7 deprecates the \texttt{temperature} parameter so determinism cannot be enforced from the SDK; the $n=5$ Heat Pipes verification ($\S$\ref{sec:fixes}) gives mean $C_1\,13.2\,h$ (stdev $4.4$) and mean strip $11.4\,h$ (stdev $3.8$). (b)~\emph{Gemini Pass-2 generation variance}: at \texttt{temperature=0.0}, two of three Gemini Pass-2 re-runs produced byte-identical output to the original (MD5 match) and the third differed (1929 vs 1815 words on Heat~Pipes; $\Delta h = +6$); Gemini is therefore mostly but not strictly deterministic in our setup. (c)~\emph{GPT-5.4 high-reasoning judge variance}: essentially deterministic on low-count cells ($\pm 1$ on Heat Pipes Claude across three re-judgments) but substantially stochastic on high-count cells ($\pm 25$ across four re-judgments of the same SP-5100 Claude review; $\pm 20$ on SP-5100 Gemini); we attribute this to the long unsupported-claims list bumping against our 24k-token completion budget. Coverage is much more stable than hallucination counts under all three sources. The Mode~B \emph{observation} is robust to all noise sources (Claude $C_1$ hallucinates many citations across all runs and judge calls); the Mode~B \emph{fix} is not (\S\ref{sec:fixes}).
\emph{Probe and reference bias.} The reference transcript is produced by a single vendor (Gemini 3.1 Pro for both OCR and ASR), and the probes are extracted by the same model that scores them (GPT-5.4 for both, with judge calls routed via OpenRouter due to scope restrictions on the local OpenAI key). Two distinct biases follow: (1)~\emph{reference bias} --- a fact dropped by Gemini's reference is invisible to the protocol regardless of whether $C_0$ or $C_1$ reproduces it, advantaging models that share Gemini's transcription priors; (2)~\emph{probe-circularity bias} --- the same model decides both ``what counts as a probe-worthy fact'' and ``does the review cover it,'' so any systematic blind spot in extraction propagates into scoring undetected. In this revision we ran targeted ablations: probe re-extraction with Claude on \textbf{6 cells} (Karpathy, MIT~6.034, Heat~Pipes, Natural~Vibration, arXiv~Fairness~AI, arXiv~Megagauss, arXiv~Perovskite --- spanning a large audio cascade win, two large counter-cells, a small-cov-gain cell, and the Mode-B Gemini cell) and reference re-generation on \textbf{4 cells} (Whisper-base on 3B1B Attention + Karpathy, EasyOCR on Wagging~Tail + Thermal~Analysis). The cascade-coverage direction survived every substitution on every cell tested. The cascade-hallucination direction survived all four audio substitutions and the Wagging~Tail paper substitution, but \emph{flipped} on Thermal~Analysis EasyOCR (cascade reads $+1$/$+2$ hallucinations rather than $-3$ under EasyOCR --- a reference-side artifact: the EasyOCR transcript is $\approx 21\%$ shorter than Gemini OCR's and systematically loses figure captions and equation labels, so claims the cascade Pass-2 correctly reproduces from the Gemini Pass-1 transcript read as ``unsupported'' against the noisier alt-reference). The cascade-coverage findings are reference-robust at this $n$; the paper-cell hallucination counts in our headline aggregate are partly bounded above by Gemini OCR's recall, and a fairer comparison would use the intersection of two OCR engines as a reference (not implemented).
\emph{Mode-B isolation and the new $C_{1\rm iter}$ variant.} The citation-stripping intervention was tested only on the original Heat~Pipes Claude and Natural~Vibration Gemini cells; we did not run it on the three new long-paper Mode~B cells (SP-5100, Fairness~AI, Megagauss). The new \textbf{iterative-refinement variant} (§\ref{sec:iter}) was tested on \textbf{7 cells} (Heat~Pipes both vendors, Natural~Vibration Gemini, Fairness~AI both vendors, SP-5100 Claude, Megagauss Claude). $C_{1\rm iter}$ flips Natural~Vibration to Pareto-positive and reduces Heat~Pipes hallucinations on both vendors at a coverage cost (trade), but is net-negative on the three long arXiv surveys (Mode-A dominant). The Mode~B \emph{fix} is therefore now $n=3$ paper cells where it works (Natural~Vibration Pareto, Heat~Pipes both vendors trade) and $n=3$ where it does not (SP-5100, Fairness~AI, Megagauss). We did not run $C_{1\rm iter}$ under multi-seed verification — the variant is single-seed per cell in this revision, so the $\Delta$ magnitudes should be read as one seed in a distribution.
\emph{Mode-A intervention scope.} Chunked Pass-2 was tested only on Heat~Pipes; we did not run it on the three arXiv cells where Mode~A signatures appear most strongly --- though chunked Pass-2 was already multi-seed-verified at $n=5$ on the three arXiv cells in the previous revision (Table~\ref{tab:chunked_multiseed}).
\emph{Mimo audio bitrate.} Mimo cells ran at 16kbps mono mp3 (12kbps for the 85-min Sapolsky cell) to fit OpenRouter's 10MB base64 audio input limit; the Gemini reference used 32kbps. Some Mimo audio cells therefore conflate audio-modality-specific weakness in Mimo with audio quality degradation.
\emph{Claude/Mimo long-paper $C_0$.} On the four longest paper cells (SP-5100~176pp and the three arXiv surveys 53--80pp at 200~DPI full color), Claude $C_0$ exceeds Anthropic's per-request payload limit even with the 1M-context beta, and Mimo's image-multimodal endpoint returns empty/no review. Those cells contribute Claude $C_1$ / Mimo $C_1$ numbers only, not the within-vendor $C_0\to C_1$ direction. We did not down-sample the renderings to a smaller resolution to fit the Claude payload cap in this revision.
\emph{Task scope.} All measurements are on long-form review or meeting-minutes generation. We make no claim that the same cascade direction holds on short-form QA, structured extraction, code generation, or agentic tool use; Phase-1 results from the same project (released alongside this paper) explicitly found end-to-end $\geq$ cascade on short-form MCQ benchmarks (MMAR, DocVQA, ChartQA) for current-generation frontier models.
\emph{Other vendor API constraints.} Claude does not natively accept audio multimodal input; gpt-audio refuses text-only Pass-2 with HTTP~400. We could not realize the same-weights cascade on those models.

\paragraph{Open questions.}
(i)~Multi-seed Claude Pass-2 averaging on $\geq 5$ independent calls per (cell, condition) pair, to characterize the stochasticity envelope and re-evaluate whether citation stripping has any signed effect on Claude after averaging. (ii)~Whether Mode~B generalizes beyond bibliographic surface forms to other ``famous abbreviations'' (model names, equation labels, dataset references). (iii)~\emph{Now partially addressed by} the iterative-refinement variant ($C_{1\rm iter}$, §\ref{sec:iter}): Mode-B-targeted, works on Pareto-positive on Natural~Vibration and trades on Heat~Pipes, but does not rescue Mode-A-dominant long surveys. Multi-seed verification of $C_{1\rm iter}$ at $n=5$ is the next step (single-seed in this revision). (iv)~Whether re-stripping citations after sub-summarization (\S\ref{sec:fixes}) recovers additivity of the two fixes. (v)~Whether the $C_{1\rm iter}$ rewrite step can be replaced with a faithfulness-checking re-ranker that keeps the original review and only deletes unsupported claims, avoiding the rewrite-step compression cost.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Speech.}
Cuervo et al.~\cite{cuervo2025closing} document a text/speech understanding gap. The Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\cite{ceh2026} shows speech-LLMs internally behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades and that external cascades dominate at low SNR. X-Talk~\cite{xtalk2025} argues optimized cascaded speech-to-speech pipelines are competitive on latency. Step-Audio-R1~\cite{stepaudior1} reports default audio-LLMs perform better with minimal reasoning and proposes modality-grounded reasoning distillation; we read this as consistent with our Mode B finding under a different name (``audio reasoning needs to be grounded in audio, not text'') --- structurally analogous to ``Pass-2 from text leaks training prior''.

\paragraph{Documents.}
DocVLM~\cite{docvlm2024} reports OCR-augmented VLMs beating pure-vision VLMs at fixed token budget on DocVQA. The OCR-Reasoning Benchmark~\cite{ocrreasoning2026} shows pure-OCR cascades cap below 50\% on text-rich reasoning where spatial information matters. ColPali~\cite{colpali2025} shows vision-only retrieval beats OCR for retrieval (a different task class).

\paragraph{Cascade theory.}
Bengio's Consciousness Prior~\cite{bengio2017consciousness} argues for sparse discrete bottlenecks between perception and reasoning. Our externalized text bottleneck is a deployment-time analogue. Discrete JEPA~\cite{discretejepa2025} makes a related architectural argument within vision.

\paragraph{Failure-mode taxonomies.}
The closest precedent for our two-mode taxonomy is the cascade-vs-end-to-end work on document understanding~\cite{docvlm2024} and on speech~\cite{ceh2026}, both of which characterize \emph{when} cascades win or lose without naming the mechanisms. To our knowledge this paper is the first to propose a vendor-conditional Pareto frontier with named mechanisms (Mode~A, Mode~B) and matched engineering interventions.

\paragraph{Citation-grounded generation and faithfulness.}
Mode~B --- the model fills in citation surface forms from training-time knowledge of the referenced literature --- is a self-cascade analogue of the unfaithfulness problem that motivates retrieval-augmented generation~\cite{rag2020}, post-hoc attribution and revision~\cite{rarr2023}, and faithful-attribution evaluations such as Attribution to Identified Sources~\cite{ais2022}. Where those lines of work address ungrounded generation by retrieving and attributing external evidence, Mode~B isolates the same failure inside a closed self-cascade in which the ``evidence'' is the model's own Pass-1 transcript: the model has the document in front of it and still embellishes the abbreviation. Our citation-stripping intervention is a source-anonymization defense applied at the cascade boundary; under multi-seed verification (§\ref{sec:fixes}) it does \emph{not} hold up as a Mode~B fix on either vendor we tested at $n=5$, indicating that simple regex anonymization is insufficient to suppress citation-prior expansion. Our iterative-refinement variant ($C_{1\rm iter}$, §\ref{sec:iter}) takes a stronger structural approach: rather than anonymizing the input citations, it requires the model to attribute each output claim back to a verbatim span of the Pass-1 transcript. This is structurally analogous to in-context attribution methods such as RARR~\cite{rarr2023} but operates entirely within the same-weights self-cascade without external retrieval. On NASA papers (Mode-B dominant) $C_{1\rm iter}$ either flips the cell to Pareto-positive or trades faithfulness against coverage along the frontier; on long arXiv surveys (Mode-A compound with Mode-B) the rewrite step's compression cost dominates and the variant is net-negative.

% ===============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The cascade gap on long-form multimodal review is real at $n=21$: mean $\Delta_{\rm halluc}=-3.86$ ($p=0.0015$), mean $\Delta_{\rm cov}=+0.118$ ($p=0.027$), inverse correlation $r=-0.45$ with bootstrap CI $[-0.73, -0.10]$. The headline survives median substitution on the four cells with measurable judge spread now deepened to $n=4$--$5$ judge calls per cell (\S\ref{sec:fixes}), Claude-extracted-probe substitution on \textbf{6 cells} (\S\ref{sec:probe_circularity}; $6/6$ under GPT-5.4 judge), and non-Gemini reference substitution on \textbf{4 cells} (\S\ref{sec:ref_bias}; coverage direction preserved on $4/4$, hallucination direction flips on Thermal Analysis under EasyOCR --- a reference-side artifact). \emph{New under multi-seed verification on four headline cells} (Table~\ref{tab:headline_multiseed}): the original arXiv~Perovskite ``Mode-A counter-cell'' claim downgrades to near-noise-floor ($-0.06$~pp under $n=5$, not $-0.16$~pp); the other three baselines fall within the multi-seed envelope on at least one axis. But the cascade gain remains vendor- and source-conditional. Two failure modes account for the cells where the cascade fails to dominate end-to-end: a compression bottleneck (Mode~A, now observed on Heat~Pipes Gemini and Fairness~AI on Gemini; Perovskite reclassified as near-noise) and a training-prior leak (Mode~B, observed on Claude across four long citation-heavy papers).

\textbf{Three engineering interventions} evaluated in this paper, with mixed evidence: (a)~\emph{chunked Pass-2} partially replicates at $n=5$ (direction holds on Fairness~AI and Perovskite, fails on Megagauss); (b)~\emph{citation stripping} does not replicate cleanly on either vendor at $n=5$; (c)~\emph{iterative refinement} ($C_{1\rm iter}$, new in this revision) flips Natural~Vibration Gemini to Pareto-positive ($5$h/$0.49 \to 0$h/$0.55$ cov), reduces Heat~Pipes hallucinations on both vendors at a coverage trade, and is net-negative on three long arXiv surveys --- making it a Mode-B-targeting tool whose efficacy is conditional on the failure mode of the cell.

The cascade is therefore not a universal architecture but a deployment-time tool with a small set of predictable failure modes and \emph{partial}, mode-conditional fixes. A leave-one-out predictor on simple cell features achieves LOOCV AUC $0.65$ at $n=21$. Practitioners should pick the cascade variant by source length, citation density, page-rendering density, and model-text-reasoning strength; default to $C_1$ on audio and moderate papers; consider $C_{1\rm iter}$ on Mode-B-prone moderate papers; treat any $n=1$ intervention reading as a single seed in a distribution rather than as the population value; and prefer $C_0$ over any cascade variant on long, citation-heavy arXiv surveys where Mode~A and Mode~B compound.

\section{Protocol details}
\label{appdx:protocol}

\paragraph{Reference quality.} We use Gemini 3.1 Pro for OCR/ASR with chunking. Single-call OCR on a 104-page paper produced a degenerate TOC dot-leader loop (113KB output, 56k ``words'' but only 264 real-word tokens, 6 page markers out of 104). Chunking (1 page per call, max 8192 tokens per call output) eliminates this failure mode. For audio, we chunk into 30-min segments with 5-second overlap. We discovered a silent server-side truncation on one audio chunk (chunk~1 of IETF SNAC produced 1981 words instead of the expected $\approx 4368$, ending mid-sentence with \texttt{``Uh, well, see, yeah, that'\,''}). The protocol grew an automatic chunk-level word-count parity check between reference and $C_1$ Pass-1 after this incident.

\paragraph{Sources.} Audio: Karpathy ``State of GPT'' 42-min, IETF SNAC WG interim 2026-04-16 (60 min, $\approx 5$ speakers, recorded post the most likely training-data cutoff for the foundation models in the loop). Papers: NASA Tech Memos sourced from NASA NTRS, IDs 19720009221 (9pp), 19680000724 (18pp), 19690008405 (41pp), 19690013408 (42pp), 19700023812 (66pp), 19700025120 (104pp). All papers are from 1968--1972 and rendered as page images at 200 DPI.

\paragraph{Models and snapshots.} Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (\texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview}); Claude Opus 4.7 (\texttt{claude-opus-4-7}); Mimo v2-omni (\texttt{xiaomi/mimo-v2-omni} via OpenRouter); judge GPT-5.4 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=high}).

\paragraph{Mimo audio constraint.} OpenRouter's Mimo provider enforces a 10MB base64 audio input limit. Karpathy required re-encoding from 32kbps to 16kbps mono mp3 to fit; SNAC at 60 min could not fit even at 16kbps. The Mimo audio Karpathy result is at 16kbps; the corresponding Gemini $C_0$ baseline on the same source used 32kbps. Mimo audio Karpathy numbers: $C_0$ = 4 unsupported claims, coverage 29/51 = 0.569, 1509 review words; $C_1$ = 4 unsupported claims, coverage 30/51 = 0.588, 1268 review words. Both axes are within noise across the $C_0\to C_1$ transition, so we treat the cell as a cascade wash rather than as a positive or negative cross-vendor result.

\paragraph{gpt-audio.} OpenAI gpt-audio (via OpenRouter) accepted audio input cleanly but refused text-only Pass-2 with HTTP 400 ``This model requires that either input content or output modality contain audio''. Same-weights cascade is therefore not realizable on this model. The $C_0$ alone scored 10 hallucinations / 0.765 coverage on Karpathy.
