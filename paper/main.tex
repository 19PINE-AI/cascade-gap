\begin{abstract}
The default way to put a multimodal model on a long-form review task is to hand it the raw source --- the audio, the page images --- and let it reason end-to-end. We give counter-intuitive evidence that this is the wrong default. \textbf{Decomposing the task into two same-weights passes --- one that transcribes the source to text, one that writes the review from that transcript alone --- is systematically more faithful and more complete}, even though the second pass sees strictly \emph{less} than the end-to-end pass. We then ask \emph{why}, with a five-experiment mechanistic study, and find the cause is not what one might assume. It is \textbf{not} a perception failure: the model's own Pass-1 transcript covers $99.9\%$ of probes, and $361/362$ ($99.7\%$) of the probes the end-to-end pass drops are present in that transcript --- the model perceives the content, then loses it. It is \textbf{not} that text is an intrinsically better reasoning substrate: on an open VLM, identical propositional content is read equally well from text and from an \emph{image} of that text, for a single fact and for sixteen packed facts alike (a layer-wise logit-lens shows no readout-depth gap). The cause is a \textbf{generation-side bottleneck}: a single pass cannot simultaneously perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review, so it satisfices and drops roughly a third of what it saw. Decomposition helps because it gives perception a dedicated externalization pass and synthesis a dedicated, full-budget pass over complete text --- collapsing the two into one call (transcribe-then-review in a single response) crashes coverage or fails to emit a review at all, because one output budget cannot hold both. We measure the effect on a 21-cell suite on Gemini~3.1~Pro spanning 11 audio sources (26--85~min) and 10 papers (9--176~pp), with cross-vendor extensions to Claude~Opus~4.7 and Mimo~v2-omni, two-judge replication (GPT-5.4 and Claude on all 21 cells), $n{=}5$ multi-seed verification on four headline cells, and probe-circularity and reference-bias ablations. Decomposition reduces hallucinations on 18/21 cells (mean $-3.86$, sign-test $p=0.0015$) and improves coverage on 16/21 (mean $+0.118$, $p=0.027$); the gain scales inversely with the end-to-end baseline ($r=-0.45$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.73,-0.10]$) --- the more the monolithic pass satisfices, the more decomposition recovers. The boundary is equally informative: decomposition stops paying off in two failure modes --- a \emph{compression bottleneck} (Mode~A) when the synthesis pass cannot fit the transcript into its output budget, and a \emph{training-prior leak} (Mode~B) where, with the raw source gone, the synthesis pass confabulates from prior knowledge (replicated on Claude across four citation-heavy papers). A quote-grounded \textbf{iterative-refinement variant} ($C_{1\rm iter}$) re-grounds the synthesis pass and flips the Mode-B Gemini counter-cell from $5$h/$0.49$cov to $\mathbf{0}$h/$0.55$cov. Perceive, externalize, then synthesize --- the win is decomposition, not the modality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multimodal foundation models can read audio and document images directly. The intuitive way to deploy them on a long-form review task is therefore end-to-end: feed the model the raw source and ask for the review in one call. Keeping the original modality in context looks strictly safer --- no transcription step to lose information, the model sees everything. We argue, and measure, that this intuition is backwards for long-form review.

The alternative is a same-weights two-pass decomposition: the model first transcribes the source to text (Pass-1, \emph{perceive and externalize}), then writes the review from that transcript alone (Pass-2, \emph{synthesize}; same weights, raw modality removed from context). This wins --- and the obvious explanation, that text is simply a better substrate for the model's reasoning, turns out to be wrong. We run a five-experiment mechanistic study (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) that isolates the cause:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Not perception.} The model's own Pass-1 transcript covers $99.9\%$ of probes; of the $362$ probes the end-to-end pass drops, $361$ ($99.7\%$) are present in that transcript. The model perceives the content, then loses it downstream.
\item \textbf{Not substrate.} On an open VLM, a layer-wise logit-lens finds identical propositional content is read equally well from text and from an \emph{image} of that text --- one fact or sixteen packed facts, same answer-token log-prob and the same readout depth. There is no intrinsic ``pixels are harder to reason over'' penalty for legible content.
\item \textbf{It is generation under load.} A single pass cannot simultaneously perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review; it satisfices and drops $\sim\!1/3$ of what it saw. Decomposition gives perception a dedicated externalization pass and synthesis a dedicated, \emph{full-budget} pass over complete text. Collapsing the two into one call crashes coverage or fails to emit a review at all (the transcript exhausts the output budget), so the separation --- not the modality --- is the active ingredient.
\end{itemize}
We call the pattern \textbf{perceive--externalize--synthesize}. This account also predicts the two failure modes (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}): a compression bottleneck when synthesis cannot fit the transcript in its output budget (Mode~A), and a training-prior leak when removing the source lets synthesis confabulate (Mode~B) --- the latter a genuine but secondary cost of dropping the modality, not the engine of the win.

This relates to, but is distinct from, prior single-modality work. Cuervo et al.~\cite{cuervo2025closing} and the Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\cite{ceh2026} find speech-LLMs internally behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades, and Step-Audio-R1~\cite{stepaudior1} shows audio LLMs reasoning over a \emph{textual surrogate} of the audio. Our activation-level tests find \emph{no} reasoning-substrate penalty for propositional content in \emph{either} modality (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): a VLM reasons over an image of text as well as over text (E6), and --- once the speech is intelligible --- an omni model reasons over spoken facts as well as written ones (E7), with an apparent audio gap vanishing when low-quality TTS is replaced by a clean neural voice. The Step-Audio-R1 effect is best read as task-payload-specific (its tasks --- music, environmental sound --- have \emph{acoustic} answers that transcription discards), not a contradiction. On documents, DocVLM~\cite{docvlm2024} reports OCR-augmented VLMs beating pure-vision VLMs at fixed budget, while vision-only configurations dominate on GUI tasks~\cite{uground2024,uitars2025}. Few cross-modality same-weights measurements exist, and fewer still isolate \emph{why}; this paper provides both, focused on \textbf{long-form review generation}, the deployment case where end-to-end models are observably weak (they drop content and embellish) and two-pass decomposition is the obvious alternative.

\paragraph{Findings.}
(i) The two-pass decomposition ($C_1$) beats end-to-end ($C_0$): across 21 Gemini cells it reduces hallucinations on 18/21 (mean $-3.86$, $p=0.0015$) and improves coverage on 16/21 (mean $+0.118$, $p=0.027$), despite the synthesis pass seeing strictly less than $C_0$. (ii) The gain scales inversely with the $C_0$ baseline ($r=-0.45$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.73,-0.10]$): the more the monolithic pass satisfices, the more decomposition recovers. (iii) \emph{The mechanism is generation-side, not perception or substrate} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): the model's Pass-1 transcript covers $99.9\%$ of probes and $99.7\%$ of $C_0$-dropped probes appear in it (perception is fine); an open-VLM logit-lens finds no text-vs-image readout gap (substrate is fine); but collapsing the two passes into one call crashes coverage (the output budget cannot hold transcript $+$ review). (iv) The same account predicts the two failure modes --- a compression bottleneck when synthesis cannot fit the transcript in its budget (Mode~A) and a training-prior leak when removing the source lets synthesis confabulate (Mode~B), replicated across four long Claude cells. (v) A quote-grounded iterative-refinement variant ($C_{1\rm iter}$) re-grounds synthesis against Mode~B: it flips the Natural~Vibration Gemini counter-cell to a clean Pareto win but is net-negative where Mode~A binds. (vi) Direction agreement between GPT-5.4 and Claude judges is 19/21 on coverage and 14/21 on hallucinations; per-probe agreement median $0.94$.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~A 21-cell same-weights measurement of end-to-end ($C_0$) vs.\ two-pass decomposition ($C_1$), across two modalities, three vendors, and two judges. (2)~\emph{A five-experiment mechanistic study} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) that adjudicates among perception, substrate, and generation-side accounts --- ruling the first two out and supporting \textbf{perceive--externalize--synthesize}: perception coverage vs.\ surfacing (E3), an open-VLM cross-modal logit-lens under load (E6), an omni-model audio analogue (E7), the single-call collapse test (E2), a both-modalities control (E1), and DPI/native-text controls (E4/E5, reported with their confounds). (3)~A two-mode account of where decomposition breaks down (Mode~A compression, Mode~B training-prior leak) with deployment triggers, derived from the mechanism. (4)~An iterative-refinement variant ($C_{1\rm iter}$) that re-grounds synthesis with verbatim quotes. (5)~Systematic robustness checks ($n{=}5$ multi-seed on four cells, inter-judge on all 21, probe-circularity on 6, reference-bias on 4), with all retract/preserve verdicts reported transparently.

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig0_protocol_schematic.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Reason-over-modality ($C_0$) vs.\ reduce-to-text-then-reason ($C_1$), same weights.} $C_0$ is one multimodal call: the model under test reads the raw audio or page images and writes a comprehensive review --- reasoning directly over the modality. $C_1$ first reduces the source to text (Pass-1 ASR/OCR call, same model weights), then reasons over that transcript alone (Pass-2, text-only; the raw modality is not in the Pass-2 context). The two configurations differ only in the representation the reasoning step operates over.}
\label{fig:protocol_schematic}
\end{figure}

For each source (audio or scanned paper): (1)~A \textbf{reference transcript} is produced by Gemini~3.1~Pro ASR/OCR with chunking (30-min audio chunks; 1-page-per-call OCR; Appendix~\ref{appdx:protocol}). (2)~$C_0$ \emph{reason-over-modality} (end-to-end): the model under test reads the raw modality and writes a review article in one call. (3)~$C_1$ \emph{reduce-to-text-then-reason} (the same-weights cascade): the model under test produces its own transcript (Pass-1, same prompt as the reference), then writes the review from that transcript (Pass-2, text-only). The only thing that changes between $C_0$ and $C_1$ is whether the reasoning step sees pixels/audio or text. Figure~\ref{fig:protocol_schematic} schematizes the two configurations.

\paragraph{Scoring.} GPT-5.4 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=high}) acts as judge. From the reference, it extracts 30--50 atomic factual probes. For each review the judge then lists every claim not supported by the reference (\emph{hallucination test}) and marks each probe as COVERED / MISSING (\emph{probe coverage}). The protocol is sharper than short-form QA: the review must be both exhaustive (high coverage) and faithful (zero hallucination). Because most frontier models fail the zero-hallucination bar, we report counts and rates as operational measures.

\paragraph{Noise floor and statistics.} We treat $|\Delta h|\le 1$ on cells with $h<10$, and $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 0.04$ ($\approx 2$ probes on a 50-probe cell), as within noise (presentation conventions calibrated against inter-judge direction flips, \S\ref{sec:robust}). For aggregate effects we report Wilson 95\% CIs on coverage rates, percentile-bootstrap 95\% CIs ($n_{\rm boot}{=}10000$, seeded) on cross-cell means and Pearson $r$, and sign tests against $\Delta{=}0$.

\paragraph{Cells.} 21 Gemini cells: 11 audio (Karpathy~42m, MIT~6.034~47m, Sapolsky~85m, Stanford~Decarb~72m, Doudna~CRISPR~48m, 3B1B~Attention~26m, Veritasium~Math~32m, NeurIPS~BinAI~panel~31m, Harvard~Moot~Court~82m, IETF~SNAC~60m, plus two meeting-minutes re-prompts of Karpathy and SNAC), and 10 papers spanning 9--176~pp (seven NASA NTRS reports 1968--1972, including Heat~Pipes 104pp and SP-5100 ``Shock and Vibration'' 176pp; three modern post-cutoff arXiv reviews: Fairness~AI 53pp, Megagauss 75pp, Perovskite 80pp). \emph{Cross-vendor extensions:} Claude~Opus~4.7 ($C_0$ on Thermal~Analysis~66pp; $C_1$-only on the four longest papers where Claude's multimodal $C_0$ exceeds its request-size limit even with the 1M-context beta); Mimo~v2-omni (Xiaomi, via OpenRouter, on every audio source and every paper cell where the long-paper $C_0$ does not return empty). Reference and probes are reused from the Gemini run, isolating the question to ``does the reduce-to-text advantage depend on the $C_0/C_1$ model?''. Full per-cell table in Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}.

\paragraph{Notation.} $C_0$: reason-over-modality (end-to-end). $C_1$: reduce-to-text-then-reason (single-pass cascade). Subscript $c$ ($C_{1c}$): chunked Pass-2 (sub-summarize per chunk, then merge). Superscript $\rm concat$: chunked, no merge. Superscript $\rm strip$: citation surface forms replaced by neutral \texttt{[REF]} placeholders before Pass-2. Subscript $\rm iter$: the iterative-refinement variant of \S\ref{sec:iter}.

\section{Main result: two-pass decomposition beats end-to-end}
\label{sec:main}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/fig1_inverse_correlation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Reduce-to-text coverage gain vs.\ $C_0$ baseline across all 21 Gemini cells.} The worse the model reasons over the raw modality (lower $C_0$), the more reducing to text recovers. Circles: audio; squares: paper. OLS line with bootstrap 95\% CI band; Pearson $r=-0.45$ with bootstrap CI $[-0.73,-0.10]$ (fraction $r\geq 0$: $0.9\%$). The five counter-cells (NeurIPS panel, Wagging~Tail, arXiv~Perovskite, Natural~Vibration, arXiv~Fairness~AI) sit at high $C_0$ baseline (no headroom) or trigger Mode~A/B (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).}
\label{fig:inverse}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Aggregate effect at $n=21$.}
The cascade reduces hallucinations on 18/21 cells: mean $\Delta_{\rm halluc}=-3.86$, percentile-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-5.62,-2.33]$, sign-test $p=0.0015$. It improves coverage on 16/21 cells: mean $\Delta_{\rm cov}=+0.118$, 95\% CI $[+0.048,+0.189]$, sign-test $p=0.027$. Both bootstrap intervals exclude zero. Substituting the median across multi-judge data on the four cells with measurable judge spread (\S\ref{sec:robust}; Appendix~\ref{appdx:stoch}) shifts $\Delta_{\rm halluc}$ to $-3.95$ ($[-5.71,-2.38]$); $\Delta_{\rm cov}$ is mechanically identical because coverage is stable under judge re-runs.

\paragraph{Direction of effect by baseline.}
Coverage gain scales inversely with the end-to-end baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:inverse}) --- the central prediction of the reduce-to-text account: $C_0$ measures how well the model already reasons over the raw modality, and the cells where it reasons worst are exactly where moving the reasoning onto text recovers the most. Excluding the two meeting-minutes re-prompts (artificially low absolute coverage because their probes use atomic-fact style while the prompt asks for structured minutes), $r=-0.47$ on $n=19$ with CI $[-0.83,+0.10]$ --- direction survives but the CI just crosses zero on this subset.

\paragraph{Cascade-positive cells.}
The largest single coverage gain is Harvard~Moot~Court audio ($0.36\to 0.86$, $+50$pp; multi-speaker legal argumentation with little $C_0$ headroom). The largest paper-cell gain is Thermal~Analysis~66pp ($0.68\to 0.84$, $+16$pp). Among the new audio sources, 3B1B Attention is the cleanest hallucination win ($11h\to 0h$) and MIT~6.034 the largest combined gain ($17h/0.77\to 4h/0.98$).

\paragraph{Counter-cells.}
The five cells where cascade fails to improve on at least one axis divide into two clusters: \emph{high-baseline / no-headroom} (NeurIPS panel $C_0{=}0.92$; Wagging~Tail~9pp $C_0{=}0.80$; arXiv~Perovskite~80pp $C_0{=}0.90$); and \emph{mode-triggered failures} (Natural~Vibration 42pp $0.66\to 0.49$, $-17$pp, Mode~B citation-prior leak; arXiv~Fairness~AI 53pp $0.50\to 0.40$, $-10$pp, Mode~A compression bottleneck). The Perovskite counter-cell shrinks from $-16$pp at $n{=}1$ to $-6$pp under $n{=}5$ multi-seed verification (Appendix~\ref{appdx:multiseed}), so we treat it as a near-noise-floor cell going forward.

\paragraph{Cross-vendor: 3/3 vendors confirm cascade on a moderate paper.}
On Thermal~Analysis~66pp (the moderate-length cell where all three vendors realize $C_0$ and $C_1$), all three improve under cascade: Gemini $-60\%$ halluc, $+16$pp cov; Claude $-64\%$ halluc, $+8$pp cov (reaching $1.00$ probe-coverage, the dataset's first such cell); Mimo $+1$ halluc (within noise), $+24$pp cov. Gemini and Claude converge to $\approx 1.4$ hallucinations per 1k output words under cascade despite Claude producing reviews $\approx 2\times$ longer (Appendix~\ref{appdx:perword}); end-to-end hallucination rates differ across vendors but cascade brings them to a common rate.

\paragraph{Long-paper divergence.}
On longer cells the three vendors diverge sharply. \emph{Heat~Pipes~104pp:} Gemini cascade is small Pareto-positive ($14$h/$0.72\to 11$h/$0.78$); Claude cascade \emph{regresses} on faithfulness ($4$h/$0.90\to 20$h/$0.90$), leaving $C_0$ on the Pareto frontier; Mimo trades $+5$ halluc for $+38$pp coverage ($6$h/$0.26\to 11$h/$0.64$). \emph{SP-5100~176pp:} Gemini $C_0$ median $46$h/$0.52$cov $\to C_1$ median $45$h/$0.60$cov; Claude $C_1$ median $85$h/$0.81$cov (Claude $C_0$ not realizable; 176 page-images exceed Anthropic's per-request payload limit even with the 1M-context beta). Both Gemini and Claude $C_1$ sit at very high hallucination rates; spot-checking flagged claims shows the same bibliographic-expansion signature as Heat~Pipes (Appendix~\ref{appdx:xvendor}). \emph{Modern arXiv reviews (post-cutoff for Gemini~3.1~Pro):} the cascade direction can \emph{reverse} relative to NASA papers --- Gemini Fairness~AI cov $0.50\to 0.40$, Gemini Perovskite cov $0.90\to 0.74$ at $n{=}1$ ($-6$pp under multi-seed). We attribute the arXiv-vs-NASA inversion primarily to higher typesetting density forcing more aggressive synthesis-pass compression at the same output budget (Mode~A, \S\ref{sec:failure_modes}), with a possible secondary contribution from full-color rendering carrying more per-page signal. The long-paper picture motivates the mechanism study (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) and the two regimes where decomposition stops paying off (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).

\section{Mechanism: perceive, externalize, synthesize}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Why does decomposition win? Three hypotheses are consistent with the headline result but make different predictions. \textbf{H1 (substrate):} the model reasons better over text tokens than over raw audio/image tokens, so moving the work onto text helps. \textbf{H2 (externalization / generation under load):} a single pass cannot simultaneously perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review; decomposition gives perception a dedicated externalization step and synthesis a dedicated, full-budget step over complete text. \textbf{H3 (attention dilution):} raw-modality tokens are numerous and low-density, diluting the reasoning attention. We ran five experiments (E1--E6) to separate them; the verdict is that H2 is the operative mechanism, H1 is \emph{not} supported (and is directly contradicted for documents), and H3 is untestable on Gemini with our tools. All experiments reuse the exact probe/hallucination judge of \S\ref{sec:protocol}; artifacts are released (\S\ref{sec:code}). Several are single-seed on an 8-cell subset and are reported as mechanistic probes, not headline estimates --- \S\ref{sec:mechanism} closes with the statistical caveats and \S\ref{sec:discussion} with the validation plan.

\paragraph{E3 --- the bottleneck is not perception.}
We scored the model's \emph{own} Pass-1 transcript against the same probes used for the reviews (all 21 cells). The transcript covers a mean $99.9\%$ of probes, versus $65.2\%$ for the $C_0$ review and $77.0\%$ for the $C_1$ review (Figure~\ref{fig:mech_e3}). Decisively, of the $362$ probes the end-to-end pass drops across the suite, $361$ --- $99.7\%$ (Wilson 95\% CI $[98.5\%,99.9\%]$) --- are present in the model's own transcript. The model perceives essentially everything it later omits; the content is lost \emph{downstream} of perception. (The transcript ceiling is partly definitional --- probes are extracted from a near-identical reference transcript --- but the load-bearing inference is one-directional: content the $C_0$ review drops is nonetheless capturable-as-text by the same weights.)

\paragraph{E6 --- text is not an intrinsically better reasoning substrate.}
We tested H1 directly at the activation level on an open VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), controlling for prior knowledge with novel synthetic facts about nonsense entities (e.g.\ ``the Zorblatt device weighs 7 kilograms''). Each fact is presented either as digital text or as an \emph{image} of that same text, with a matched single-token question; a layer-wise logit-lens reads off, at every transformer layer, the log-probability and rank of the correct answer token. For an isolated fact the two modalities are indistinguishable: answer accuracy $20/20$ in both conditions, final answer log-prob $-0.11$ (text) vs $-0.05$ (image), and readout depth (first layer the answer is top-1) $24.2$ vs $24.5$ of $28$ layers. Crucially this holds \emph{under load}: packing $K\in\{1,4,8,16\}$ facts into one stimulus and querying one of them, image and text remain at $1.00$ accuracy with a readout-depth gap $\le 0.34$ layers at every $K$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mech_e6}). For legibly rendered text there is simply no cross-modal reasoning penalty --- the strong form of H1 is false for documents. (Caveat: one open 7B VLM, clean rendered text; not frontier models or dense scanned pages --- see validation plan.)

\paragraph{E7 --- no audio substrate gap either, once the speech is intelligible.}
We replicate the E6 logit-lens on an omni model (Qwen2.5-Omni Thinker), presenting each novel fact as text vs as \emph{synthesized speech}. The result is a methodological caution turned finding: with low-quality TTS (espeak; only $71\%$ of clips intelligible to the model's own ASR) audio appeared to have a substrate gap (answer accuracy $0.83$, readout-depth gap $90\%$ CI $[0.64, 1.78]$, \emph{not} equivalent) --- but this tracked \emph{intelligibility}, not modality. With a clean neural voice (\texttt{mms-tts-eng}; $88\%$ ASR-intelligible) the gap vanishes: audio answer accuracy $1.00$ ($\geq$ text $0.96$), readout depth $22.96$ vs.\ text $22.58$, depth-gap $90\%$ CI $[0.18, 0.57]$ --- \textbf{TOST-equivalent within $\pm 1$ layer}. So neither documents (E6) nor audio (E7) carry an intrinsic reasoning-substrate penalty for propositional content; the cascade win is decomposition, not substrate, in both modalities. (CosyVoice2 was our intended voice but its packaged build was unusable in our environment; we used \texttt{mms-tts-eng}, and report the espeak-vs-clean contrast because it is a cautionary result in its own right --- garbled TTS manufactures a spurious substrate gap.)

\paragraph{E2 --- the win requires a separate, full-budget synthesis pass.}
If decomposition merely externalizes content into one context, doing both steps in a single call should suffice. It does not. A single call instructed to first transcribe verbatim and then write the review (modality attached) \emph{collapses}: coverage falls far below $C_1$ on every paper that produced a review (Thermal $0.84\!\to\!0.40$, Fairness~AI $0.40\!\to\!0.24$; Table~\ref{tab:mech_cond}), and on the three longest sources (MIT~audio, Natural~Vibration, Heat~Pipes) the call never emits a review at all --- the transcript consumes the entire output budget, with long documents degenerating into the TOC dot-leader loop the chunked protocol was built to avoid (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). The active ingredient is therefore the \emph{decomposition into two passes with independent output budgets}, not text-in-context per se.

\paragraph{E1 --- removing the modality is a minor faithfulness bonus, not the engine.}
Giving the synthesis pass \emph{both} the transcript and the raw modality ($C_{1+}$) does not break the benefit and sometimes maximizes coverage (3B1B $C_{1+}$ reaches $1.00$, above both $C_0$ and $C_1$; Table~\ref{tab:mech_cond}) --- consistent with E6, pixels are not harmful to content recovery. What their presence does do is re-introduce a few hallucinations relative to pure-text $C_1$ (e.g.\ 3B1B $0\!\to\!5$h, Perovskite $3\!\to\!5$h), which is the same effect Mode~B names. So dropping the modality buys faithfulness at the margin, but the coverage win --- the larger effect --- comes from the pass structure, not from removing pixels.

\paragraph{E4, E5 --- two controls that did not separate the hypotheses (reported for completeness).}
E4 sought to vary vision-token count via DPI on a fixed document to probe H3; but Gemini normalizes images internally, so $96$ and $200$ DPI yield near-identical prompt token counts ($112{,}007$ vs $111{,}855$ on Heat~Pipes) --- the manipulation does not move the independent variable, and H3 is untestable this way on Gemini. E5 fed native digital text (\texttt{pdftotext}) to a single text-only call as a clean H1 test, but on two-column arXiv PDFs \texttt{pdftotext} scrambles reading order, so native-text coverage is confounded downward (Fairness~AI $0.34$, below even $C_0$). Neither supports H1; we report both with their confounds rather than omit them.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
The evidence converges on \textbf{perceive--externalize--synthesize}. The model perceives the content (E3); pixels-vs-text is not the reasoning bottleneck for legible documents (E6); and the recovery requires decomposing the task into two passes with independent output budgets (E2), not merely removing the modality (E1). The headline inverse correlation ($r{=}-0.45$) follows directly: a low $C_0$ baseline marks cells where the monolithic pass satisfices most, and E3 shows the dropped content is sitting in the transcript waiting to be synthesized. The two failure modes (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}) are the account's two predicted breakdowns: synthesis-budget overflow (Mode~A) and source-removal confabulation (Mode~B).

\paragraph{Statistical treatment and scope.}
E3 is computed over all 21 cells and $362$ dropped probes (CI above). E6 is a controlled within-item comparison ($20$ facts $\times$ $2$ modalities $\times$ $29$ layers, plus a $4{\times}12$-trial load sweep) on one open VLM. E1/E2/E4/E5 are \emph{single-seed} probes on an 8-cell subset: the single-call collapse (E2) is large and consistent (coverage below $C_1$ on $4/5$ cells that emitted a review, plus $3/8$ that emitted none), but the smaller E1 contrasts sit within the per-cell judge/seed noise envelope quantified in \S\ref{sec:robust} and should be read as directional. We therefore treat the mechanism as \emph{strongly supported by E3+E6+E2} and \emph{corroborated} by E1, with E4/E5 inconclusive. A pre-registered multi-seed, multi-model replication is the natural next step (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Where decomposition breaks down: two failure modes}
\label{sec:failure_modes}

The perceive--externalize--synthesize account (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) predicts exactly two ways decomposition can fail, and both appear in the data: when the synthesis pass cannot carry the externalized transcript through its output budget (Mode~A), and when removing the raw source lets the synthesis pass fill gaps from prior knowledge (Mode~B, the secondary cost of dropping the modality). These are the two modes below.


\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig4_failure_modes.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Two cascade failure modes on Heat~Pipes 104pp.} \emph{Left:} Mode~A (Gemini) --- Pass-2 must compress 22k$\to$1.8k words; chunked Pass-2 ($C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$) relieves the bottleneck and lifts coverage 0.78$\to$0.94 at $+5$ halluc cost (a trade along the frontier). \emph{Right:} Mode~B (Claude) --- $C_1$ hallucinates $5\times$ more than $C_0$ (4$\to$20); citation-stripping ($C_1^{\rm strip}$) reduces hallucinations 20$\to$13 at $n{=}1$ (the $n{=}5$ replication retracts the magnitude, \S\ref{sec:failure_modes} and Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}). Halluc on left axis (lower better); coverage on right axis (higher better).}
\label{fig:failure_modes}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Mode~A: compression bottleneck (3 papers).}
On long sources, Pass-2 has a fixed output budget ($\approx 2$k words). When the cascade transcript is much longer than what fits, Pass-2 must compress --- dropping content the same way $C_0$ does. Three Gemini cells exhibit this signature: Heat~Pipes~104pp (Pass-1 transcript 22k words, compression ratio $\sim 11\times$, $C_1$ gain $+6$pp); arXiv~Fairness~AI~53pp ($\sim 15\times$, \emph{loses} $-10$pp); arXiv~Perovskite~80pp ($\sim 9\times$, $-16$pp at $n{=}1$, $-6$pp under multi-seed). The fix tested on Heat~Pipes is \textbf{chunked Pass-2}: split the transcript into $\sim 5$k-word chunks, summarize each, then concatenate (or merge). Heat~Pipes Gemini $C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$ lifts coverage $0.78\to 0.94$ ($+16$pp) at $+5$ halluc (Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}~left). At $n{=}5$ multi-seed verification (Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}, Table~\ref{tab:chunked_multiseed_appdx}) the chunked-Pass-2 direction replicates on Fairness~AI and Perovskite but \emph{not} on Megagauss --- so we report chunked Pass-2 as a $2/3$ replicated Mode-A fix.

\paragraph{Mode~B: training-prior leak (4 papers).}
This is the genuine cost of reduction: removing the raw source removes a grounding anchor, and on documents with heavy training-data overlap Pass-2 fills the gap from prior knowledge when the transcript abbreviates a detail. The Heat~Pipes paper cites foundational references like ``Reference~2 (Cotter)''; Claude's $C_1$ expands these to ``Cotter's 1965 analysis (LA-3246-MS)'' (Appendix~\ref{appdx:examples}). Of 20 Claude $C_1$ hallucinations, $\approx 7$ are citation-format expansions and $\approx 13$ are author-name / affiliation additions (the two categories overlap on several claims); one is a sign-error misreading. Claude $C_1$ hallucination counts on the four long citation-heavy papers (judge-median where multi-judge data exist): Heat~Pipes~104pp $20$, SP-5100~176pp $85$ (range $\{47,85,85,97\}$ on the same review across four judge calls --- the original $47$ was a low-end outlier), arXiv~Fairness~AI $23$, arXiv~Megagauss $17$. Spot-checking the unsupported lists shows the same bibliographic-expansion signature across all four. By contrast, on the moderate citation-light Thermal~66pp paper Claude $C_1$ has only 4 hallucinations.

\paragraph{Two diagnostic signals.} Two thresholds emerge as starting points for practitioner triage:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Mode~A trigger:} Pass-1 transcript length $\gtrsim 5\times$ Pass-2 output budget with $C_1$ coverage gain $<+10$pp (Heat~Pipes Gemini: $11\times$, $+6$pp).
\item \textbf{Mode~B trigger:} input transcript contains $>\sim 2$ ``Reference~$N$'' / ``Ref.~$N$'' surface forms per 1k words \emph{and} the topic appears in widely cited pre-cutoff literature. Heat~Pipes~104pp has 55 such surface forms (2.48/1k); Thermal~66pp has 0 and shows no Mode~B even on Claude.
\end{itemize}
The two modes can compound (Mimo Heat~Pipes shows both) or operate in isolation (Heat~Pipes Gemini is dominantly Mode~A, Heat~Pipes Claude dominantly Mode~B).

\paragraph{Engineering interventions tested.} We evaluated two interventions across cells: \emph{chunked Pass-2} (Mode-A target, $2/3$ replication under $n{=}5$ multi-seed; details Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}) and \emph{citation stripping} ($C_1^{\rm strip}$, Mode-B target). \textbf{Citation stripping does not survive replication.} At $n{=}5$ Pass-2 calls on Heat~Pipes Claude, $C_1$ hallucinations are $\{9,11,11,15,20\}$ and $C_1^{\rm strip}$ are $\{5,11,13,14,14\}$, giving $\hat{\Delta}=-1.8\pm 2.6$ with 95\% CI $[-6.9,+3.3]$ --- the original ``$20\to 13$'' point estimate was within the combined Pass-2 + judge noise envelope on a vendor (Claude Opus 4.7) that deprecates \texttt{temperature} so per-call determinism is not enforceable. \textbf{We retract the citation-stripping claim on Claude}, motivating the stronger structural intervention in \S\ref{sec:iter}.

\section{Iterative refinement: a quote-grounded Mode-B fix ($C_{1\rm iter}$)}
\label{sec:iter}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/fig11_iter_pareto.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Iterative-refinement Pareto frontier across 7 cells.} Each cell's baseline $C_1$ (open circle) is connected by an arrow to its $C_{1\rm iter}$ result (filled star). Blue: NASA papers. Red: modern arXiv surveys. NASA papers move up-left (hallucinations down, coverage either up or flat); arXiv surveys move down-left (both axes worse, because the rewrite step cannot fit the source into the output budget after claim atomization).}
\label{fig:iter_pareto}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Pipeline.} $C_{1\rm iter}$ is a three-step cascade variant:
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Pass-2a (extract).} The Pass-1 transcript is fed to the Pass-2 model with a prompt that asks for $50$--$150$ atomic factual claims, each accompanied by a verbatim quote ($\leq 25$ words) from the transcript. The prompt explicitly forbids claims without a quotable span.
\item \textbf{Faithfulness filter.} Each (claim, quote) pair is string-matched against the Pass-1 transcript on the normalized form (lowercased, punctuation collapsed). Exact substring passes; otherwise a sliding-window \texttt{SequenceMatcher.quick\_ratio} at threshold $0.85$ accepts close paraphrases (handles minor OCR/ASR substitutions). Claims whose quote fails are dropped.
\item \textbf{Pass-2b (rewrite).} The surviving claims are fed back as a bullet list to a final text-only rewrite call that produces the review using \emph{only} the listed claims, with explicit instructions to add no further facts or attributions.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Filter pass-through is high; the discipline is in extraction.} Across all 7 cells we tested ($\{67, 98, 93, 75, 126, 138, 139\}$ extracted claims), \emph{every} claim passes the quote filter. This is informative: asked for a quote, the model self-disciplines and stops emitting claims whose quote does not exist --- exactly the bibliographic expansions Mode~B catalogues. The filter step is a tripwire that confirms extraction-step discipline rather than a post-hoc culler.

\paragraph{Reading.} Two regimes (Table~\ref{tab:iter_results}, Figure~\ref{fig:iter_pareto}):
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{NASA papers.} $C_{1\rm iter}$ cleanly reduces hallucinations ($-5$ on each cell). On Natural~Vibration this is Pareto-positive --- the cell goes from a Mode-B counter-cell to a clean win (zero hallucinations is the minimum possible). On the two Heat~Pipes cells the rewrite step compresses content and coverage drops; the move is a trade along the frontier.
\item \emph{Long arXiv surveys.} $C_{1\rm iter}$ is net-negative on every axis. The model extracts $75$--$139$ claims but the rewrite step over-compresses (output budget $\sim 2$k words against $30$--$47$k-word transcripts). Hallucinations also increase, suggesting the rewrite step --- separated from the source --- itself becomes a venue for Mode-B-like embellishment.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanism.} $C_{1\rm iter}$ is a \emph{Mode-B-targeting} intervention: forcing a quote rules out unsupported expansions, but it does not relieve Mode~A. On cells where Mode~A is the dominant signal (the three arXiv surveys), the rewrite step makes both axes worse. On NASA papers where Mode~B is the dominant signal, the intervention either flips the cell to Pareto-positive (Natural~Vibration) or trades along the frontier (Heat~Pipes). We do not claim $C_{1\rm iter}$ is universal; we claim it suppresses Mode~B exactly where Mode~B is binding.

\paragraph{Connection to retrieval-grounded generation.} The pipeline is structurally analogous to retrieval-augmented generation but operates entirely \emph{within} the cascade: ``retrieval'' is the Pass-1 transcript and ``attribution'' is the verbatim quote. Compared to citation stripping (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}, retracted), $C_{1\rm iter}$ targets the same Mode-B failure with a stronger structural constraint: stripping anonymizes the input citation surface form whereas $C_{1\rm iter}$ requires the output claim to be quote-attributable. The trade-off is the rewrite-step compression cost, which is why efficacy is conditional on the cell's dominant failure mode.

\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robust}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig10_inter_judge_scatter.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Inter-judge agreement across all 22 cell--condition pairs (GPT-5.4 vs Claude as judge).} Dashed: identity ($y=x$); dotted: Claude $\approx 0.71\times$ GPT-5.4 (the median per-pair ratio). Hallucination counts (left) show Claude systematically reporting fewer unsupported claims than GPT-5.4 with most pairs along the $0.71\times$ line; the two outliers (top-left) are the SP-5100 and Megagauss high-count cells. Probe-coverage counts (right) sit nearly on identity --- consistent with per-probe agreement median $0.94$.}
\label{fig:inter_judge_scatter}
\end{figure}

We ran four independent robustness checks; full tables in Appendices \ref{appdx:inter_judge} and \ref{appdx:probe_circ}.

\paragraph{Inter-judge replication on all 21 Gemini cells.}
Claude Opus 4.7 re-scored every $C_0$ and $C_1$ review using GPT-5.4's prompts (Figure~\ref{fig:inter_judge_scatter}). Direction agreement: \textbf{coverage 19/21} ($90\%$), \textbf{hallucination 14/21} ($67\%$). Per-probe agreement: $0.82$--$1.00$ (median $0.94$). Disagreements concentrate on (i)~near-tie cells where a $\pm 1$ judge difference flips direction (NeurIPS panel, Wagging~Tail) and (ii)~high-count cells where the two judges agree ``many claims are unsupported'' but differ in absolute count by $10{+}$ (SP-5100, Megagauss). Claude systematically reports fewer hallucinations than GPT-5.4 (median ratio $0.71$). The Mode~B cross-vendor finding should therefore be read as ``GPT-5.4 sees $17$--$85$ Claude $C_1$ unsupported claims; Claude judges those same Claude reviews at $13$--$36$ --- still consistent with the bibliographic-expansion signature, at lower absolute counts.''

\paragraph{Probe-circularity check (6 cells).}
Probes are originally extracted by GPT-5.4, which also scores them. We re-extracted probes via Claude on six cells spanning the cascade-direction spectrum (largest audio win, near-noise paper, Mode-A and Mode-B counter-cells, high-baseline saturated, low-baseline long-paper) and re-scored the existing $C_0/C_1$ reviews. Under \emph{GPT-5.4 judge}, the cascade $\Delta_{\rm cov}$ sign is preserved on \textbf{6/6} (magnitude shifts $\leq 0.10$ pp on all). Under \emph{Claude judge} the sign flips on $1/6$ (Fairness~AI $-0.10\to +0.04$, within noise). The Mode-A loss on Fairness~AI and Mode-B loss on Natural~Vibration both survive both substitutions.

\paragraph{Reference-bias check (4 cells).}
The Gemini reference advantages models that share its transcription priors. We re-transcribed two audio cells with Whisper-base (3B1B Attention, Karpathy) and re-OCRed two paper cells with EasyOCR (Wagging~Tail, Thermal~Analysis), re-extracted probes via Claude, and re-judged with both judges. Cascade-coverage direction is preserved on $4/4$ under both judges. Cascade-hallucination direction is preserved on the two audio cells (magnitudes shrink: Karpathy $-10\to -4$ on GPT) and on Wagging~Tail, but \emph{flips} on Thermal~Analysis under EasyOCR ($-3\to +2$): EasyOCR's transcript is $\approx 21\%$ shorter than Gemini's and systematically loses figure captions, so claims the cascade correctly reproduces \emph{from the Gemini Pass-1 transcript} read as unsupported against the noisier EasyOCR reference. This is a reference-side artifact, not a cascade-side finding.

\paragraph{Multi-seed Pass-2 envelopes on four headline cells.}
We re-ran Gemini $C_1$ Pass-2 with five seeds at $T{=}0.7$ on MIT~6.034 (largest audio win), Sapolsky (longest audio), Heat~Pipes (long paper key cell), and arXiv~Perovskite (strongest Mode-A counter-cell); Figure~\ref{fig:headline_multiseed} shows the four envelopes alongside published $n{=}1$ points. Three of four published $n{=}1$ baselines fall within the $n{=}5$ envelope on at least one axis; the exception is Perovskite, whose $n{=}1$ coverage $0.74$ is below all five $T{=}0.7$ seeds (range $[0.78, 0.90]$, mean $0.844$). The cascade-cov loss on Perovskite shrinks from $-16$pp at $n{=}1$ to $-6$pp under multi-seed --- below the per-cell noise floor. The three cells reported as wins all preserve their direction (seed-mean cov $\geq 0.90$ on each). Full multi-seed tables in Appendix~\ref{appdx:multiseed}.

\paragraph{Three sources of stochasticity.}
Re-runs separate three independent noise sources. \emph{(a)~Pass-2 generation variance.} Claude Opus 4.7 deprecates \texttt{temperature} so per-call determinism is not SDK-enforceable; Heat~Pipes Claude $C_1$ swings $20\to 9$ across two runs (range~$11$). Gemini at $T{=}0.0$ is mostly but not strictly deterministic --- two of three Pass-2 re-runs on Heat~Pipes / SP-5100 produced byte-identical output (MD5 match); the third differed by $\Delta h = +6$. \emph{(b)~GPT-5.4 judge variance.} On low-count cells the judge is essentially deterministic ($\pm 1$ on Heat~Pipes Claude across three re-judgments of the same review). On high-count cells it is substantially stochastic: SP-5100 Claude $C_1$ judged four times on the same review gives $\{47, 85, 85, 97\}$ (range $50$); SP-5100 Gemini gives $\{25, 45, 59\}$ on the byte-identical review. We attribute this to long unsupported-claim lists running near our 24k-token completion budget --- the judge truncates and resamples across calls. \emph{(c)~Coverage is much more stable than hallucination counts across all three sources} (HP $45\to 45\to 46$ probes covered; SP-5100 Claude $39\to 39\to 39\to 39$). \textbf{Implications:} (i)~headline aggregates use median substitution on the four high-count cells; per-cell hallucination counts $> 30$ should be read with $\pm 10$--$25$ judge-side uncertainty. (ii)~The Mode~B \emph{observation} survives all three noise sources (Claude $C_1$ produces many unsupported claims across every Pass-2 call and every judge re-run); the Mode~B \emph{fix} (citation stripping) does not, which motivated $C_{1\rm iter}$ in \S\ref{sec:iter}. Detailed decomposition in Appendix~\ref{appdx:stoch}.

\paragraph{A simple held-out predictor (LOOCV, $n{=}21$).}
A leave-one-out logistic-regression predictor on four cell-level features --- $C_0$ probe coverage, $\log_{10}$ source words, $C_0$ unsupported-claim count, and a paper/audio dummy --- reaches LOOCV AUC $= 0.65$ on $\text{sign}(\Delta_{\rm cov}>0)$. All four coefficient signs match the qualitative picture (higher baseline reduces cascade benefit, longer source reduces benefit, more $C_0$ hallucinations \emph{increases} benefit, paper modality harder than audio). AUC $0.65$ is meaningful but well below deployment-ready; we report it as an honest $n{=}21$ baseline. Adding a citation-density feature drops AUC to $0.61$ --- the residual Mode-B structure is not captured by a single density feature at this sample size.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Cascade cost.}
$C_0$ is one multimodal call; $C_1$ is $N+1$ calls for an $N$-page paper (chunked OCR + a single text Pass-2) or $N_{\rm audio}+1$ calls for an audio source split into $N_{\rm audio}$ 30-min chunks. Call-count ratios in our cells range from $\approx 2\times$ (3B1B 26-min audio) to $\approx 177\times$ (SP-5100 176pp). Output-token volume scales similarly: the Pass-1 transcript is roughly as long as the source. The 21-cell evidence says cascade pays off on long-form review where end-to-end has measurable headroom, but buys nothing on already-saturated cells (NeurIPS panel $C_0{=}0.92$, Perovskite $C_0{=}0.90$); full cost-vs-gain scatter in Appendix~\ref{appdx:cost}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Cross-vendor $n$.} The cleanest cross-vendor comparison is Thermal~66pp (3/3 vendors). On longer papers Claude $C_0$ exceeds Anthropic's per-request payload cap, so the $C_0$-vs-$C_1$ within-vendor comparison is incomplete on Claude. \emph{Judge variance.} The inter-judge replication does not address single-judge intra-rater variance from re-runs with different random seeds. \emph{Probe and reference bias.} Both are partially controlled (6 cells / 4 cells); the cascade-hallucination direction on paper cells is partly bounded above by Gemini OCR's recall. \emph{$C_{1\rm iter}$ multi-seed.} We did not run $C_{1\rm iter}$ under multi-seed verification; per-cell magnitudes are single-seed. \emph{Mimo audio bitrate.} Mimo audio cells ran at 16kbps mono (12kbps for Sapolsky) to fit OpenRouter's 10MB limit; the Gemini reference used 32kbps, so Mimo cells partly conflate audio quality with model weakness. \emph{Task scope.} All measurements are on long-form review; we make no claim about short-form QA, code, or agentic tasks. \emph{Mechanism $n$.} E1/E2/E4/E5 are single-seed on an 8-cell subset (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}); E6 is one open 7B VLM on clean rendered text. The mechanism rests on E3 (all 21 cells) and E6 plus the large, consistent E2 collapse; the finer contrasts await the replication below.

\paragraph{Validation plan.}
The mechanism finding merits pre-registered, statistically powered replication, which we scope as follows. \emph{(1)~Multi-seed, all conditions.} Run $C_0/C_1/C_{1+}/$single-call at $n{\ge}5$ seeds on all 21 cells with paired per-seed contrasts and Wilson/bootstrap CIs, powering the E1/E2 effects rather than reading point estimates. \emph{(2)~Model generality.} Repeat the E6 logit-lens across an open-model ladder (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B/32B, InternVL, Llama-Vision) and the E7 audio test across omni models and higher-quality voices (e.g.\ CosyVoice2), and push E7 to the multi-fact load regime --- E7 found audio equivalent to text at the single-fact level with clean TTS, and the open question is whether that equivalence holds under load and across voices. \emph{(3)~Realistic-page stress.} Re-test E6 on real scanned pages (dense multi-column, equations, figures) and degraded scans, where a substrate/dilution effect is most likely to appear, since E4's DPI knob is inert on Gemini. \emph{(4)~Task generality.} Extend beyond long-form review to other generation-under-load tasks (long-document QA with many required facts, multi-document synthesis, meeting-minutes) and to a short-form control where the account predicts \emph{no} decomposition benefit (the bottleneck only binds when output demand is high). \emph{(5)~Causal budget manipulation.} Hold the pipeline fixed and vary only the synthesis output budget to confirm Mode~A is budget-bound, and ablate transcript completeness to map the coverage--faithfulness frontier.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Speech and documents.} Prior cascade-vs-end-to-end results are often read as substrate claims; our mechanism study suggests they are at least partly about decomposition and modality-specific recoverability. Cuervo et al.~\cite{cuervo2025closing} document a text/speech understanding gap, and the Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\cite{ceh2026} shows speech-LLMs \emph{internally} behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades; X-Talk~\cite{xtalk2025} finds explicitly cascaded speech-to-speech pipelines competitive. On documents, DocVLM~\cite{docvlm2024} reports OCR-augmented VLMs beating pure-vision VLMs on DocVQA, while the OCR-Reasoning Benchmark~\cite{ocrreasoning2026} shows pure-OCR cascades cap below 50\% where spatial layout carries meaning, ColPali~\cite{colpali2025} shows vision-only retrieval beats OCR, and GUI agents do better from raw pixels than from structured-text intermediates~\cite{uground2024,uitars2025}. The common thread is not ``text is a better substrate'' (our E6 finds no such gap for legible documents) but that decomposition helps when the task is generation-under-load and the content survives transcription, and hurts when the answer lives in layout/signal that transcription discards.

\paragraph{Step-Audio-R1: a contrast, not a corroboration.} Step-Audio-R1~\cite{stepaudior1} is the sharpest statement of the boundary --- and it is worth being precise, because it cuts against a naive reading of our claim. They observe that audio LLMs, when asked to deliberate, spontaneously reason over a \emph{textual surrogate} of the audio (e.g.\ attributing musical sadness to ``lyrics mention sadness'' rather than to a minor key), and they treat this as the \emph{failure} their modality-grounded reasoning distillation must correct. That is consistent with us, not contradictory: their tasks (music, environmental sound, paralinguistics) are precisely the case where the answer is in the acoustic signal, so reducing to text discards it. We study the complementary regime --- long-form review, where the payload is what was \emph{said}. Crucially, our activation tests find no reasoning-substrate gap for propositional content in \emph{either} modality (E6 documents, E7 audio); the Step-Audio-R1 effect is therefore best read as \emph{task-payload}-specific --- their tasks (music, environmental sound, paralinguistics) have answers that live in the acoustic signal, which transcription discards --- rather than as a general claim that audio is a worse reasoning substrate. When the payload is propositional, as in long-form review, reducing to text loses nothing and the win is decomposition. Our Mode~B (the synthesis pass confabulates once the source is removed) is the within-regime echo of their concern.

\paragraph{Cascade theory and faithfulness.} Bengio's Consciousness Prior~\cite{bengio2017consciousness} argues for sparse discrete bottlenecks between perception and reasoning; our externalized text bottleneck is a deployment-time analogue. Discrete JEPA~\cite{discretejepa2025} makes a related architectural argument within vision. Mode~B --- the model embellishes a citation surface form from training-time knowledge --- is a self-cascade analogue of the unfaithfulness problem that motivates RAG~\cite{rag2020}, RARR~\cite{rarr2023}, and Attribution to Identified Sources~\cite{ais2022}. Our $C_{1\rm iter}$ variant adopts the in-context-attribution discipline of RARR but operates entirely within a same-weights self-cascade without external retrieval.

% =================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On long-form review, a same-weights two-pass decomposition beats end-to-end at $n{=}21$ (mean $\Delta_{\rm halluc}=-3.86$, $p{=}0.0015$; mean $\Delta_{\rm cov}=+0.118$, $p{=}0.027$) even though the synthesis pass sees strictly less, and the benefit scales with how badly the monolithic pass satisfices ($r=-0.45$ between gain and baseline). The result survives median substitution on the four high-count judge-spread cells, probe re-extraction on 6 cells, reference re-generation on 4 cells, and multi-seed Pass-2 on four headline cells (with one $n{=}1$ counter-cell, Perovskite, retracted to near-noise-floor). Our mechanistic study locates the cause: not perception ($99.7\%$ of dropped content is in the model's own transcript) and not a text-vs-image substrate gap (an open-VLM logit-lens finds none), but \textbf{generation under load} --- one pass cannot perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review at once, and only a decomposition with independent output budgets recovers the content (collapsing it into one call fails). The boundary is the account's two predicted breakdowns: synthesis-budget overflow (Mode~A) and source-removal confabulation (Mode~B, replicated across four citation-heavy Claude cells), the latter targeted by the quote-grounded $C_{1\rm iter}$ variant. The practical rule: \textbf{perceive, externalize, then synthesize} --- decompose long-form multimodal review into a transcription pass and a full-budget synthesis pass; the win is the decomposition, not the modality. This is a deployment-time pattern with predictable failure modes, not a universal architecture.

\section{Protocol details}
\label{appdx:protocol}

\paragraph{Reference quality.} Gemini~3.1~Pro is used for OCR/ASR with chunking. Single-call OCR on a 104-page paper produced a degenerate TOC dot-leader loop (113KB output, 56k ``words'' but only 264 real-word tokens, 6 page markers out of 104). Chunking (1 page per call, max 8192 output tokens) eliminates this failure mode. For audio we chunk into 30-min segments with 5-second overlap. We discovered a silent server-side truncation on one audio chunk (chunk~1 of IETF SNAC produced 1981 words instead of the expected $\approx 4368$, ending mid-sentence with \texttt{``Uh, well, see, yeah, that'\,''}). \paragraph{Pareto terminology.} We call a cascade condition \emph{strictly Pareto-positive} relative to $C_0$ when it has both fewer hallucinations and higher coverage by amounts exceeding the noise floor; \emph{Pareto-positive within noise} means strictly better on one axis and no worse than noise on the other; \emph{Pareto-dominant} on a set means no other condition has lower hallucination at equal or higher coverage. These are presentation labels, not statistical claims.

\paragraph{Detailed decomposition.}
\emph{(a)~Claude Pass-2 generation variance.} Same input + same API + same prompt: Heat~Pipes Claude $C_1$ swings $20\to 9$ between two runs (strip $13\to 14$ more stable); the deltas are large enough that the strip effect cannot be distinguished from Pass-2 noise. Claude Opus 4.7 deprecates the \texttt{temperature} parameter so determinism cannot be enforced from the SDK. \emph{(b)~Gemini Pass-2 generation variance.} At \texttt{temperature=0.0}, two of three Gemini Pass-2 re-runs produced byte-identical output (MD5 match) on Heat~Pipes / SP-5100; the third differed in similar length (1929 vs 1815 words, $\Delta h = +6$). Gemini is therefore mostly but not strictly deterministic --- consistent with GPU-level non-associativity. \emph{(c)~GPT-5.4 high-reasoning judge variance.} Essentially deterministic on low-count cells ($\pm 1$ on Heat~Pipes Claude across three re-judgments) but substantially stochastic on high-count cells ($\pm 25$ on SP-5100; $\pm 17$ on the byte-identical SP-5100 Gemini review). We attribute this to long unsupported-claim lists running up against our 24k-token completion budget --- the judge truncates and resamples across calls. Coverage is stable on every cell across judge re-runs.

\paragraph{$n{=}5$ multi-seed verification on Heat Pipes Claude.} $C_1$ halluc $\{9,11,11,15,20\}$ (mean $13.2$, stdev $4.4$, range 9--20); $C_1^{\rm strip}$ halluc $\{5,11,13,14,14\}$ (mean $11.4$, stdev $3.8$, range 5--14). Coverage means identical ($0.936$ both) with similar spread. Strip effect on hallucinations at $n{=}5$: $\hat{\Delta}=-1.8$, SE $\approx 2.6$, 95\% CI $[-6.9, +3.3]$ --- crosses zero. \textbf{The Mode~B fix is statistically null on Claude/Heat~Pipes at $n{=}5$.}

\section{Specific hallucination examples (Heat Pipes Claude)}
\label{appdx:examples}

The 20 unsupported claims from Claude $C_1$ on Heat~Pipes 104pp are dominated by training-prior expansions of bibliographic abbreviations:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item ``Cotter's 1965 analysis (LA-3246-MS)'' --- transcript cites only ``Reference 2 (Cotter)''; year and report number are training-data details.
\item ``correlation from Allingham and McEntire (1961)'' --- transcript only says ``Ref.~12''; author names and year are training-data details.
\item ``McAdams' laminar horizontal-tube formula'' --- transcript says ``Reference 13 for laminar condensation on horizontal tubes''; ``McAdams'' is a training-data detail.
\item ``Vapor chamber-fin radiator (Haller, Lieblein, and Lindow at NASA Lewis)'' --- transcript says only ``proposed by Haller, et al of NASA''.
\end{itemize}

One outright misreading: the transcript states the no-boiling condition as $p_v - p_\ell \le 2\sigma\cos\theta/r_c$; Claude's review writes $\ge$. This is a sign error in Pass-2 reading of Pass-1 text, not training-prior leak.
