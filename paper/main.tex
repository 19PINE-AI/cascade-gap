\begin{abstract}
The default way to deploy a multimodal model on long-form review is end-to-end: feed it the raw audio or page images and ask for the review in one call. We show this default quietly \emph{satisfices}: across 21 review cells, the end-to-end pass drops roughly a third of the source content and embellishes what it keeps---despite perceiving it, since $99.7\%$ of the dropped content is captured in the same model's own transcript of the same source. A nine-experiment mechanistic study shows the bottleneck is neither perception nor the reasoning substrate---identical content is read equally well from text and from an \emph{image} of that text---but \textbf{generation under load}: one pass cannot perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review at once. The mechanism dictates the fix: two same-weights passes---transcribe, then review the transcript---each with its own full output budget. This decomposition beats end-to-end on faithfulness on 18/21 cells and coverage on 16/21 (both sign-test significant), the gain scaling inversely with the end-to-end baseline ($r{=}-0.45$): the more the monolithic pass satisfices, the more decomposition recovers. The same headroom law organizes two further vendors and mixed-model pipelines, and predicts both observed failure modes. The win is the decomposition, not the modality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/fig0_protocol_schematic.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The two configurations, same weights.} $C_0$ (end-to-end) reads the raw audio/page images and writes the review in one call. $C_1$ (two-pass decomposition) first transcribes the source (Pass-1, same weights and prompt as the reference pipeline), then writes the review from that transcript alone (Pass-2, text-only); only the reasoning step's input representation differs.}
\label{fig:schematic}
\end{figure}

Multimodal foundation models read audio and document images directly, so the intuitive way to deploy them on a long-form review task---summarize this lecture, review this scanned report---is end-to-end: one call, raw source in, review out (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}, top). Keeping the original modality in context looks strictly safer. No transcription step can lose information, and the model sees everything.

This paper argues, and measures, that the intuition is backwards for long-form review, and explains why. The argument runs in three steps.

\textbf{The observation (\S\ref{sec:observation}).} When a frontier model reviews a long source end-to-end, it quietly satisfices. On our running example---Andrej Karpathy's 42-minute \emph{State of GPT} talk---Gemini~3.1~Pro's end-to-end review covers barely six in ten of the atomic facts an independent judge extracts from the source, and a quarter of its claims are unsupported (Figure~\ref{fig:example}). The failure is not perception: asked to simply \emph{transcribe} the same audio, the same weights produce a transcript containing every single fact the review dropped. Across all 21 cells, $99.7\%$ of the content the end-to-end pass drops is present in the model's own transcript (Figure~\ref{fig:mech_e3}); on the running example the dropped sentences appear there verbatim. The model perceives the content and then loses it downstream.

\textbf{The mechanism (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}).} Where downstream? Three hypotheses fit the observation: text is a better reasoning substrate than raw audio/pixels (H1); a single pass cannot perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review at once (H2, generation under load); attention dilutes over long low-density modality tokens (H3). Nine experiments (E1--E9) separate them. The substrate hypothesis fails everywhere we can test it: identical propositional content is read equally well from text and from an \emph{image} of that text---at the activation level on open VLMs, and behaviorally on the frontier models themselves, where under reasoning load the image is, if anything, \emph{better} (Figure~\ref{fig:substrate}). What does bind is generation: collapsing transcription and review into one call crashes coverage or produces no review at all, while re-attaching the raw modality to a transcript-equipped pass changes little (Figure~\ref{fig:e2_collapse}). The bottleneck is the single pass, not the pixels.

\textbf{The solution (\S\ref{sec:solution}).} The mechanism dictates the fix: give each step a dedicated, full-budget pass. Pass~1 transcribes the source (\emph{perceive and externalize}); Pass~2 writes the review from the transcript alone (\emph{synthesize}), same weights throughout. This two-pass decomposition ($C_1$) beats end-to-end ($C_0$) on faithfulness and coverage across the 21-cell suite (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}), and the gain obeys an \emph{inverse-baseline law}: it scales with how badly the monolithic pass satisfices ($r{=}-0.45$, Figure~\ref{fig:solution}). The same law---not a uniform win---organizes the cross-vendor picture (Figure~\ref{fig:xmodel}) and predicts exactly two failure modes, both observed: synthesis-budget overflow on very long sources (Mode~A) and training-prior confabulation once the grounding source is removed (Mode~B), the latter targeted by a quote-grounded refinement variant (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~A 21-cell same-weights measurement of end-to-end vs.\ two-pass decomposition on long-form review, across two modalities, three vendors, and two judges (\S\ref{sec:observation}, \S\ref{sec:solution}).
(2)~A nine-experiment mechanistic study that adjudicates among perception, substrate, and generation-side accounts, ruling out the first two---including behavioral substrate probes on the frontier models themselves---and supporting \textbf{perceive--externalize--synthesize} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}).
(3)~A two-mode account of where decomposition breaks, with deployment triggers derived from the mechanism, plus two tested interventions and a quote-grounded iterative-refinement variant (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).
(4)~Systematic robustness checks---inter-judge replication on all 21 cells, probe re-extraction, reference regeneration, $n{=}5$ multi-seed verification---with all retract/preserve verdicts reported transparently (\S\ref{sec:robust}).

\section{The observation: end-to-end long-form review satisfices}
\label{sec:observation}

\paragraph{Task and protocol.}
The task is long-form review: given a 26--85-minute recording or a 9--176-page paper ingested as page images, write a review article that is \emph{exhaustive} (covers the source's content) and \emph{faithful} (adds nothing the source does not support). For each source, a reference transcript is produced by Gemini~3.1~Pro ASR/OCR with chunking (30-min audio chunks, 1-page-per-call OCR; Appendix~\ref{appdx:protocol}). GPT-5.4 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=high}) acts as judge: from the reference it extracts ${\sim}50$ atomic factual probes (47--51 across cells), then scores each review for \emph{probe coverage} (each probe COVERED/MISSING) and lists every claim the reference does not support (\emph{hallucination count}). We treat $|\Delta h|\le 1$ on low-count cells and $|\Delta_{\rm cov}|\le 0.04$ as within noise, and report Wilson/bootstrap CIs and sign tests for aggregates (Appendix~\ref{appdx:protocol}). The suite has 21 Gemini cells: 11 audio cells over 10 recordings (talks, lectures, panels, meetings) and 10 papers (seven 1968--1972 NASA reports, three post-cutoff 2026 arXiv reviews); Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table} lists them all. The configuration under study first is $C_0$, \emph{end-to-end}: the model reads the raw modality and writes the review in one call.


\paragraph{The end-to-end pass drops a third of the content\ldots}
Figure~\ref{fig:headline_cells} (gray points) shows the $C_0$ picture across the suite: mean probe coverage is $0.65$, and every cell carries unsupported claims---most frontier reviews fail the zero-hallucination bar. The running example (Figure~\ref{fig:example}) shows what this looks like concretely: invented precision (``10{,}000 tokens''), structural misdescription (RLHF as a fourth stage), and silently dropped content (the talk's closing demonstration).

\paragraph{\ldots that it demonstrably perceived (E3).}
The natural suspicion is perception: perhaps the model cannot hear or read the source well enough. It can. We asked the same model to simply \emph{transcribe} each source (the same chunked ASR/OCR that builds the reference) and scored that transcript against the same probes. Figure~\ref{fig:mech_e3} shows the result: the model's own transcript covers essentially every probe on every cell, sitting far above the review it writes from the same input. Pooled across the suite, $361$ of the $362$ probes the end-to-end pass drops---$99.7\%$---are present in the model's own transcript. On Karpathy it is 19 of 19. The transcript ceiling is partly definitional (probes come from a near-identical reference), but the load-bearing inference is one-directional: the content the end-to-end review drops \emph{is} capturable as text by the same weights. Whatever is lost is lost \emph{downstream} of perception. That is the puzzle the next section resolves.

\section{Mechanism: why one pass fails}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Three hypotheses are consistent with \S\ref{sec:observation} but make different predictions. \textbf{H1 (substrate):} the model reasons better over text tokens than over raw audio/image tokens. \textbf{H2 (generation under load):} a single pass cannot simultaneously perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review, so it satisfices; each step needs a dedicated, full-budget pass. \textbf{H3 (attention dilution):} raw-modality tokens are numerous and low-density, diluting attention. We ran nine experiments (E1--E9) to separate them, reusing the probe/hallucination judge of \S\ref{sec:observation} throughout; all artifacts are released (\S\ref{appdx:code}). The verdict: H1 fails every test we can run, H2 is supported by the two load-bearing contrasts (both multi-seed on all 21 cells), and H3 is untestable on Gemini with our tools (its image pipeline normalizes DPI away, E4). Details and per-experiment tables are in Appendix~\ref{appdx:substrate}.

\paragraph{Not substrate (E6--E9).}
The intuitive explanation---text is intrinsically easier to reason over than pixels or audio---is directly contradicted at two levels (Figure~\ref{fig:substrate}). At the \emph{activation} level, a layer-wise logit-lens on two open VLMs reads novel synthetic facts equally well from text and from an image of that text, for one fact and for up to 24 packed facts (E6); an omni-model analogue finds the same for speech once the TTS is intelligible---an apparent audio gap with robotic TTS vanishes with two independent clean neural voices, so audio-substrate claims must control for intelligibility (E7). \emph{Behaviorally on the headline models}, retrieval from pixels equals retrieval from text at every load up to 48 packed facts, and a degraded-image positive control confirms the probes are sensitive (E8). Forcing multi-hop \emph{reasoning} (E9) sharpens the contradiction: on the one model with headroom, the image is reliably better than text, not worse. The frontier probes are behavioral, not activation-level, and Gemini's reasoning probe sits at ceiling, so Claude supplies the single headroom case; with those caveats, there is no ``pixels are harder to reason over'' penalty for legible content anywhere we can measure. The contrast with \S\ref{sec:observation} is the point: single-fact retrieval from pixels is \emph{perfect} on these same models, yet their end-to-end reviews drop a third of the content. The loss is not in reading the modality.

\label{fig:e2_collapse}

\paragraph{It is generation under load (E2, E1).}
If externalizing content into context were sufficient, one call that first transcribes verbatim and then writes the review---everything in a single context window---should match the two-pass decomposition. It does not (Figure~\ref{fig:e2_collapse}): the single call collapses, either compressing the review far below the two-pass level or exhausting its entire output budget on the transcript and emitting no review at all. The complementary control (E1) shows the flip side: giving the synthesis pass \emph{both} the transcript and the raw modality does not break the benefit---pixels in context are not harmful to coverage, consistent with E6---but their presence re-introduces some hallucinations relative to pure-text synthesis. So the active ingredient is the \emph{decomposition into two passes with independent output budgets}; removing the modality buys faithfulness only at the margin. Two further controls did not separate the hypotheses and are reported with their confounds in Appendix~\ref{appdx:substrate}: DPI cannot move Gemini's vision-token count (E4), and \texttt{pdftotext} scrambles two-column reading order (E5).

\paragraph{Verdict: perceive, externalize, synthesize.}
The evidence converges on H2. The model perceives the content (E3); the modality is not the reasoning bottleneck for legible content, at the activation level and behaviorally on the frontier models (E6--E9); and recovery requires two passes with independent budgets (E2), not merely text in context (E1). One pass asked to do everything satisfices under generation load; a dedicated externalization pass followed by a dedicated, full-budget synthesis pass over complete text does not. This account makes two predictions we test next: the benefit should scale with how much the end-to-end pass satisfices (an \emph{inverse-baseline law}, \S\ref{sec:solution}), and decomposition should fail in exactly two ways---when synthesis cannot fit the transcript through its output budget, and when removing the grounding source lets synthesis confabulate from training priors (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).

\section{The solution: two-pass decomposition, and where it wins}
\label{sec:solution}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figures/fig1_inverse_correlation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The inverse-baseline law.} Coverage gain vs.\ $C_0$ baseline across all 21 cells: the worse the model does end-to-end, the more decomposition recovers. Circles: audio; squares: paper. OLS line with bootstrap 95\% CI band; Pearson $r{=}-0.45$, bootstrap CI $[-0.73,-0.10]$ (fraction $r\ge 0$: $0.9\%$). Excluding the two meeting-minutes re-prompts (whose atomic-fact probes depress absolute coverage under a structured-minutes prompt), $r{=}-0.47$ on $n{=}19$ with CI $[-0.83,+0.10]$: the direction survives but that subset's CI just crosses zero. The five counter-cells sit at high baseline (no headroom) or trigger Mode~A/B (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).}
\label{fig:solution}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The configuration.}
Figure~\ref{fig:schematic} formalizes the fix that \S\ref{sec:mechanism} dictates. $C_1$, the \emph{two-pass decomposition}: Pass-1 transcribes the source to text (perceive and externalize; same prompt as the reference pipeline); Pass-2 writes the review from that transcript alone, with the same weights and the raw modality removed from context (synthesize). The only difference from $C_0$ is whether the reasoning-and-writing step sees pixels/audio or text. Variants used later: $C_{1c}$ (chunked Pass-2, with $^{\rm concat}$ for chunked-no-merge), $C_1^{\rm strip}$ (citation surface forms replaced by \texttt{[REF]} before Pass-2), $C_{1\rm iter}$ (quote-grounded iterative refinement, \S\ref{sec:failure_modes}), and $C_{1+}$ (transcript $+$ modality, the E1 control).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_headline.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Per-cell results, all 21 Gemini~3.1~Pro cells} (sorted by end-to-end baseline within each modality; the bolded row is the running example). Decomposition improves coverage on 16/21 (mean $\Delta_{\rm cov}{=}+0.118$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[+0.048,+0.189]$, sign-test $p{=}0.027$; 14/17 positive outside the $\pm0.04$ noise floor, $p{=}0.013$) and cuts unsupported claims on 18/21 (mean $\Delta_{\rm halluc}{=}-3.86$, CI $[-5.62,-2.33]$, $p{=}0.0015$). \paragraph{Headline result at $n{=}21$.}
Figure~\ref{fig:headline_cells} gives the per-cell picture behind Figure~\ref{fig:headline}: decomposition cuts hallucinations on 18/21 cells and improves coverage on 16/21, both sign-test significant, despite Pass-2 seeing strictly less than the end-to-end pass; the direction survives restriction to cells outside the noise floor and median substitution on the four high-count judge-spread cells (\S\ref{sec:robust}). The largest coverage gain is Harvard Moot Court ($+50$pp, multi-speaker legal argumentation where the end-to-end baseline is weakest); the cleanest faithfulness win is 3B1B Attention ($11$ hallucinations to $0$). One structural caveat qualifies the headline and is examined in \S\ref{sec:robust}: the reference transcript and Pass-1 share a model, which can favor $C_1$; a reference-bias control with independent ASR/OCR (Whisper, EasyOCR) preserves the direction on 4/4 cells, but that control is small, so we flag the shared-model reference as the principal threat to the headline statistic.

\paragraph{The inverse-baseline law.}
The organizing regularity is Figure~\ref{fig:solution}: coverage gain scales inversely with the end-to-end baseline. This is exactly what perceive--externalize--synthesize predicts. $C_0$ coverage measures how much the monolithic pass satisfices on a given cell; E3 says the dropped content is sitting in the transcript; so the cells with the most headroom are the cells where a full-budget synthesis pass over that transcript recovers the most. It is a statement about \emph{headroom}, not a promise of a universal win: the five counter-cells sit either at saturated baselines (NeurIPS panel at $C_0{=}0.92$; Wagging Tail ${\approx}0.80$) or in the two predicted failure regimes (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}), and Perovskite's $-16$pp loss shrinks to $-6$pp---near the noise floor---under $n{=}5$ multi-seed re-runs (\S\ref{sec:robust}).

\paragraph{Cross-model and cross-vendor.}
Every cross-model result is an instance of the same law (Figure~\ref{fig:xmodel}): wins where the end-to-end baseline leaves headroom, washouts where it is already strong. We emphasize what this is \emph{not}: not a blanket vendor-independent win, and the headline aggregate remains single-vendor (Gemini). A clean cross-\emph{company} same-weights audio cascade is impossible by API constraint (no non-Google frontier model both ingests audio and accepts a text-only Pass-2; \S\ref{sec:discussion}), which is why the mixed-model pipelines of Figure~\ref{fig:xmodel}d are the deployment-relevant form: in production one mixes the best model per stage, and there decomposition is what makes audio review possible at all for text-only models. On long papers the vendors diverge sharply---Gemini stays small-Pareto-positive on Heat Pipes 104pp while Claude regresses on faithfulness, and on SP-5100 176pp both vendors' $C_1$ show the same bibliographic-expansion signature (Appendix~\ref{appdx:xvendor})---which is the failure-mode structure the next section dissects.

\paragraph{Cost.}
$C_1$ is $N{+}1$ calls versus one: call ratios range from ${\approx}2\times$ on short audio to ${\approx}177\times$ on the 176pp paper, with output-token volume scaling with source length (cost-vs-gain scatter in Figure~\ref{fig:cost}, \S\ref{sec:discussion}). The evidence says decomposition pays off where end-to-end has measurable headroom and buys nothing on saturated cells---so the inverse-baseline law is also the cost rule.

\section{Where decomposition breaks: two failure modes and their fixes}
\label{sec:failure_modes}

The mechanism predicts exactly two ways decomposition can fail, and both appear. \textbf{Mode~A (compression bottleneck):} Pass-2 has a fixed output budget (${\approx}2$k words); when the transcript is many times longer, synthesis must compress and drops content the same way $C_0$ does. \textbf{Mode~B (training-prior leak):} removing the raw source removes a grounding anchor; on documents with heavy training-data overlap, Pass-2 fills abbreviated details from prior knowledge. Mode~B is the genuine---but secondary---cost of dropping the modality (it is the same effect E1 shows in reverse). Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}a shows both on Heat Pipes 104pp, the cell where they separate cleanly by vendor.

\paragraph{Mode~A: compression bottleneck (3 papers).}
Three Gemini cells show the signature of transcript-length-to-budget ratios of ${\sim}9$--$15\times$: Heat Pipes 104pp (small $+6$pp gain), arXiv Fairness AI 53pp ($-10$pp), arXiv Perovskite 80pp ($-16$pp at $n{=}1$, $-6$pp under multi-seed). The tested fix is \textbf{chunked Pass-2}: split the transcript into ${\sim}5$k-word chunks, summarize each, concatenate. It lifts Heat Pipes coverage $0.78\to0.94$ (Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}a) and replicates directionally on Fairness AI and Perovskite at $n{=}5$ but \emph{not} on Megagauss, so we report chunked Pass-2 as a $2/3$-replicated Mode-A fix (Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}).

\paragraph{Mode~B: training-prior leak (4 papers).}
The Heat Pipes paper cites ``Reference~2 (Cotter)''; Claude's $C_1$ expands this to ``Cotter's 1965 analysis (LA-3246-MS)''---the year and report number are training-data details absent from the transcript (more examples in Appendix~\ref{appdx:examples}). The same bibliographic-expansion signature appears on all four long, reference-dense Claude cells---Heat Pipes, SP-5100, Megagauss, and Fairness~AI, whose author--year citations (${\approx}6$ per 1k words) the numeric-form regex behind the \emph{cite/1k} feature undercounts at $0.03$---and is absent on the genuinely citation-light Thermal 66pp. Two deployment triggers emerge (thresholds and per-cell citation densities in Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}): Mode~A when the transcript is $\gtrsim 5\times$ the Pass-2 output budget and the coverage gain is small; Mode~B when the source carries $\gtrsim 2$ citation surface forms per 1k words in \emph{any} style \emph{and} its cited literature is widely represented pre-cutoff (true of Fairness~AI's fairness canon even though the paper itself is post-cutoff). The modes compound (Mimo Heat Pipes shows both) or occur alone (Heat Pipes: Gemini dominantly A, Claude dominantly B).

\paragraph{Interventions, including a retraction.}
Beyond chunked Pass-2, we tested \emph{citation stripping} ($C_1^{\rm strip}$) as a Mode-B fix. \textbf{It does not survive replication}: the original ``$20\to13$'' Heat Pipes point estimate lies within the combined Pass-2$+$judge noise envelope at $n{=}5$, and across four cells the fix has zero replicated wins (Appendix~\ref{appdx:fixes}). We retract it, and it motivates the stronger structural intervention below.

\paragraph{$C_{1\rm iter}$: a quote-grounded refinement.}
Mode~B calls for re-grounding synthesis in the transcript. $C_{1\rm iter}$: (a)~extract 50--150 atomic claims from the transcript, each with a verbatim $\le$25-word quote; (b)~drop any claim whose quote does not fuzzy-match the transcript; (c)~rewrite the review from surviving claims only. The filter is a tripwire, not a culler---$99.8\%$ of claims pass ($3731/3738$)---so its effect is to make the model self-discipline: asked for a quote, it stops emitting the bibliographic expansions Mode~B catalogues. It is RAG's attribution discipline applied \emph{within} a same-weights self-cascade. The verdict at $n{=}5$ on seven cells (Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}b) is conditional on the dominant failure mode: it suppresses Mode~B where Mode~B binds, pays a rewrite-compression cost everywhere, and is therefore a Pareto win only where the rewrite does not over-compress.

Separating stochasticity sources (Figure~\ref{fig:stoch}, \S\ref{sec:discussion}; Appendix~\ref{appdx:stoch}) yields three operational facts. Pass-2 generation varies (Claude deprecates \texttt{temperature}, so determinism is not enforceable; Gemini at $T{=}0$ is mostly but not strictly deterministic); the GPT-5.4 judge is essentially deterministic on low-count cells but spreads $\pm10$--$25$ on cells with very long unsupported-claim lists (which run near its completion budget); and coverage is far more stable than hallucination counts under all three sources. Finally, a leave-one-out logistic predictor of $\mathrm{sign}(\Delta_{\rm cov})$ from four cell features reaches LOOCV AUC $0.65$ with all coefficient signs matching the qualitative picture---an honest $n{=}21$ baseline, well short of deployment-ready (Appendix~\ref{appdx:cell_table}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Speech and documents.} Prior cascade-vs-end-to-end results are often read as substrate claims; our mechanism study suggests they are at least partly about decomposition and modality-specific recoverability. Cuervo et al.~\cite{cuervo2025closing} document a text/speech understanding gap, and the Cascade Equivalence Hypothesis~\cite{ceh2026} shows speech-LLMs \emph{internally} behave like ASR$\to$LLM cascades, while X-Talk~\cite{xtalk2025} finds explicitly cascaded speech-to-speech pipelines competitive. On documents, DocVLM~\cite{docvlm2024} reports OCR-augmented VLMs beating pure-vision VLMs on DocVQA at fixed budget, while the OCR-Reasoning Benchmark~\cite{ocrreasoning2026} shows pure-OCR cascades cap below 50\% where spatial layout carries meaning, ColPali~\cite{colpali2025} shows vision-only retrieval beating OCR, and GUI agents do better from raw pixels than from structured-text intermediates~\cite{uground2024,uitars2025}. The common thread matches our mechanism: decomposition helps when the task is generation-under-load and the content survives transcription, and hurts when the answer lives in layout or signal that transcription discards.

\paragraph{Step-Audio-R1: a boundary, not a contradiction.} Step-Audio-R1~\cite{stepaudior1} observes audio LLMs spontaneously reasoning over a \emph{textual surrogate} of the audio (attributing musical sadness to ``lyrics mention sadness'' rather than a minor key) and treats this as the failure its modality-grounded distillation must correct. Their tasks---music, environmental sound, paralinguistics---have \emph{acoustic} answers that transcription discards; ours is the complementary regime, where the payload is what was \emph{said}. Our activation tests find no reasoning-substrate gap for propositional content in either modality (E6/E7), so we read their effect as task-payload-specific rather than as ``audio is a worse substrate.'' Our Mode~B is the within-regime echo of their concern.

\paragraph{Cascade theory and faithfulness.} Bengio's Consciousness Prior~\cite{bengio2017consciousness} argues for sparse discrete bottlenecks between perception and reasoning; our externalized text bottleneck is a deployment-time analogue, and Discrete JEPA~\cite{discretejepa2025} makes a related architectural argument within vision. Mode~B is a self-cascade analogue of the unfaithfulness problem motivating RAG~\cite{rag2020}, RARR~\cite{rarr2023}, and Attribution to Identified Sources~\cite{ais2022}; $C_{1\rm iter}$ adopts RARR's in-context-attribution discipline but operates entirely within a same-weights self-cascade without external retrieval.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What generalizes.} The portable result is not ``decomposition always wins'' but the inverse-baseline law: decomposition recovers content in proportion to how much the end-to-end pass satisfices, so it pays off precisely where a model reasons poorly over the raw modality and buys nothing where it is already strong. This is what makes the cross-vendor picture coherent rather than contradictory---Claude's near-ceiling NASA papers, Gemini~2.5~Flash's strong end-to-end audio, and Gemini~Pro's weak end-to-end audio all fall on the same line (Figure~\ref{fig:xmodel}). The most deployable corollary is that decomposition \emph{unlocks} text-only frontier models for audio (Figure~\ref{fig:xmodel}d). Table~\ref{tab:checklist} compresses the per-regime recommendations ($C_1$, $C_{1\rm iter}$, $C_{1c}^{\rm concat}$, or $C_0$).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Scope:} the headline aggregate is single-vendor; the within-Claude comparison replicates the faithfulness direction on 5/6 papers but is not independently significant at $n{=}6$, and Claude $C_0$ is unrealizable beyond 104pp. A cross-company same-weights \emph{audio} cascade is API-impossible (Claude takes no audio; OpenAI's audio model refuses text-only Pass-2; both verified), so the mixed pipeline is the natural production form. The Mimo audio cells ran at reduced bitrate (OpenRouter cap), conflating quality with model weakness; the full-quality 2.5~Flash arm removes that confound and is headroom-gated as predicted.
\emph{Measurement:} probe and reference bias are only partially controlled (7 and 4 cells); the shared-model reference remains the principal threat to the headline statistic; single-judge intra-rater variance affects high-count cells ($\pm10$--$25$; Figure~\ref{fig:stoch}). Several sources predate the model cutoff, but memorization biases \emph{against} the cascade (it aids $C_0$), making the headline conservative; the post-cutoff sources span wins and counter-cells, consistent with the law.
\emph{Mechanism breadth:} all measurements are on long-form review---we claim nothing about short-form QA, code, or agentic tasks. E8/E9 are behavioral, not activation-level (frontier activations are inaccessible); Gemini's reasoning probe sits at ceiling, so Claude supplies the one headroom case; real scanned/degraded pages remain untested (the degraded control is synthetic); E4/E5 are single-seed and confounded.

\paragraph{Validation plan.}
The load-bearing effects are already multi-seed on all 21 cells, so remaining replication is about breadth, not statistical power: extend the E6 logit-lens to other open families and, where activations become accessible, lift E8/E9 to the activation level; push the audio analogue (E7) into the multi-fact load regime; re-test on real scanned and degraded pages, where a substrate or dilution effect is most likely to appear; extend to other generation-under-load tasks plus a short-form control where the account predicts \emph{no} decomposition benefit; and causally vary the synthesis output budget to confirm Mode~A is budget-bound.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On long-form review, the intuitive end-to-end deployment of a multimodal model quietly satisfices: it drops a third of what the model demonstrably perceived and embellishes the rest. The cause is not perception and not the reasoning substrate---text and images of text are read equally well, on open models at the activation level and on the frontier models behaviorally---but generation under load: one pass cannot perceive, reason, and write a long faithful review at once. A same-weights two-pass decomposition with independent output budgets recovers the loss, beats end-to-end on faithfulness and coverage at $n{=}21$, and obeys an inverse-baseline law: it helps most exactly where the monolithic pass satisfices most. Its two predicted failure modes---synthesis-budget overflow and source-removal confabulation---both appear, with a chunked Pass-2 and a quote-grounded refinement as partial, honestly-replicated fixes. The practical rule is \textbf{perceive, externalize, then synthesize}: the win is the decomposition, not the modality---a deployment-time pattern with predictable failure modes, not a universal architecture.

\paragraph{E3: perception is not the bottleneck.}
The per-cell breakdown is Figure~\ref{fig:mech_e3} (main text). The transcript covers a mean $99.9\%$ of probes, versus $65\%$ for the $C_0$ review and $77\%$ for $C_1$ synthesis; $361/362$ probes dropped by $C_0$ are present in the model's own transcript.

\paragraph{E6: no cross-modal substrate gap at the activation level.}
We tested H1 directly on an open VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), controlling for prior knowledge with novel synthetic facts about nonsense entities (e.g.\ ``the Zorblatt device weighs 7 kilograms''). Each fact is presented either as digital text or as an \emph{image} of that same text, with a matched single-token question, and a layer-wise logit-lens reads off the log-probability and rank of the answer token at every layer. The two modalities are indistinguishable for an isolated fact and under load: packing $K\in\{1,4,8,16,24\}$ facts into one stimulus, image and text stay at $1.00$ accuracy with a readout-depth gap $<1$ layer (TOST-equivalent within $\pm 1$ layer) at every $K$ (Figure~\ref{fig:substrate}a). The same null replicates on Qwen2.5-VL-3B; the per-$K$ ladder for both models is in Appendix~\ref{appdx:vision_ladder}. (The 32B variant verbalizes answers in a form our single-token matcher cannot score, so it is excluded.)

\paragraph{E7: no audio substrate gap either, once the speech is intelligible.}

\section{Protocol details}
\label{appdx:protocol}

\paragraph{Reference quality.} Gemini~3.1~Pro is used for OCR/ASR with chunking. Single-call OCR on a 104-page paper produced a degenerate TOC dot-leader loop (113KB output, 56k ``words'' but only 264 real-word tokens, 6 page markers out of 104). Chunking (1 page per call, max 8192 output tokens) eliminates this failure mode. For audio we chunk into 30-min segments with 5-second overlap. We discovered a silent server-side truncation on one audio chunk (chunk~1 of IETF SNAC produced 1981 words instead of the expected $\approx 4368$, ending mid-sentence with \texttt{``Uh, well, see, yeah, that'\,''}). The protocol grew an automatic chunk-level word-count parity check after this incident.

\paragraph{Statistics.} We treat $|\Delta h|\le 1$ on cells with $h<10$, and $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 0.04$ ($\approx 2$ probes on a 50-probe cell), as within noise (presentation conventions calibrated against inter-judge direction flips, \S\ref{sec:robust}). For aggregate effects we report Wilson 95\% CIs on coverage rates, percentile-bootstrap 95\% CIs ($n_{\rm boot}{=}10000$, seeded) on cross-cell means and Pearson $r$, and sign tests against $\Delta{=}0$. The judge runs with a 24k-token completion budget.

\paragraph{Sources.} Audio: Karpathy ``State of GPT'' (42 min, 2023), MIT~6.034 L1 Patrick Winston (47 min, 2010), Sapolsky Behavioral~Genetics~1 Stanford (85 min, post-2026 reupload), Stanford Decarbonized~Power~Sector Zach~Ming (72 min, 2025), Doudna CRISPR Basics (48 min, 2017), 3Blue1Brown ``Attention in Transformers'' (26 min, 2024), Veritasium ``Strange Math'' (32 min, 2025), NeurIPS Black-in-AI panel (31 min, 2021), Harvard Law School Ames Moot Court (82 min, 2025), IETF SNAC WG interim 2026-04-16 (60 min, $\approx 5$ speakers, recorded post the most likely training-data cutoff). Karpathy yields both a review and a meeting-minutes task. Papers: NASA NTRS IDs 19720009221 (Wagging Tail, 9pp), 19680000724 (Env Test, 18pp), 19690008405 (Dynamic Response, 41pp), 19690013408 (Natural Vibration, 42pp), 19700023812 (Thermal Analysis, 66pp), 19700025120 (Heat Pipes, 104pp), and SP-5100 (Shock and Vibration Technology, 176pp). The arXiv papers are arXiv:2605.09852 (Fairness~AI, 53pp), arXiv:2605.13991 (Perovskite Tandem PV, 80pp), and arXiv:2605.11379 (Megagauss Physics, 75pp). NASA papers are rendered as page images at 200 DPI, and arXiv papers at 200 DPI full color.

\paragraph{Models and snapshots.} The models are Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (\texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview}), Claude Opus 4.7 (\texttt{claude-opus-4-7}), and Mimo v2-omni (\texttt{xiaomi/mimo-v2-omni} via OpenRouter). The judge is GPT-5.4 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=high}, completion budget 24k tokens), routed via OpenRouter due to scope restrictions on the local OpenAI key. Cross-vendor extensions reuse the Gemini reference and probes, isolating the question to whether the advantage depends on the $C_0$/$C_1$ model: Claude Opus 4.7 (complete $C_0$-vs-$C_1$ on the six papers where its multimodal $C_0$ fits; $C_1$-only on the longer papers) and Mimo v2-omni on every cell where its $C_0$ does not return empty.

\paragraph{Mimo audio constraint.} OpenRouter's Mimo provider enforces a 10MB base64 audio input limit. Karpathy required re-encoding from 32kbps to 16kbps mono mp3 to fit, and Sapolsky at 85 min required 12kbps. The Mimo audio Karpathy result ($C_0$ 4h/0.569, $C_1$ 4h/0.588 across 51 probes, 1509 / 1268 review words) is within noise across the $C_0\to C_1$ transition, so we treat it as a wash. \emph{gpt-audio} (via OpenRouter) accepted audio input but refused text-only Pass-2 with HTTP 400 ``This model requires that either input content or output modality contain audio''. The same-weights cascade is not realizable on this model, and $C_0$ alone scored 10h/0.765 on Karpathy.

\paragraph{Pareto terminology.} We call a cascade condition \emph{strictly Pareto-positive} relative to $C_0$ when it has both fewer hallucinations and higher coverage by amounts exceeding the noise floor. \emph{Pareto-positive within noise} means strictly better on one axis and no worse than noise on the other. \emph{Pareto-dominant} on a set means no other condition has lower hallucination at equal or higher coverage. These are presentation labels, not statistical claims.

\paragraph{A held-out predictor (LOOCV, $n{=}21$).}

\section{Mixed-model audio cascade: decomposition unlocks text-only models}
\label{appdx:mixed}

The same-weights audio cascade is Gemini-only by API constraint (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). In production one would not use one model for everything; one would use the best model per stage. This probe does exactly that, and is therefore \emph{off-protocol}: it mixes vendors across stages, so it speaks to the generality of \emph{decomposition}, not the same-weights claim. We take an \emph{independent}, non-Google Pass-1 transcript from OpenAI \texttt{gpt-audio} (chunked, full quality---distinct from the Gemini reference used for judging), then run a text-only synthesis Pass-2 with three frontier text models, two of which (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.4) cannot ingest audio at all on their own. Reviews are scored on the shared Pro reference and probes with the GPT-5.4 judge, plus a Claude-judge cross-check on the GPT-5.4-authored review to rule out self-grading.

The result is unambiguous (Table~\ref{tab:mixed}; Figure~\ref{fig:xmodel}d): on all three cells, every mixed pipeline beats Gemini's \emph{end-to-end} $C_0$ on both axes---coverage rises to $0.85$--$1.00$ (vs $C_0$ $0.63$--$0.88$) and hallucinations fall to $2$--$9$ (vs $11$--$17$). Decomposition thus \emph{unlocks} text-only frontier models for audio review and lets them outperform the native audio model's end-to-end pass, using an off-the-shelf transcript. The GPT-5.4 self-grading concern is unfounded: the Claude judge scores the GPT-5.4-authored reviews \emph{lower} on hallucinations (1/3/3 vs the GPT-5.4 judge's 3/6/9), consistent with the $0.71\times$ inter-judge ratio (\S\ref{sec:robust}). Where the mixed pipeline trails the Gemini same-weights $C_1$ (most clearly on MIT: coverage $0.85$--$0.90$ vs $0.98$ across all three Pass-2 models), the deficit tracks \texttt{gpt-audio}'s lossier transcript, i.e.\ the ceiling is set by Pass-1 transcript quality, exactly as the perceive--externalize--synthesize account predicts.

\paragraph{Detailed decomposition.}
\emph{(a)~Claude Pass-2 generation variance.} Same input + same API + same prompt: Heat~Pipes Claude $C_1$ swings $20\to 9$ between two runs (strip $13\to 14$ more stable), and the deltas are large enough that the strip effect cannot be distinguished from Pass-2 noise. Claude Opus 4.7 deprecates the \texttt{temperature} parameter so determinism cannot be enforced from the SDK. \emph{(b)~Gemini Pass-2 generation variance.} At \texttt{temperature=0.0}, two of three Gemini Pass-2 re-runs produced byte-identical output (MD5 match) on Heat~Pipes / SP-5100, and the third differed in similar length (1929 vs 1815 words, $\Delta h = +6$). Gemini is therefore mostly but not strictly deterministic, consistent with GPU-level non-associativity. \emph{(c)~GPT-5.4 high-reasoning judge variance.} Essentially deterministic on low-count cells ($\pm 1$ on Heat~Pipes Claude across three re-judgments) but substantially stochastic on high-count cells ($\pm 25$ on SP-5100, $\pm 17$ on the byte-identical SP-5100 Gemini review). We attribute this to long unsupported-claim lists running up against our 24k-token completion budget, so the judge truncates and resamples across calls. Coverage is stable on every cell across judge re-runs.

The third (SNAC) is driven by the judges assigning different $C_0$ baselines (14 vs 10) while agreeing on $C_1$.

\section{Specific hallucination examples (Heat Pipes Claude)}
\label{appdx:examples}

The 20 unsupported claims from Claude $C_1$ on Heat~Pipes 104pp are dominated by training-prior expansions of bibliographic abbreviations:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item ``Cotter's 1965 analysis (LA-3246-MS)'': the transcript cites only ``Reference 2 (Cotter)'', so the year and report number are training-data details.
\item ``correlation from Allingham and McEntire (1961)'': the transcript only says ``Ref.~12'', so the author names and year are training-data details.
\item ``McAdams' laminar horizontal-tube formula'': the transcript says ``Reference 13 for laminar condensation on horizontal tubes'', so ``McAdams'' is a training-data detail.
\item ``Vapor chamber-fin radiator (Haller, Lieblein, and Lindow at NASA Lewis)'': the transcript says only ``proposed by Haller, et al of NASA''.
\end{itemize}

One outright misreading: the transcript states the no-boiling condition as $p_v - p_\ell \le 2\sigma\cos\theta/r_c$, but Claude's review writes $\ge$. This is a sign error in Pass-2 reading of Pass-1 text, not training-prior leak.
