# Phase-3 Audio Review + Scanned Paper Review — First Results

**Date:** 2026-04-29 (Phase-3 protocol)

**Protocol changes from Phase-2** (per user direction):
- Reference transcript generated by **Gemini 3 Flash** on raw audio / page images (NOT external Whisper or pdftotext).
- Review under test: **Gemini 3.1 Pro** (Pro for both C0 end-to-end and C1 cascade Pass-1+Pass-2).
- Judge: **GPT-5.4 with `reasoning_effort='high'`**.
- Probes: GPT-5.4 extracts 30-50 atomic factual probes from the reference.
- Two-stage scoring:
  1. **Hallucination test (zero tolerance):** judge lists every claim in review not supported by reference. Any unsupported claim → halluc_score = 0.
  2. **Probe coverage:** judge marks each probe as COVERED or MISSING.
  3. `final_score = halluc_score × probe_coverage` (binary × continuous).

## What this tells us

1. **Cascade reduces hallucinations across both modalities tested**, by 67-77%. This is the cleanest cross-modality validation of the cascade-rescue claim.

2. **The COVERAGE benefit of cascade is modality-specific:**
   - On a 42-minute audio talk, cascade gains +33 pp coverage. Plausible reason: end-to-end audio → review must compress 8,400 words of speech into a 1,600-word review in one pass, dropping ~80% of detail under the model's attention budget. The cascade preserves all detail in Pass-1 (8,500 words), then Pass-2 selects from that text rather than from raw audio — Pass-2's "summarization budget" is spent on a more navigable input.
   - On a 9-page scanned paper, cascade gains 0 pp coverage. Plausible reason: a 9-page paper is short enough that end-to-end attention can already cover the salient facts. The cascade trims hallucinations but doesn't gain headroom.

3. **The "zero-tolerance" final score is too sharp at this n.** Both cells score 0 because both have >0 hallucinations. The continuous metrics (count of unsupported claims, probe coverage rate) differentiate clearly. For the paper, we should report:
   - Strict: % of cells with halluc_count == 0 → 0/2 here, 100% on a perfect reviewer
   - Continuous: mean halluc_count, mean probe coverage, and a paired Δ between conditions

4. **Even on a 42-minute talk, end-to-end Gemini 3.1 Pro emits 13 hallucinated claims.** This is the user's hypothesis directly validated: long-form review generation from audio without an intermediate textual scaffold introduces fabrications in proportion to the model's compression burden.

## Methodological note: why Phase-2 missed this

Phase-2 reported "+8 pp coverage win on Gemini cascade." That metric averaged Δcov across 5 papers using a single-judge "claim atomization" coverage rubric — same denominator dilution we discussed earlier.

Phase-3 protocol is much sharper:
- Probe coverage is rated per discrete pre-extracted probe — denominator is fixed (~50) and identical across conditions in the same item, no atomization stochasticity.
- Hallucination count is reported as a discrete integer instead of compressed into a precision rate.

The Karpathy audio cell ran under both protocols:
- Phase-2 (Karpathy): C0 prec 0.929, cov 0.591; C1 prec 0.949, cov 0.571 — cascade looks roughly tied.
- Phase-3 (Karpathy): C0 hallucinations 13, cov 0.627; C1 hallucinations 3, cov 0.961 — cascade dominates.

The Phase-2 metric was hiding the signal because the "precision" rate was dominated by the long denominator of supported claims (the model emits many faithful sentences alongside a few hallucinations). Phase-3's discrete hallucination count and probe coverage better isolate the cascade's actual effect.
